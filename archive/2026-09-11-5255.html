NORMAL Asia-Pacific HIGH · 72
US Pacific airfields get $8B missile defense upgrade
The Pentagon is investing in an Enhanced Integrated Air and Missile Defense System built around 16 interceptor and radar sites layering Aegis Ashore, Patriot, and THAAD batteries across Guam, with Northwest Field — a derelict WWII-era strip — now restored as tactical overflow capacity for Andersen Air Force Base as part of an $8 billion broader program. Additional upgrades are planned at bases in Korea, Australia, and Japan including Kadena and Misawa, reflecting a deliberate effort to distribute force concentration across the Pacific theater.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
The $8 billion Pacific hardening program directly benefits suppliers of Aegis, Patriot, and THAAD systems, and the parallel reactivation of historically dormant airfields signals a doctrine of distributed resilience that will drive sustained base infrastructure and sensor network spending well into the next decade.

NORMAL North America HIGH · 70
SpaceX flies classified USSF-153 payload on Falcon 9
SpaceX launched the USSF-153 mission from Space Launch Complex 4 East at Vandenberg Space Force Base on 10 September, carrying an undisclosed number of satellites to low Earth orbit for the US Space Force. Booster B1081 completed its 27th flight and landed on the droneship Of Course I Still Love You, marking the 224th landing on that vessel and the 660th Falcon booster landing overall; this was SpaceXs fifth national security payload mission of the year.

NORMAL Asia-Pacific HIGH · 68
China reaches 62 launches with six-satellite Yaogan mission
A Long March 4B rocket lifted off from Jiuquan Satellite Launch Center on 10 September carrying three Yaogan-53 and three Yaogan-56 satellites developed by the China Academy of Space Technology, pushing Chinas annual launch count to 62 and marking the 666th Long March launch overall. Chinese authorities describe the satellites as serving crop yield estimation and land survey purposes, but Western analysts have consistently assessed the Yaogan series as serving a dual civilian-military reconnaissance role.

Chinas accelerating Yaogan deployment rate increases its persistent low-Earth-orbit surveillance capacity over contested Pacific and Middle East theatres, directly relevant to operators and analysts tracking the intelligence asymmetry between Beijing and US partners in both regions.

ESA has signed an agreement with US company Vast on behalf of Greece, naming flight surgeon Adrianos Golemis as mission specialist on the Vast-PAM-1 flight to the International Space Station, planned for 2027 and subject to approval by the Multilateral Crew Operations Panel. BREAKING Middle East CRITICAL · 97
Iran strike damages A-10 and eight F-15s at Jordan base
Iranian strikes hit Muwaffaq Salti Air Base in Jordan, damaging one A-10 Thunderbolt II — leaving it with a missing wing — and roughly eight F-15s, which sustained light damage and were returned to service, a US official told Reuters. The attack required US forces to fire roughly 30 Patriot interceptors to counter the incoming missiles, intensifying existing concern about the inventory of US air defense interceptors; since the war began in February, 18 US service members have been killed and over 800 injured, and a Congressional Research Service report from May noted 42 US military aircraft had already been lost or damaged in the conflict.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
The expenditure of roughly 30 Patriot interceptors in a single engagement, combined with cumulative aircraft attrition since February, makes the rate of US munitions and platform consumption the defining supply-chain risk for defense primes and interceptor manufacturers for the remainder of this conflict.

BREAKING Middle East CRITICAL · 97
US and Iran trade tanker strikes near Strait of Hormuz
Iran declared it had attacked 10 ships near the Strait of Hormuz after US forces destroyed five Iranian crude oil carriers — one sunk, the remainder disabled or rendered inoperable — in the largest declared wave of tit-for-tat shipping attacks since the war began in February; the international Brent crude benchmark breached $100 a barrel for the first time since July. The Revolutionary Guards warned they would hit 20 targets for every two or three Iranian targets struck, and said Iran would soon publish maps of a new, much larger maritime exclusion zone extending as far as the Arabian Gulf off Chabahar near Pakistan.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
A declared 10-to-1 Iranian retaliation ratio and an expanding maritime exclusion zone covering approaches to both the Strait of Hormuz and Chabahar create the conditions for a rapid escalation to full shipping corridor closure, which would constitute the most severe global energy supply shock since the 1973 oil embargo and is a category risk for any portfolio with Middle East exposure.

URGENT Middle East CRITICAL · 92
Ansar Allah seizes Mokha port, threatening Bab el-Mandeb
Ansar Allah has seized the key Yemeni port of Mokha, with Ahmed Nagi of the International Crisis Group calling the move a major game changer on Thursday, as the group simultaneously stepped up cross-border attacks against Saudi Arabia and gained ground against Riyadh-backed forces in the most intensive clashes since an April 2022 ceasefire. The seizure brings Ansar Allah closer to the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, a crucial alternative shipping corridor to the Strait of Hormuz through which trade is already disrupted; Pakistan has signaled it intends to honor its mutual defense pact with Saudi Arabia, signed last month, alongside Turkey.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Control of Mokha gives Ansar Allah a forward position from which to interdict Bab el-Mandeb traffic, meaning both of the Middle East primary maritime chokepoints — Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb — now face simultaneous disruption risk, a scenario that would structurally reprice global shipping insurance and energy supply chains for months.

URGENT North America CRITICAL · 88
Pentagon removes Anthropic from 90% of classified AI work
The Pentagons chief technology officer stated that approximately 90 percent of the Defense Departments classified AI workloads previously running on Anthropic technology have been transitioned to different model providers, with all Anthropic technology in the Maven Smart Systems program removed months ago — despite a federal judge ruling in late August that the Pentagons designation of Anthropic as a supply chain risk was illegal and baseless. Separately, Anthropic published a threat report documenting that Claude models were used in northern Yemen in an attempt to develop missile guidance and flight-control software, with the company stating it found no evidence a working weapon was fielded though an unsuccessful test-fire appeared to have occurred.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
The simultaneous military divorce from Anthropic and the emergence of Claude in adversary weapons development pipelines illustrates the core dilemma of dual-use AI: the Pentagon is restructuring its classified AI supply chain at the precise moment that commercial AI tools are filling the gap for state-adjacent weapons programs, creating urgency around which alternative model providers will capture the reallocated classified workloads.

URGENT North America CRITICAL · 88
F/A-XX carrier fighter award said to be imminent
The Pentagon has selected which company will build the US Navys next-generation stealth carrier fighter, designated F/A-XX, and could announce the winner within weeks or sooner, according to two people briefed on the matter; Boeing and Northrop Grumman are competing, with previous programs of similar scope suggesting a contract worth tens of billions of dollars over its lifetime. Congress earmarked $750 million for F/A-XX in the 2025 spending bill and an additional $1.4 billion in fiscal year 2026, providing immediate procurement momentum after years of delays driven by concerns about engineering and supply chain capacity.

An imminent F/A-XX award would be the largest single platform decision in US naval aviation in a generation, and the winner will anchor decades of production, sustainment, and derivative program revenue while the loser faces a structural gap in its combat aircraft pipeline at a moment of elevated geopolitical demand.

NORMAL United Arab Emirates CRITICAL · 75
Dubai airport recovers to 84% of pre-war capacity
Dubai Airports CEO Paul Griffiths said on Thursday that capacity has recovered to 84 percent of pre-Iran war levels and passenger traffic has reached 78 percent, following 111 threat alerts and 17 ballistic missile strikes on the airport this year with no deaths recorded. Passenger traffic dropped to 2.5 million in March before rising to 5 million in June and 6.3 million in July, a 26 percent monthly rise, with Griffiths projecting 100 million annual passengers possible by end of 2027.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
A sustained recovery trajectory at one of the worlds busiest aviation hubs signals improving commercial confidence in UAE airspace, but the remaining 16-point capacity gap and active threat history continue to weigh on airline scheduling and aerospace insurance risk models.

URGENT Yemen CRITICAL · 85
Anthropic claims Claude used for missile guidance
Anthropic published a threat report documenting misuse of its Claude models between December 2025 and August 2026, alleging it blocked an operation in northern Yemen that attempted to use Claude to write missile guidance and flight-control software for a guided rocket and a long-range ballistic missile, assigning different model instances specific engineer roles, according to Al Jazeera. The company said internal safeguards blocked many requests but admitted several slipped through, and it found no evidence a working weapon was fielded, though it claims operators conducted an unsuccessful test-fire.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
The documented attempt to use a commercial large language model as a substitute for human software engineers on missile guidance tasks confirms that AI misuse in weapons development has moved from theoretical to operational, fundamentally shifting the threat calculus for export controls and AI safety governance in the defence sector.

AI removes skill gap between state and lone hackers
