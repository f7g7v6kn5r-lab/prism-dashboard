URGENT North America CRITICAL · 90
Starship reaches orbit, deploys 26 Starlink sats
SpaceX launched its Super Heavy-Starship on its first orbital flight, with the Starship upper stage deploying 26 third-generation Starlink internet satellites after completing an orbit-insertion burn on a single Raptor engine following an early shutdown of one of its six engines. Mission managers brought the Starship back early with a Pacific Ocean splashdown north of Hawaii three hours after launch, ending a mission originally planned to last six orbits. NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman congratulated SpaceX and described the flight as a critical step toward Artemis moon landing missions targeted for 2028.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Starship reaching orbit and successfully deploying operational satellites removes the single largest technical risk gate for both NASAs lunar architecture and SpaceXs own Starlink V3 constellation buildout, shifting investor focus to the pace of the reflight cadence rather than whether the vehicle can reach orbit at all.

NORMAL North America HIGH · 72
Falcon Heavy flies first NRO mission on NSSL Phase 3
A SpaceX Falcon Heavy launched the classified NROL-97 mission from Launch Complex 39A at Kennedy Space Center, marking the first time the National Reconnaissance Office has used the heavy-lift vehicle after 22 prior NRO flights on Falcon 9. The mission was the first procured through the National Security Space Launch Phase 3 Lane 2 contract, and both side boosters recovered successfully at Landing Zones 1 and 2 at Cape Canaveral Space Force Station while the core stage was expended. NROL-97 was one of three Falcon launches on the same day, the first time SpaceX has landed four boosters in a single day.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
The NROs first use of Falcon Heavy under the NSSL Phase 3 framework signals a deliberate expansion of heavy-lift classified payloads, potentially opening a larger sustained revenue line for SpaceX beyond the Falcon 9 cadence that dominates its current manifest.

NORMAL North America HIGH · 65
Crew-13 launches to ISS on Falcon 9
A SpaceX Falcon 9 carried commander Jessica Watkins, pilot Luke Delaney, cosmonaut Sergey Teteryatnikov and Canadian astronaut Joshua Kutryk to the International Space Station from pad 40 at Cape Canaveral Space Force Station, with docking at the Harmony module scheduled approximately eight hours after liftoff. The mission was delayed from mid-September after a leaking valve was found in the Crew Dragon propulsion system. Kutryk is expecting his third child around Thanksgiving, approximately two months into the mission, making the couple the fourth in space history to have a child born while one parent was off the planet.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Crew-13s successful launch sustains the unbroken US commercial crew rotation and keeps the ISS fully staffed ahead of Boeings planned unpiloted Starliner test flight in the December-January window, a sequencing that matters to investors tracking whether NASA can certify a second crew transport provider before the stations 2030 retirement.

NORMAL North America HIGH · 55
NASA plans unpiloted Starliner test by year-end
NASA is working with Boeing to launch an unpiloted Starliner capsule in the December-January timeframe to test propulsion system upgrades ordered after the 2024 mission that stranded two astronauts for more than nine months. If the unpiloted flight succeeds, NASA and Boeing are targeting a piloted mission in mid-2028, with veteran astronaut Warren Hoburg assigned as commander. The Starliner has relied on United Launch Alliance Atlas 5 rockets, but with only a half dozen remaining in inventory, NASA plans to help certify ULAs new Vulcan rocket to carry astronauts aboard Starliner capsules.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
The December-January test window is the last realistic gate before Boeings Starliner program either re-enters the commercial crew rotation or faces a formal program restructuring, making the test outcome a binary catalyst for Boeing Defense, Space and Securitys near-term standing with NASA.

NORMAL North America MEDIUM · 45
Victor Glover on Artemis 2: the race is to Mars
Artemis 2 pilot Victor Glover, who became the first Black astronaut to travel to the Moon and the first to pilot the Orion spacecraft, has retired as an active NASA astronaut and returned to California Polytechnic State University. In an interview, Glover said he believes the United States will be first to land on the Moon and described the true strategic race as getting humans to Mars, estimating that milestone could come in 10 to 20 years depending on contributions from companies such as SpaceX and Blue Origin. Glover also defended the removal of diversity pledges regarding the first Moon landing crew, saying the astronaut corps is diverse by nature and the next crew will represent the best of the country.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Glovers framing of the Moon as a waypoint rather than the destination signals that NASA and its commercial partners must plan industrial and budgetary continuity well beyond the Artemis lunar program, which affects the long-horizon investment case for deep-space propulsion, life support and habitat contractors.

◆ PRISM ANALYSIS

NORMAL France CRITICAL · 78
France plans loyal wingman flight in 2028 under Hypairion
Frances Air Force, together with its Directorate General for Armament and the Ministerial Agency for Defence AI, plans to fly crewed fighter jets in collaboration with combat drones in 2028 under a project called Hypairion, which targets open, modular, government-owned AI architecture for combat aviation. The Air Force is modifying two Mirage 2000 fighters at Mont-de-Marsan airbase to become AI test beds, with initial use cases to begin by the end of the year covering areas such as optimizing flight paths and tactical decisions on when to deploy weapons. The Ministerial Agency for Defence AI, known as Amiad, will be responsible for delivering sovereign AI technology across command and control and mission autonomy areas.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Frances insistence on government-owned AI architecture rather than company-driven proprietary systems sets a regulatory and procurement precedent that could influence how other European nations structure sovereign AI requirements for next-generation combat aviation programmes.

Galileo Space builds AI signal-fusion satellites for defence
