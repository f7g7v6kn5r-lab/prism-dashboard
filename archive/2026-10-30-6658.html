NORMAL Europe HIGH · 62
Eutelsat first-quarter revenue falls 2.4%
Eutelsat reported total revenues of 286 million euros for the first quarter ended 30 September 2026, down 2.4 percent on a reported basis and 0.9 percent like-for-like, with video revenues down 17.8 percent year-on-year. The IRIS2 programme has entered its construction phase following a successful first rendez-vous outcome, adding 8.9 billion euros of future revenues to the backlog, with 5 million euros recognised in the quarter. Connectivity revenues rose 11.9 percent like-for-like year-on-year, driven by a 30.1 percent rise in low earth orbit revenues to 70.9 million euros.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
The 8.9 billion euro IRIS2 backlog entry transforms Eutelsats long-term revenue profile even as its legacy video business structurally contracts, making the pace of IRIS2 construction-phase recognition the critical metric for investors assessing the companys trajectory.

URGENT Middle East CRITICAL · 85
IRGC claims three tankers hit mines in Hormuz
Irans Revolutionary Guards stated that three oil tankers caught fire after striking mines while attempting to cross the Strait of Hormuz via what the IRGC described as an unauthorised southern route, adding that the vessels had switched off radar and navigation systems and ignored warnings. The Strait has remained largely closed since the start of the war on February 28, with the waterway normally carrying about one-fifth of global oil and gas consumption. The IRGC also claimed its electronic warfare unit captured a US MQM-170 drone during an operation in the western Strait of Hormuz.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Confirmed tanker losses in Hormuz would accelerate insurance premium spikes and route diversions that compound the energy supply pressure Europes 42-million-barrel reserve release is already attempting to contain, directly threatening regional supply chains that Gulf-based operators and investors depend on.

NORMAL Europe CRITICAL · 78
Destinus monthly missile output tops Europes pre-war annual total
Defence startup Destinus told Reuters it has produced more than 1,750 Ruta cruise missiles in Europe and that monthly output has reached nearly 300 units, exceeding Europes entire annual cruise missile production before Russias full-scale invasion of Ukraine. Chief Executive Mikhail Kokorich said the company expects from next year to meet demand across Europe and Ukraine for turbojet engines in the roughly 1.5-kilonewton thrust class, supplying both its own missiles and third-party manufacturers. Destinus has also begun production of the larger Ruta Block 2 missile, designed to carry a 250-kg payload and travel more than 700 km.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Destinus achieving this production rate while simultaneously supplying propulsion to other manufacturers positions it as a potential chokepoint supplier for European cruise missile programmes, making its engine capacity a strategic acquisition or partnership target for larger primes seeking to scale munitions output quickly.

URGENT North America CRITICAL · 75
USS Spruance sets post-WWII destroyer deployment record
The Arleigh Burke-class destroyer USS Spruance has been deployed for 342 days as of October 29, far exceeding the Navy commonly cited seven-month deployment benchmark, after departing Naval Base San Diego on November 21, 2025, as part of the USS Abraham Lincoln carrier strike group. Chief of Naval Operations Adm. Daryl Caudle warned October 26 that the effects of extended deployments could reverberate through the fleet maintenance schedule for years, saying maintenance availabilities could snowball as ships return from deployments exceeding 300 days.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
A 342-day deployment without return creates a compounding maintenance liability that the Navy acknowledges could generate years of additional pressure on the fleet, directly degrading the readiness and availability of destroyers that would be required for any Hormuz or Indo-Pacific contingency operation.

NORMAL Europe HIGH · 68
RAND warns hasty US Europe exit would hurt American arms sales
A RAND study released Wednesday, drawing on interviews with 51 officials and experts in France, Germany, Poland and the United Kingdom conducted mostly between April and November 2025, found that a rapid, uncoordinated US military drawdown from Europe would create major capability gaps, potentially embolden Russia and push allies away from American weapons over the long term, harming the US defense industrial base. The report found the four allies see a stark difference between a coordinated, orderly withdrawal they believe they could manage and a rapid, uncoordinated one they regard as creating serious risk, and recommends the US agree on a long-term timeline with allies before reducing forces.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
For US prime contractors, the RAND finding that uncoordinated retrenchment drives allies toward European sourcing is a direct commercial warning: the diplomatic style of any drawdown matters as much as its scale in determining whether NATO allies remain buyers of American platforms.

NORMAL Europe HIGH · 67
Poland reportedly agrees to send MiG-29s and F-16 ammo to Ukraine
Polish Lt. Gen. Dariusz Lukowski, director of the Strategy and Defense Planning Department at the Polish Ministry of National Defense, told the Polish Rzeczpospolita outlet that a decision has been made to hand over two MiG-29 Fulcrums to Ukraine in the near future, with one aircraft still flying and the other at end of service life for parts; Deputy National Defense Minister Pawel Zalewski confirmed the agreement to TVP Info and said discussions on remaining MiGs are ongoing. Europe releases 42 million barrels to ease energy prices
French Economy Minister Roland Lescure confirmed Europe will release 42 million barrels of oil reserves over three months, with half comprising diesel, as part of a Group of Seven coordinated release of up to 100 million barrels. France is releasing strategic reserves for the first time specifically to reduce prices, with a 10-million-barrel diesel tranche expected to hit markets within days and lower prices by 12 to 18 cents per litre, while French President Emmanuel Macron secured a deal with Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, the UAE and Bahrain for 300,000 tonnes of diesel a month.

Pentagon investigates base freezer failures as suspected hack

NORMAL Canada / Europe HIGH · 60
Canada-EU summit draft eyes Galileo and IRIS2 access
A draft joint statement from a two-day Canada-EU summit in Montreal proposes negotiations for Canada to access services from Europes Galileo satellite constellation and to explore Canadian participation in the European IRIS2 satellite communications project, alongside cooperation on Arctic maritime capabilities and critical raw materials for defence industries. The draft does not announce new troop deployments or weapons purchases, and language describing Canada as an EU associate member floated by European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen in September has been removed, replaced with the phrase ever-closer association of Canada to the EU, short of full membership itself.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
A Canadian pathway into Galileo and IRIS² would diversify Western satellite-navigation dependencies away from US systems and open a new procurement and industrial-participation market for space and defence primes operating in both jurisdictions.
