NORMAL North America CRITICAL · 85
Starship reaches orbit, deploys 26 Starlink sats
SpaceX flew Starship on its first orbital mission, with the upper stage completing an orbit-insertion burn using a single Raptor engine after one of six engines shut down during ascent, then deploying 26 third-generation Starlink satellites with ten times the capacity of the previous generation. Mission managers then opted to bring the ship down early, ending the mission approximately three hours after launch with a Pacific Ocean splashdown north of Hawaii rather than the originally planned splashdown off Chile after six orbits. NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman congratulated SpaceX and described the mission as getting Ship to orbit and managing every step in a safe, responsible and inspirational way.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Starship reaching orbit and deploying operational satellites in a single mission removes the last programmatic uncertainty blocking NASA Artemis planning and validates SpaceXs heavy-lift commercial architecture at a moment when the US military is also evaluating Starship for large-payload national security missions.

NORMAL North America CRITICAL · 80
Falcon Heavy flies first NRO mission on NSSL Phase 3
A SpaceX Falcon Heavy launched the classified NROL-97 payload from Launch Complex 39A at NASA Kennedy Space Center at 11:54 p.m. EDT, marking the first time the National Reconnaissance Office has used the heavy-lift vehicle after 22 prior missions flown on Falcon 9. The mission was the first NRO flight procured through the competitive National Security Space Launch Phase 3 Lane 2 contract, with both side boosters recovered at Cape Canaveral Space Force Station while the core stage was expended. NROL-97 was the third Falcon launch of the day for SpaceX and the first time the company landed four boosters in a single day.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
The NROs first Falcon Heavy mission under NSSL Phase 3 Lane 2 signals a deliberate shift toward heavier classified payloads, expanding SpaceXs footprint in the most sensitive segment of US national security space launch and increasing competitive pressure on United Launch Alliance.

NORMAL North America CRITICAL · 78
Space Force awards GHOST-R contracts to Northrop and True Anomaly
Space Systems Command awarded prototype contracts to Northrop Grumman and True Anomaly on September 18 under the Geosynchronous High-Resolution Optical Space-Based Tactical Reconnaissance programme, known as GHOST-R, to build satellites capable of delivering high-resolution space-to-space imagery of objects more than 22,000 miles above Earth. Both companies are already under separate Space Force contracts to develop space-based interceptors for the Golden Dome missile defence initiative. The new GHOST-R prototypes are set to launch directly to geosynchronous orbit by 2028 and fly government-owned and operated missions by 2029, officials said.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
GHOST-R prototype awards to both an established prime and a four-year-old startup accelerate the targeting layer of US space domain awareness, directly supporting the ability to negate threats in geostationary orbit and making both companies worth tracking as the Golden Dome programme matures.

NORMAL Asia-Pacific HIGH · 72
China posts ninth and tenth September launches
A Long March 8A carried the 26th batch of Guowang satellites into roughly 1,100-kilometre orbits on September 23, followed within a day by a Long March 6A delivering the fourth group of Yaogan-40 satellites from Taiyuan Satellite Launch Center on September 24, completing Chinas ninth and tenth launches of September. The pace included four rockets launching from three sites within 48 hours on September 15 and 16, with the Guowang constellation managed by China SatNet and planned to comprise around 13,000 satellites. The pair of launches took China to 70 orbital launch attempts for 2026, with the country aiming to surpass 100 launches in a calendar year for the first time.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Chinas sustained September cadence, combining a national broadband constellation with reconnaissance satellite expansion, signals a maturing dual-use launch infrastructure that US Space Force is tracking in real time and that directly competes with Western LEO satcom and intelligence architectures.

NORMAL North America HIGH · 65
Crew-13 launches to ISS on eight-hour Dragon transit
A SpaceX Falcon 9 lifted off from pad 40 at Cape Canaveral Space Force Station at 11:10 a.m. EDT on Thursday, carrying NASA commander Jessica Watkins, pilot Luke Delaney, Roscosmos cosmonaut Sergey Teteryatnikov and Canadian Space Agency astronaut Joshua Kutryk to the International Space Station, with docking planned at the forward port of the Harmony module at 7 p.m., approximately eight hours after launch. The mission had been delayed from a mid-September target after a leaking valve in the Crew Dragon propulsion system required replacement. Canadian astronaut Kutruk and his wife are expecting their third child around Thanksgiving, about two months into the mission, making them the fourth couple in space history to have a child born while one parent was off the planet.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
The record transit time demonstrates maturing Dragon operational efficiency and reinforces SpaceXs position as the sole US crew ferry to the ISS ahead of Boeings planned unpiloted Starliner test flight later this year.

NORMAL North America HIGH · 62
NASA plans unpiloted Starliner test in December-January window
NASA and Boeing are targeting a December-to-January unpiloted Starliner test flight, designated Starliner 1, to validate redesigned propulsion valves and upgraded subsystems following the 2024 mission that stranded two astronauts on the space station for more than nine months. If the uncrewed flight succeeds, a piloted Starliner mission commanded by veteran astronaut Warren Hoburg is planned for mid-2028, incorporating redesigned service module thruster poppets, replacement valves in all 12 crew module maneuvering thrusters, a faster flight data recorder, upgraded batteries and improved parachutes. NASA cited the need for two US crew transportation providers to support the ISS through its planned retirement in 2030 and future commercial stations thereafter.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Boeings path back to crewed spaceflight depends entirely on the unpiloted test outcome, and a second propulsion failure would effectively consolidate US crew access at SpaceX until at least 2029, a significant single-provider risk for NASA and a material programme uncertainty for Boeing investors.

URGENT North America CRITICAL · 88
DoD loses buying power as reconciliation funds miss deadline
The Pentagon obligated approximately $142 billion of the roughly $152 billion in defence funding from last years One Big Beautiful Bill reconciliation legislation before the fiscal 2027 start on October 1, leaving about 7 percent unspent and subject to an 8.3 percent sequestration cut, according to a source familiar with the matter cited by Breaking Defense. A defence official confirmed that only 93 percent of the total amount had been obligated ahead of fiscal 2027 but could not provide further details about what funding lines remained unspent. The unobligated residual covered modernisation priorities including Golden Dome, Arleigh Burke-class destroyers, munitions and fighter jets.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
The sequestration of roughly $830 million from unobligated reconciliation funds, combined with industry warnings that Congress has not yet appropriated against the largest multiyear munitions contracts, creates a direct funding gap at the moment US operational tempo in the Gulf demands maximum restocking velocity.

NORMAL North America CRITICAL · 85
Raytheon wins $24.4B SM-6 multiyear Navy contract
RTXs Raytheon unit received a multiyear contract with a ceiling of up to $24.4 billion from the US Navy to produce Standard Missile-6 interceptors over five years with two additional option years, as the Pentagon pushes to replenish munitions stockpiles depleted by Middle East operations. ◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
The SM-6 ceiling of up to $24.4 billion reinforces RTXs position as the most exposed beneficiary of US munitions restocking, but the investment case is contingent on congressional appropriation, and absent that action the production ramp the carrier deployment demands cannot be fully funded.

Saudi Arabia plans Houthi offensive to reopen Red Sea lane
Saudi Arabia is preparing an offensive against Houthi militants in Yemen expected to launch in the coming weeks, led by Yemeni ground forces overseen by Riyadh and supported by Saudi air strikes, with estimates from people with knowledge of the preparations ranging from within a week to after the US midterm elections in early November, according to regional and Western officials cited by Reuters. Taiwan receives first two F-16V Block 70 jets after delays
The first two of 66 F-16V Block 70 fighters from a 2019 US-approved $8 billion Lockheed Martin sale landed at Chihhang Air Base in Taitung on Friday after months of delays caused by software problems, with Taiwan President Lai Ching-te publicly thanking the United States for its support. NORMAL India / Gulf region HIGH · 60
India extends Gulf export risk cover to March 2027
India has extended its government-backed export insurance scheme for West Asia shipments, known as RELIEF, until March 31, 2027, administered by ECGC Ltd under the Export Promotion Mission. The scheme was originally approved on March 19, 2026, in response to surging freight and war-risk premiums following escalation around the Strait of Hormuz, and its Component II now provides up to 95 percent credit risk cover above standard ECGC terms for qualifying new shipments to destinations including the UAE, Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, Israel, Qatar, Oman, Bahrain, Iraq, Iran and Yemen. Of the schemes total 497 crore rupee allocation, 282 crore rupees is ring-fenced for Component III, which targets MSME exporters who held no ECGC insurance during the disruption period.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
The continued extension of sovereign export risk cover signals that Indian exporters and their insurers still regard Gulf shipping lanes as materially elevated in risk, reinforcing the strategic importance of Hormuz security and maritime defence capabilities in the region.

France plans crewed-drone Hypairion flight in 2028
Frances Air Force, Directorate General for Armament and the Ministerial Agency for Defence AI plan to fly crewed fighter jets alongside combat drones in 2028 under a project called Hypairion, which aims to develop open, modular and government-owned architecture for combat aircraft rather than relying on proprietary industry systems, according to Col. NORMAL United States / Arizona HIGH · 70
US Army opens three permanent counter-drone test lanes
The US Army has established three permanent counter-drone test lanes at Yuma Proving Ground in Arizona, each focused on a separate function: sensor detection, electronic jamming or takeover, and kinetic defeat of small drones. The lanes were used during Falcon Peak 26.2, an operational experiment that spent much of September testing systems from multiple companies against scenarios modelled on drone incursions along the US southern border, run by Joint Interagency Task Force 401 in support of US Northern Command and Joint Task Force Southern Border. On September 28 the Army awarded counter-drone contracts to 10 companies including Allen Control Systems with a combined ceiling of $4.15 billion to support its Domestic Shield effort.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Permanent dedicated test infrastructure at Yuma accelerates the evaluation and fielding cycle for counter-UAS technologies, increasing procurement velocity and creating a sustained competitive testing environment that favours vendors with mature detect-jam-defeat integrated solutions.

USAF CCA programme nears autonomy software downselect
The US Air Force is approaching a downselect for mission autonomy software for its Collaborative Combat Aircraft programme, with Brig Gen Timothy Helfrich stating at a Mitchell Institute webinar on September 28 that the service is getting close to that decision, according to Shephard Media. Italy risks losing up to €8B in defence windfall
Italian Deputy Prime Minister Matteo Salvini succeeded at a Cabinet meeting in pushing down the amount Italy would draw under the EU National Escape Clause, cutting the projected defence windfall from between €21 billion and €22 billion to around €14 billion, a drop of up to €8 billion, according to a government source cited by Defense News.
