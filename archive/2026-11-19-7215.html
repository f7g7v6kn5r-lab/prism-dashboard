URGENT North America CRITICAL · 90
Vulcan loses 40 Amazon Leo satellites in anomaly
United Launch Alliances Vulcan rocket lifted off at 12:40 p.m. EST from Space Launch Complex 41 at Cape Canaveral Space Force Station on 15 November and suffered an anomaly about 10 minutes into flight, with ULA later confirming the vehicle failed to deliver the Amazon mission to orbit and all 40 Amazon Leo satellites were lost. The mission, called Vulcan Leo 1 or LV-01, had already slipped twice before Sunday, and ULA said it did not yet have specific data to understand what occurred. No return-to-flight date has been announced, and Amazon must wait for ULAs investigation before using Vulcan again, while its Federal Communications Commission license requires the full first-generation constellation in orbit by July 30, 2029.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
The failure directly delays Amazons competitive entry into satellite broadband for rural markets and raises urgent questions about Vulcans readiness as a primary heavy-lift alternative to SpaceX for US government and commercial customers.

URGENT Europe CRITICAL · 82
Iceye makes 2 billion cash offer for Avio
Finnish operator Iceye made a 2 billion cash offer, equivalent to $2.2 billion, for Italian rocket maker Avio, pledging 1 billion in investment in Avio in Italy over five years to expand the customer base for Avios Vega rocket and to put Iceye in charge of Avios expansion into the US missile business. Iceye said the offer of 41 per share, to be launched through wholly owned Italian subsidiary Iris 2, carried a 21.3 percent premium to Avios closing price on Monday and a 34.8 percent premium to its three-month volume-weighted average price. Leonardo, which holds an 18 percent stake in Avio, said it was pleasantly surprised by the bid, while Avios board confirmed it had begun assessing the offer.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
If completed, the deal would create a vertically integrated European space and defence player combining SAR intelligence with sovereign launch capability, directly challenging US and Chinese dominance in both markets and reshaping European industrial consolidation dynamics.

Acting Navy Secretary Hung Cao told the Senate Armed Services Committee during his confirmation hearing that the US Navy recoded and fired missiles classified as unserviceable, designated code echo and code foxtrot under Defense Logistics Agency classification, during Operation Epic Fury and the Iran war. ◆ PRISM ANALYSIS

Pentagon IG flags fraud risk as $1.5T defence budget sought
Days after the Pentagon failed its ninth consecutive audit, the Department of Defense Inspector General released its annual report identifying eliminating fraud and achieving a clean financial audit among the departments most pressing strategic challenges for the upcoming fiscal year, alongside deterring China and energising the defence industrial base. The Trump administration is asking Congress for a record $1.5 trillion for national defence, a 42 percent increase on current funding, against a backdrop in which the 2026 National Defense Strategy elevated homeland defence and the Trump Corollary to the Monroe Doctrine above countering China. ◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
A ninth consecutive failed audit combined with a record $1.5 trillion budget request and a confirmed wartime munitions drawdown creates compounding oversight risk that could constrain supplemental appropriations and delay surge procurement at the worst possible moment for readiness.

Hanwha fires Chunmoo missiles from containerised sea launcher
Hanwha Defense USA fired two Chunmoo missiles from a palletizable multi-pod launcher system aboard a test range vessel operated by the Spaceport Company off the coast of Mississippi, demonstrating that heavy maritime strike capability can be packaged into shipping-container-sized units. NORMAL Europe CRITICAL · 75
EU proposes 45 billion Ukraine loan tied to reparations
The European Commission proposed a new 45 billion dollar-equivalent loan to Ukraine to help fill a projected 70 billion dollar-equivalent funding gap for next year, with European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen stating Ukraine would only need to repay the loan after Russia had paid reparations. Up to 25 billion of the loan could be spent on military aid and up to 20 billion on general budget support, requiring approval from EU member countries and coming on top of a previously agreed 90 billion loan covering 2026 and 2027. Von der Leyen said she was confident all EU members would back the new loan, even though three — Hungary, the Czech Republic and Slovakia — opted out of the last one.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
The reparations-linked repayment structure insulates Ukraine from near-term debt obligations while sustaining European defence industry order flow, reinforcing a long-duration demand signal for European primes and ammunition manufacturers through 2027 and beyond.
