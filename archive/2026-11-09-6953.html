NORMAL Asia Pacific HIGH · 72
Taiwan targets independent satellite launches
President Lai Ching-te opened TASTI 2026 in Tainan and confirmed the government had raised funding for the third phase of its National Space Technology Long-term Development Program to NT$71 billion, approximately US$2.16 billion, and extended its implementation through 2031. Lai confirmed a rocket research, development, assembly and testing facility approved for Shalun in Tainan and a national launch site selected at Jiupeng in Pingtung County, with the Shalun base expected operational by 2030 and the Jiupeng facility expected to be completed and operational by 2032. Taiwan currently relies on foreign launch service providers such as SpaceX to send its satellites into orbit.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Taiwan committing NT$71 billion toward sovereign launch infrastructure and domestic satellite development, with programme implementation extended through 2031, reduces its dependence on foreign providers such as SpaceX and signals a new state-funded market for launch, satellite and testing suppliers willing to operate within a geopolitically sensitive Indo-Pacific environment.

Quantum Space joins Arrow team on $185M NASA award
Quantum Space announced its selection as subcontractor to Arrow Science and Technology on a $185 million award under NASAs Moon Base Transportation program, with Quantum Space receiving approximately 25 percent of the total contract value. CCIA urges spectrum reform for satellite access
The Computer and Communications Industry Association released a report titled Connecting the World: Unlocking the Potential of Satellite Connectivity, recommending that policymakers modernize outdated spectrum and licensing rules, strengthen satellite supply chains and launch capacity, and adopt technology-neutral policies to improve connectivity affordability and digital literacy. NORMAL North America CRITICAL · 82
Pentagon loans Neros $450M for small drone manufacturing
The Defense Departments Office of Strategic Capital provided a $450 million conditional loan to California-based Neros Technologies to expand domestic manufacturing of small drone components, with Neros committing an additional $165 million to bring total investment to $615 million. The loan follows a previous $820 million Office of Strategic Capital loan to drone maker PDW in July, as the Pentagon works to reduce dependence on Chinese suppliers who currently account for the vast majority of small drone components. Neros head of growth Ross Pederson said the company expects that by late next year around 80 percent by dollar value of the drone should be made in-house.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
The OSC is systematically capitalising a domestic small drone industrial base through direct government lending rather than traditional procurement, a structural shift that advantages US-manufactured UAS suppliers and disadvantages China-dependent component networks ahead of any near-peer conflict procurement surge.

NORMAL Europe HIGH · 72
Optics11 eyes unicorn valuation on navy sonar expansion
Dutch startup Optics11 is in talks with dozens of navies about its optical-fiber sensing technology for underwater monitoring, with between five and ten navies already signed on as clients and around 35 paid trials planned this year, CEO Paul Heiden told Defense News at the Euronaval maritime-defense exhibition. ◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Optics11s rapid client acquisition across German, Swedish, British, Belgian and French navies, combined with a projected unicorn-level fundraise, signals that optical-fiber underwater sensing is transitioning from niche technology to a NATO-wide procurement priority that is drawing serious institutional capital.

NORMAL Europe HIGH · 68
Kongsberg pitches converted trawlers for UK Arctic ASW
Norways Kongsberg Maritime is pitching an unmanned Arctic Hunter concept to the British Royal Navy, based on a converted 56-meter industry-standard fishing trawler with its fish-processing systems replaced by autonomy features, Thales UK sonar and secure communications, capable of operating unattended for roughly 60 days at a time. The proposal responds to a UK requirement for unmanned vessels to assist anti-submarine warfare, with the concept of operations envisioning a flotilla of 10 ships based west of the Bear Gap to intercept Russian submarines transiting to the open Atlantic. Kongsberg Maritime said it would build the Type 92 vessels in the United Kingdom if selected.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
A cost-effective converted-vessel approach to Arctic anti-submarine warfare, if adopted, would expand the addressable market for Thales UK sonar and autonomy suppliers well beyond traditional warship hull programmes, while giving the Royal Navy persistent Arctic coverage at a fraction of a bespoke warship programme cost.

White House leads multi-agency counter-drone effort
The White House Task Force led an inter-agency effort in which the Department of Transportation, Department of Defense, Department of Homeland Security, Department of Energy and Department of Justice signed a memo to evaluate and rapidly deploy counter-drone technologies to protect aviation safety and national security, with the FAA and FCC involved in developing systems to detect, track, stop or take control of dangerous or unauthorised drones. ◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
A whole-of-government US counter-drone mandate backed by NATO commitments creates an accelerated procurement pipeline for detection, tracking and electronic defeat technologies, directly benefiting suppliers with evaluated systems ready for rapid deployment.

NORMAL United States HIGH · 65
Pentagon FORTRESS stockpile plan hits minerals gap
The Pentagon programme FORTRESS America, formally established on September 30, aims to stockpile critical minerals, fuel and munitions on US soil at a scale described by Hudson Institute senior fellow Bryan Clark as on par with what the DoD pursued during the Cold War, but faces a severe constraint because US demand for the most common type of rare-earth magnet reached roughly 48,000 metric tons in 2025 while domestic sources supplied just 300 metric tons. China dominates the processing of many critical minerals and has kept American companies short of supply even in peacetime, according to Fortune. A senior defense official told reporters on October 20 that officials are still assessing costs, with one estimate suggesting the broader initiative could cost scores of billions of dollars, if not hundreds of billions.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
The minerals bottleneck in FORTRESS America exposes a structural vulnerability in US defence readiness and creates a long-term procurement opportunity for non-Chinese rare-earth processing and magnet manufacturing capacity.

NORMAL Switzerland HIGH · 60
Swiss vote Nov 29 on easing arms export curbs
Swiss voters will decide on 29 November whether to block a parliamentary amendment that would allow arms shipments to 36 Western countries even if those countries are involved in active armed conflicts, in a referendum proposed by a coalition of left-wing parties including Social Democrats and the Greens. The amendment would also scrap a requirement for countries to sign a non-re-export declaration on purchase, though Ukraine is not included in the list of eligible recipients and the amendment would not apply to weaponry bought on previous terms or bought with the intention of immediately sending it to a third party. Switzerland was the worlds 17th-largest arms exporter over the past 10 years according to data from the Stockholm International Peace Research Institute, with the sector employing 14,000 people and exports worth around $1.15 billion last year.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
A yes vote preserving the existing restrictions would sustain Switzerland as a constrained supplier to European rearmament partners, while a no vote defeating the referendum would open new export revenue lines for Swiss arms firms at a moment of surging European defence demand.
