NORMAL Europe HIGH · 62
Orbital Matter 3D-prints beam in orbit
German startup Orbital Matter successfully 3D-printed a half-metre-long rigid cylindrical beam aboard its Replicator 2 satellite, launched on a SpaceX rideshare mission this summer, marking what the company describes as a first for a free-flying spacecraft in orbit. The printer extrudes coils of resin cured by ultraviolet light, with Orbital Matter partnering with the European Space Agency and Thales Alenia on the technology, which targets future orbital data centres and large power structures too big to fit inside a rocket fairing.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
In-space manufacturing of structural components removes one of the core payload-volume constraints on large orbital platforms, opening a new segment for investors tracking on-orbit construction and deep-space infrastructure.

NORMAL North America MEDIUM · 48
Private space hardware investment hit $17B in 2025
A research report by investor Chamath Palihapitiya, published by Social Capital and titled Deep Dive: Where Will Value Accrue In The Space Economy?, documents that global private investment in space hardware rose from approximately $500 million in 2012 to $17 billion in 2025, a 34-fold increase that holds even when SpaceX is excluded. The report notes that the US Space Force budget surpassed NASAs by fiscal year 2023, and that the Space Forces regular FY2027 budget request is three times NASAs, excluding an additional $12.1 billion in one-off funding.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
The divergence between Space Force budget trajectory and NASAs declining federal share signals that government-driven demand for space infrastructure is now overwhelmingly a defence procurement story, not a civil science one, reshaping where commercial operators should anchor their revenue expectations.

Chennai-based Inbound Aerospace, incubated at IIT Madras, is seeking approximately $10 million — equivalent to around 90 crore rupees — in seed funding to develop an autonomous re-entry vehicle with a precision parachute-based landing system, targeting a first orbital demonstration mission in 2028. Two US airmen deaths raise Iran war toll to 23
Two US airmen died last week in non-hostile incidents — one illness or injury related, one listed as pending investigation — raising the total American fatalities since the Iran war began to 23, according to the Pentagon Defense Casualty Analysis System. Pentagon offers conditional $450M loan to drone maker Neros
The Pentagons Office of Strategic Capital has signed a conditional loan commitment of up to $450 million with US drone manufacturer Neros, supporting a planned $615 million investment to mass-produce American attack drones and expand domestic component manufacturing, with the stated objective of reducing reliance on Chinese supply chains, announced on 2 November 2026. Taiwan unfreezes funding for 74 defence budget items
Taiwans Legislative Yuan unfroze funding for 74 budget items under the Ministry of National Defense 2026 general budget, including NT$5 million for more than 1,900 man-portable Stinger missiles, NT$200 million for missile systems for attack drones, NT$100 million for a next-generation light cruiser prototype and NT$80 million for US-made submarine-launched MK48 torpedoes. The partial unfreeze signals incremental progress in Taiwans defence budget execution but the stalled NASAMS and IDS submarine lines show that supplier delivery timelines and diplomatic processes remain the binding constraint on major US-Taiwan arms transfers.
