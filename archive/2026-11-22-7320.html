SpaceX launches classified USSF-317 for Space Force
A SpaceX Falcon 9 lifted off from Space Launch Complex 4 East at Vandenberg Space Force Base at 8:20 p.m. Pacific Standard Time on November 20, 2026, carrying the classified USSF-317 national security payload for the US Space Force. USSF-317 is the sixth flight in a series of Space Force missions assigned to SpaceX that began in August, following USSF-627 which launched on November 12, with 23 satellites catalogued after each of the five preceding launches. The booster, designated B1100, flew for the 11th time and landed aboard the droneship Of Course I Still Love You in the Pacific Ocean.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS

NORMAL North America HIGH · 62
F-35C completes carrier launch trials on USS Kennedy
Two Navy F-35C Lightning II fighters completed the first round of launch and recovery testing aboard the future USS John F. Kennedy, the second Gerald R. Ford-class aircraft carrier, over 11 days beginning October 13, logging 122 catapult launches and 122 arrested landings across 34 flights, Lockheed Martin said. The tests used the Electromagnetic Aircraft Launch System and Advanced Arresting Gear unique to the Ford class, validating compatibility with the redesigned flight deck and an island set about 140 feet farther back than on Nimitz-class ships. Lockheed said the Navy has now cleared the F-35C for operations across its entire fleet of aircraft carriers upon completion of this test phase.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Successful carrier compatibility testing for the F-35C on the Ford-class platform removes a key integration risk for the Navys next-generation carrier air wing and strengthens Lockheed Martins position ahead of further F-35 lot negotiations.

Two tankers struck in Strait of Hormuz amid Iran tensions
Two oil tankers were attacked in the Strait of Hormuz on Saturday, with the United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations reporting one vessel struck by an unknown projectile that damaged its stern with no casualties, and a second struck in its engine room. Iran separately claimed a tanker caught fire after striking a naval mine east of the strait, alleging the vessel had switched off its identification and navigation systems, though the US military had previously stated it cleared all mines from the main shipping routes through the waterway. A Qatari delegation visited Tehran on Friday evening and held approximately two hours of discussions, with Iranian Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi confirming the visit without disclosing details or any progress toward resolving the conflict.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Simultaneous attacks on two commercial tankers in the worlds most critical energy chokepoint, combined with contradictory mine-clearance claims, pose a direct threat to energy supply chain continuity that Gulf-based operators and tanker insurers cannot absorb without immediate route and cost reassessment.

URGENT North America CRITICAL · 88
CSIS warns US missile stocks face multiyear rebuild
A Center for Strategic and International Studies analysis found that the United States expended between one-third and two-thirds of key munition inventories in the 39-day campaign against Iran until the April 8 ceasefire and subsequent operations, based on data current as of November 3. The report assessed that seven of nine missile categories carry detrimental effects for resupply capacity, with Patriot missiles, Terminal High Altitude Area Defense missiles and Standard Missiles listed as heavily used with no substitutes. The analysis warned that rebuilding inventories will require a multiyear commitment extending beyond the current crisis, and that while the US remains capable of leveraging existing missiles in the short term, shortages of long-range munitions may force reliance on munitions with ranges under 300 miles.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
A multiyear munitions rebuild requirement across seven of nine critical categories converts a tactical inventory problem into a structural procurement opportunity, favouring missile and precision-strike manufacturers able to accelerate production rates under existing or new Pentagon agreements.

URGENT North America CRITICAL · 85
GAO warns Pentagon lacks munitions production plan
The Government Accountability Office, publishing the unclassified version of a study completed in September 2026, warned that the Department of Defense does not have an overarching plan linking munitions production and funding to requirements, with Pentagon officials acknowledging they can be reactive to global events rather than driven by total requirements. The report found instances where the Army ramped up production of 155-millimeter artillery shells without basing the increase on its total requirement for that munition, and cited a new Texas plant costing nearly $500 million that failed to produce any shell components. GAO recommended that the Department of Defense create an overarching plan to match munitions requirements, production and funding, as well as metrics to track progress.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
The absence of a binding production plan means that even as Congress considers emergency munitions funding, manufacturers face demand signals that could be redirected or cancelled before long-term production investment is justified, creating contract and revenue uncertainty across the artillery and precision-strike supply chain.

Pentagon selects seven laser systems for drone shoot-off
The Pentagon selected seven high-energy laser systems from 13 industry proposals for live-fire counter-drone trials at Dugway Proving Ground in mid-December 2026, with the Office of the Under Secretary for Research and Engineering and Joint Interagency Task Force 401 jointly leading the selection. Trump and Xi agree humans must oversee military AI decisions

NORMAL Germany HIGH · 60
German attack drone variants pass live-fire trials
Two German one-way attack drone variants, Starks Virtus and Helsings HX-2, passed live-fire trials held the week of November 9 at the Bundeswehrs weapons and ammunition test centre in Meppen, clearing a step toward initial deliveries and troop training before the end of the year, Germanys military procurement office said. The procurement office announced both systems passed but did not disclose how many shots were fired or the weapons success rates, with testing focused on the approach to the target through the effect of the warhead rather than certifying the systems for service. The systems have not yet completed qualification, but the German Army plans to begin equipping its armored brigade in Lithuania in 2027, with contracts worth 268 million euros each approved for Stark and Helsing by Germanys parliamentary budget committee in a February 25 vote.

NORMAL Netherlands HIGH · 65
Netherlands targets 40 percent defence industry growth by 2030
The Netherlands presented a defence-industrial plan targeting 40 percent growth in its defence technological and industrial base by 2030, with local defence industry revenue aimed at reaching around 15 billion euros, and the government has earmarked 1.6 billion euros for the plan. The plan focuses on integrated air and missile defence, data-driven command and digital resilience, unmanned and autonomous systems, and military sustainment, with spending including 600 million euros for industrial ecosystems and scaling production, 320 million euros for international cooperation and 150 million euros on supply chain resilience. The Netherlands is counting on the domestic share of Dutch defence-industry revenue to grow 50 percent by 2030 to more than 10 billion euros, while exports are targeted to grow by about 25 percent.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
A 1.6 billion euro government commitment to defence-industrial expansion in a country that lacks a domestic prime but hosts major Thales radar operations signals new procurement and partnership opportunities for mid-tier suppliers and technology integrators across Europe.
