URGENT North America CRITICAL · 90
Vulcan failure haunts US space plans
On November 15, United Launch Alliance's Vulcan rocket suffered a mission-ending malfunction 10 minutes after launch, destroying its payload of Amazon Leo broadband satellites in the first total mission failure in ULA history. Early indications from ULA point to low first-stage performance resulting in stage separation at an altitude and velocity too low to reach orbit, with a November 18 update suggesting the cause was inadequate fuel supply to the BE-4 engines rather than a problem with the engines themselves. The loss carries implications for Amazon, the US Space Force, and NASA's Artemis moon program, which depends on ULA's Centaur V upper stage.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
With Vulcan now grounded after back-to-back problematic flights, the US has fewer assured heavy-lift pathways for both commercial broadband and national security payloads, concentrating launch dependency on SpaceX at a moment when the Space Force cannot afford schedule slippage.

NORMAL South Asia HIGH · 65
Skyroot Vikram-1 orbits payload for India
On July 18, 2026, Skyroot Aerospace's Vikram-1 rocket carried half a dozen small satellites to low-Earth orbit 280 miles up from the Satish Dhawan Space Centre, marking the first time India orbited a payload launched by a privately built and owned rocket. Prime Minister Narendra Modi called it a defining moment in India's space journey, while Union Minister Jitendra Singh witnessed the launch at the site. Skyroot founder and CEO Pawan Kumar Chandana, 35, started the Hyderabad-based company in 2018 after six years as a rocket engineer at the Indian Space Research Organization.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Indias entry into private orbital launch validates a new competitive corridor that could attract international small-satellite customers and defence-adjacent ISR constellation operators looking for non-Western launch options.

NORMAL Europe HIGH · 60
MBDA and Atmos study orbital deep-strike
Pan-European missile maker MBDA and German startup Atmos Space Cargo announced a cooperation agreement to jointly assess the feasibility of deploying precision deep-strike weapons from Earth orbit using reentry vehicles. The agreement covers system architecture, technical feasibility, payload integration and possible technology demonstrations, with Atmos CEO Sebastian Klaus arguing Europe needs an orbital deterrent able to strike any legitimate military target anywhere on Earth within 60 minutes. Atmos will contribute expertise in orbital transportation and reentry technology, with MBDA providing capabilities in defense systems and military payloads.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
If technically validated, an orbital conventional strike capability would reshape European deterrence procurement and create a new high-value market segment for reentry vehicle and mission-systems suppliers including MBDA, at a time when European defence autonomy spending is accelerating.

Iran strikes Kuwait and Bahrain after Qeshm hit
Kuwait and Bahrain said they both intercepted Iranian strikes on Wednesday, with the Kuwaiti Army posting that air defenses were countering hostile drone attacks and the Bahrain Defense Force confirming it responded to a number of Iranian air attacks. URGENT North America CRITICAL · 90
US Navy fired expired munitions in Iran war
Acting Navy Secretary Hung Cao told the Senate Armed Services Committee during his nomination hearing that the US Navy recoded and fired missiles classified as unserviceable during Operation Epic Fury and the Iran war, describing them as code echo and code foxtrot materiel. The Washington Post reported the US military burned through at least 850 Tomahawk missiles after the first month of Operation Epic Fury, with the Center for Strategic and International Studies estimating at the time that the US still had 3,000 remaining. The US military was awarded a $22.9 billion seven-year contract to Raytheon in August to increase Tomahawk production to more than 1,000 missiles a year.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
The confirmation that combat-fired munitions included materiel previously classified as unserviceable directly validates the case for accelerated Tomahawk and long-range cruise missile procurement, strengthening the investment thesis for manufacturers in that segment at a moment when the stockpile is demonstrably depleted.

URGENT Europe CRITICAL · 85
Ukraine collapses Kerch bridge arch with mesh drones
On the nights of November 15 and 16, coordinated Ukrainian drones and missiles caused enough damage to collapse one of two steel arches supporting the Kerch Strait Bridge roadway, according to an Institute for the Study of War update. The operation used an airborne mesh-radio communications network, allowing operators more than 250 kilometers away to remotely guide missiles and drones, adapting a technique Russia had developed for its own long-range strikes, according to the Financial Times. Ukrainian units involved deliberately avoided using Starshield, the military version of Musk's Starlink satellite network, because of US restrictions preventing its use on drones or anything traveling more than 100 miles per hour.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Ukraines successful adaptation of Russian mesh-radio relay tactics to strike a defended strategic target without military Starlink validates an autonomous communications architecture that reduces dependence on any single satellite network — a capability gap multiple NATO members are now racing to close.

URGENT Iran CRITICAL · 88
B-2 crews recount first Fordow strike in detail
Air and Space Forces Magazine has published an account based on interviews with crew members from the B-2s that took part in Operation Midnight Hammer, describing the moment two 30,000-pound Massive Ordnance Penetrators were dropped on Iran's Fordow Fuel Enrichment Plant. The 14 pilots who flew roundtrip from Whiteman Air Force Base in Missouri to Iran were drawn equally from the 509th and 131st Bomb Wings, half active-duty and half from the Missouri Air National Guard. After-action assessments concluded the bombs destroyed much of Iran's ability to enrich nuclear material to weapons-grade, with David Albright of the Institute for Science and International Security saying the centrifuge program as it was no longer exists.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
The first on-record pilot testimony from Operation Midnight Hammer confirms the operational employment of the Massive Ordnance Penetrator against a hardened underground nuclear facility, establishing a live reference case for deep-penetration strike doctrine and future procurement arguments.

UNIFIL exit plan set as handover complications grow
UNIFIL officials are finalising withdrawal plans after 48 years in southern Lebanon, with the mandate expiring at 11:59 p.m. on December 31 and troops expected to begin departing in the first months of 2027, Defense News reports. Gulf states rank among safest in Gallup survey
Gallup's 2026 Global Safety Report found that 95 percent of adults polled in Saudi Arabia in 2025 said they felt safe walking alone at night, the third-highest score among the 140 countries and territories surveyed, Arab News reports. EU proposes €45B Ukraine loan tied to reparations
The European Commission proposed a new €45 billion loan to Ukraine on Wednesday to help fill a funding gap of €70 billion for next year as Kyiv continues its fight against Russia's 2022 invasion.
