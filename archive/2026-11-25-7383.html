URGENT Europe CRITICAL · 95
Russia may orbit nuclear satellite within 60 days
German public broadcasters WDR and NDR and the Sueddeutsche Zeitung reported an intelligence assessment from early November stating that a satellite dubbed Sputnik-S, reportedly assembled near Moscow, could be launched within 60 days. Placing a nuclear weapon in orbit violates the 1967 Outer Space Treaty, to which Russia and 117 other states are parties; detonation in space would destroy or incapacitate large numbers of satellites and could collapse communications and power grids on Earth through its electromagnetic pulse. NATO chief Mark Rutte, asked about the reporting during a visit to Berlin, said the alliance would always ensure it defends every inch of NATO territory, including the space element.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
If the sixty-day assessment is accurate, every commercial and government satellite constellation operator faces an acute survivability risk that no current insurance or continuity framework adequately prices.

Cyber threats rise as orbital economy hits $686 billion
Recorded Future reports that 329 orbital launch attempts were made in 2025, up from 263 the year before, placing a record 4,517 satellites in orbit, 87 percent of them commercially owned, within a space economy that reached $686 billion. The analysis highlights that the February 2022 AcidRain malware attack on the KA-SAT broadband network, launched roughly an hour before Russian forces crossed into Ukraine, knocked out tens of thousands of satellite modems across Europe and caused collateral damage including loss of remote monitoring for around 5,800 wind turbines in Germany. Air Force Secretary Troy Meink confirmed on September 14, 2026 that the United States has on-orbit space-control weapons capable of offensive and defensive use, while the UK stood up No. III Space Effects Squadron nine days later.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Operators and investors building dual-use constellation businesses must treat ground-segment cyber hardening as a first-order cost item, not an afterthought, as the attack surface grows proportionally with each new satellite placed in orbit.

URGENT Middle East CRITICAL · 90
US tanker fleet in Europe and CENTCOM shrinks by 13
Open-source tracking by the Military Air Tracking Alliance recorded 17 KC-135 and KC-46 tanker departures from Europe and CENTCOM between 19 and 22 November, against only four inbound replacements, leaving the deployed fleet 13 aircraft smaller; the tracked fleet had recently peaked at just over 200 aircraft. Flight paths show departures originating from the Gulf, the eastern Mediterranean, Romania, Germany and the United Kingdom, all routing back to bases in the United States. The figures are based on open-source tracking of aircraft transponders and filed flights and do not represent an official US order of battle.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
A net loss of thirteen aerial refuelling aircraft compresses the sustained strike and air-superiority radius available to US commanders in both theatres simultaneously, reducing operational flexibility precisely when Iran policy remains unresolved.

Ukraine defends drone strikes on Russian vessels

URGENT North America CRITICAL · 80
Army sergeant pleads guilty to selling HIMARS secrets to China
Jian Zhao, 37, an active-duty US Army battery supply sergeant stationed at Joint Base Lewis-McChord in Washington, pleaded guilty Monday to conspiring to sell national defense information to China, including excerpts from technical manuals for the HIMARS multiple-rocket launcher system that Ukraine has deployed against Russia. Zhao, who enlisted in 2017, also provided sensitive information related to US military readiness in a potential armed conflict with China and offered military computers and government hard drives for sale; prosecutors described the material as sensitive, secret or top secret. He faces a maximum sentence of 10 years in prison and a fine of $250,000, and is scheduled to be sentenced March 1.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
The compromise of HIMARS technical manuals is directly operationally significant given the systems active combat use in Ukraine, and signals that adversary intelligence collection against high-demand US weapons systems is ongoing and successful at the unit level.

NORMAL Asia-Pacific CRITICAL · 78
Japan steers auto suppliers into defence buildup
Japanese manufacturer Aster, formerly a Toyota parts supplier, now generates a tenth of its revenue from defence work after pivoting to electric motors that power drones built by US military technology company Anduril, illustrating a broader Tokyo initiative to channel existing auto supply chains into military production as Japan's draft defence budget for fiscal 2026 rises 9.4 percent from 2025. Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi's government views the auto-to-defence conversion as the fastest route to scaling production capacity against threats from nuclear-armed neighbors China, Russia and North Korea, with the Acquisition, Technology and Logistics Agency pairing suppliers with arms firms and offering new funding streams. Japan's big three automakers Toyota, Honda and Nissan have shown little appetite for a defence tilt, with Toyota stating its basic position is that it will not do anything related to defence.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Defence investors with exposure to Japans auto supply chain should monitor second-tier parts makers for rapid margin improvement as defence revenue displaces shrinking automotive revenue, creating a structural re-rating opportunity outside traditional prime contractor equities.

NORMAL Global HIGH · 68
Ondas acquires weapons firms worth over $2B after radar pivot
Ondas, a Nasdaq company that until recently sold radios to freight railroads and reported $7.2 million in revenue for all of 2024, has acquired around twenty weapons firms for more than $2 billion over the past eighteen months, reaching a market value near $4 billion; its latest acquisition was Israeli fuze maker Gate Technologies, purchased alongside its Polish manufacturing partner for up to $390 million. Gate Technologies, founded in 1996, builds electronic fuzes integrated into more than ninety weapon systems including loitering munitions, giving Ondas a position inside a critical component of the drone-to-bomb kill chain.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
The rapid Ondas roll-up illustrates that defence-critical component makers — particularly those embedded across dozens of weapon platforms — are attracting non-traditional acquirers who can access public capital markets at valuations legacy primes would not pay, compressing the window for traditional defence firms to consolidate the same assets.

URGENT United States / Iran CRITICAL · 80
Poll: Majority of US voters want Iran war ended now
A University of Maryland and Ipsos Critical Issues Poll of 1,022 US adults found 55 percent want the Iran war ended immediately regardless of terms, with 80 percent of Democrats and 59 percent of independents agreeing; just 21 percent of all respondents favoured continuing on the current path and 18 percent supported escalation. The survey, released Tuesday, also found that a majority of Democratic voters supported Congress withholding funding for military operations and backed an Iran war-funding cutoff even if it risked a government shutdown. Principal investigator Shibley Telhami said he was surprised by the level of support for ending the war regardless of the terms.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Sustained majority public opposition to the conflict, cutting across party lines, raises the credibility of a congressional funding fight and directly increases the political risk premium for defence contractors and Gulf-based operators dependent on US military presence continuity.

NORMAL Israel HIGH · 65
Eisenkot secures first coalition partner in Israel
Gadi Eisenkot's Yashar party announced on Tuesday it had agreed to sign a coalition pact with Avigdor Lieberman's Our Home party, the first alliance of its kind since Israel's October elections, giving Eisenkot a combined 28 seats as he works toward the 61 needed to form a government by his December 6 deadline. NORMAL Arctic / United States HIGH · 58
Coast Guard cutter Storis tests drones in Arctic voyage
The US Coast Guard Cutter Storis, commissioned in August 2025, completed a 133-day voyage around the Arctic Ocean and Alaska, carrying staff from the Coast Guard Research and Development Center and the Pentagon's Defense Innovation Unit to test aerial drones made by Textron and Skyfront in extreme cold conditions. Captain Corey Kerns said the drone trials demonstrated practical uses for the platforms in ice navigation, with the unmanned aerial vehicles helping the ship maintain its intended station by seeing out better and farther than bridge-based piloting alone. Kerns also said the Storis demonstrated a mock refueling capability for a national security cutter, suggesting it could potentially service Navy vessels in the Arctic and save up to a week and a half of transit time off-station.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Validated drone performance in high-Arctic conditions aboard a new icebreaker class extends autonomous ISR and navigation capability into a region of rapidly growing strategic competition, with direct implications for allied Arctic surveillance and logistics programmes.
