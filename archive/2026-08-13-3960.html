Iran vs Trump: Hormuz Control Disputed
Iran has rejected US claims of control over the Strait of Hormuz and insists any shipping route must be under Iranian terms. BREAKING Gulf Region / Arabian Peninsula CRITICAL · 90
Gulf States Race to Bypass Hormuz
Months of blockades and persistent uncertainty around the Strait of Hormuz have driven Gulf energy exporters to urgently fast-track alternative export routes. Both Saudi Arabia and the UAE already operate bypass pipelines -- Saudi Arabias East-West Petroline and the UAE ADCOP pipeline -- though combined available capacity falls well short of the roughly 20 million barrels per day that transited Hormuz pre-war. Saudi Arabia is planning to expand Petroline capacity by up to 2 million bpd. However, UAE loadings at Fujairah have been disrupted by Iranian drone strikes, and Goldman Sachs projects pipeline capacity could cover more than 60 percent of pre-war Gulf export volume only by end-2028, underscoring that infrastructure gaps and targeting vulnerability leave energy markets highly exposed (CNBC, Jul 16, 2026; EnergyNow, Aug 12, 2026).
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
The scramble for alternative routes signals sustained infrastructure investment opportunities in pipeline security, port surveillance, and energy logistics protection across the Arabian Peninsula.
