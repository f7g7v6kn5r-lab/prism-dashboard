URGENT Global CRITICAL · 82
Starshield cap drives push for second LEO network
Military Starshield terminals in Ukraine began dropping links on Oct. 14 whenever they moved faster than about 100 mph, cutting interceptor drone connections in flight and prompting governments to seek alternative low Earth orbit capacity; the Pentagon had already placed OneWeb on contract under its Warrior Plans program, and on Nov. 17 Eutelsat Network Solutions announced a Warrior Plans award it estimates at more than $60 million over a two-year period covering OneWeb airtime, deployable terminals and alternative positioning, navigation and timing. France holds an armed forces contract worth up to one billion euros for priority military access to OneWeb, while Taiwan is in early talks with three to four partners on a network of more than 300 communications satellites, and NATO has begun implementing STARLIFT, a brokerage letting militaries book slots on commercial rockets through spaceports in member countries and South Korea.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Operators and investors in OneWeb and Eutelsat face a genuine demand pull from sovereign governments, but the value of that demand depends entirely on whether a credible launch pipeline can be assembled before the 2030 window closes, making launch-vehicle capacity — not satellite manufacturing — the binding constraint on returns.

Nvidia chips reportedly reached Chinas military via Alibaba
The New York Times reported that Alibaba used its AI computing technology to support a lab it co-founded with strong ties to Chinas military, where researchers worked on tasks including automating cyberattacks and running war games; a Singapore firm called Megaspeed, whose customer and financial backer turned out to be Alibaba, purchased more than $4 billion in Nvidia chips over several years following a pitch that cited former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huangs personal relationship as a guarantee of supply. The direct chain from US commercial AI silicon to Chinese military applications will intensify Congressional pressure on export controls and may accelerate Pentagon requirements for verified, closed-supply AI hardware in defence programmes, reshuffling procurement toward vendors who can demonstrate clean supply chains.

URGENT Global CRITICAL · 75
US calls Cosmos 2612 pass a weapons operation
The United States told a United Nations committee on Oct. 21 that Russian military satellite Cosmos 2612 spent about 24 hours within 2 kilometers of ICEYE-X36 — a US-registered radar imaging satellite operated by ICEYE US that also supplies imagery to Ukraines military — in maneuvers the US said appeared consistent with an on-orbit weapons operation. COMSPOC estimated the closest pass at about 60 meters, while LeoLabs said it has high confidence the satellites came within about 500 meters of each other, and tracking firms told Military.com they see no sign of an intentional approach toward ICEYE-X37, toward which two related Russian satellites were becoming better positioned in early November.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Confirmed proximity operations against commercially operated ISR satellites supplying a warfighting customer establish a precedent that commercial satellite operators providing dual-use imagery now sit inside a military targeting calculus, raising insurance, valuation and regulatory risk for the entire commercial radar-imagery sector.

Philippines in early talks to host SpaceX launch sites
President Ferdinand Marcos Jr., speaking in Shenzhen on November 20, said the Philippines was exploring whether it could serve as a commercial launch site for SpaceX, noting discussions were already under way but announcing no agreement, investment commitment or timetable; he said local facilities could also reduce the cost of putting Philippine satellites into orbit. The Philippine Space Agency reported that engineers completed practical training in South Korea in October 2026 under the SIKLAB project, covering assembly, integration and testing of a liquid-fuelled sounding rocket, with the hardware due to be shipped to the Philippines for a demonstration targeting early 2027 carrying a locally developed payload weighing up to 2 kg to an altitude of at least 5 km.

A SpaceX launch presence in the Philippines would shift the geopolitical geography of commercial launch into the South China Sea corridor, adding sovereign and strategic sensitivity to what Marcos framed as a purely commercial arrangement.

URGENT North America CRITICAL · 92
CSIS warns US long-range missile stocks critically depleted
A Center for Strategic and International Studies analysis found that the US expended between a third and two-thirds of key munition inventories in the 39-day campaign against Iran through the April 8 ceasefire and subsequent operations, with seven of nine assessed missile categories — including Patriot, THAAD and Standard Missiles classed as heavily used with no substitutes, and Tomahawks, AGM-158 JASSMs and Precision Strike Missiles as heavily used with some substitutes — rated as having detrimental resupply conditions. The analysis warned that while the US retains enough munitions to renew the fight against Iran, there is great concern regarding stocks against China, and that rebuilding inventories will require a multiyear commitment extending beyond the current crisis.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Defence investors with exposure to Patriot and THAAD manufacturers face a structurally favourable demand environment, but the multi-year replenishment timeline means revenue recognition will be back-weighted and production bottlenecks — not funding — are the near-term constraint on returns.

NORMAL Europe HIGH · 72
Denmark ratifies Greenland security deal with US
Denmarks parliament unanimously ratified a security agreement with the United States on Greenland that expands the US militarys role on the island, including permission to establish a Golden Dome missile defence system, while reaffirming Danish sovereignty and Greenlanders right to self-determination; the deal was signed in September after Trump repeatedly threatened to take control of the mineral-rich territory of 57,000 people. Danish Defence Minister Jeppe Bruus told Reuters that the parties would now begin work on a new Greenland investment-screening law and plans for two new US military bases as outlined in the agreement, with US Secretary of State Marco Rubio describing it as modernising the US-Denmark-Greenland defence partnership.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Two new US Arctic bases and a Golden Dome deployment on Greenland represent a durable northward shift in US missile defence architecture, creating long-duration infrastructure and sustainment revenue for contractors who can operate in extreme-environment conditions.

Trump and Xi agree humans must control military AI
President Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping agreed that humans should always be involved in military decisions using artificial intelligence and that AI should not be allowed to act autonomously on major military actions, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent told CNBC after the Shenzhen summit. NORMAL Europe HIGH · 55
Europe supermarket cyberattacks expose connectivity risks
Cyberattacks manipulating refrigeration controls hit hundreds of French supermarkets on the night of October 11-12, extending defrost cycles and causing substantial food losses including one cold room at an Intermarche store in Gradignan reported by its owner to have reached 50 degrees, with Reuters documenting a second wave on November 2 affecting supermarkets in Sweden, Belgium and other European countries including another reported case of freezers reaching 50 degrees in Stockholm. Italian authorities confirmed a limited number of stores were affected, and Irish refrigeration incidents were also reported; the incidents are assessed as deliberate manipulation of remotely controlled connected infrastructure rather than AI-driven attacks, though the author writing on HackerNoon notes that increasingly autonomous AI systems could make identifying and exploiting similar weaknesses faster and more scalable.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
The coordinated targeting of connected civilian infrastructure through operational technology manipulation signals that adversaries are actively probing supply-chain and food-security systems, with direct implications for critical infrastructure cyber defence investment across NATO nations.
