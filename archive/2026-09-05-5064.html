URGENT North America CRITICAL · 82
RAND warns US lacks workers to rebuild war-lost satellites
A RAND report published August 31 found the United States does not have enough engineers, technicians and assemblers to reconstitute military space assets destroyed in a conflict with China or Russia within the 12-to-24-month benchmark the study sets. The report, sponsored by Gen. Michael Guetlein while serving as vice chief of space operations and now serving as Golden Dome director, examined workforce gaps between 2027 and 2032 and concluded the existing shortfall could take years to close, putting rapid reconstitution out of reach.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
For defence space investors, the workforce gap is a structural constraint that lengthens every satellite programme schedule and raises the cost of any reconstitution contract, making workforce-integrated primes and training pipeline partners disproportionately valuable.

The European Space Agency signed the first contracts under its European Launcher Challenge, awarding €197.8 million to Isar Aerospace, €186.9 million to Rocket Factory Augsburg, and €158.9 million to PLD Space, with a fourth contract involving MaiaSpace still being finalised. Nancy Grace Roman telescope launches on Falcon Heavy
NASA and SpaceX successfully launched the $4.3 billion Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope on a Falcon Heavy from Launch Complex 39A at Kennedy Space Center, with the observatory now en route to the Sun-Earth Lagrange Point 2. NORMAL North America CRITICAL · 90
Army awards $464.8M E-HEL laser production contract
The US Army awarded a $464.8 million production contract to AeroVironment for the Enduring-High Energy Laser program under an Other Transaction Agreement, marking the first production contract for a directed-energy system in US military history. The 30 kW LOCUST laser weapons are designed to defeat groups one through three unmanned aircraft by firing a laser beam that reloads through the power supply rather than consuming physical munitions.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
The E-HEL award creates a production-scale directed-energy baseline at a moment when Iran-war Reaper attrition and long-range missile depletion are exposing the unsustainability of conventional counter-drone munitions expenditure, positioning AeroVironment as the first mover in a category likely to attract follow-on competitive awards.

Air Force accelerates Reaper successor after Iran losses
Air Force Secretary Troy Meink directed acceleration of a cheaper, more expendable MQ-9A successor after combat losses in Iran pushed the fleet to roughly 135 aircraft, approximately 54 below the service 189-aircraft floor, according to Defense News. Accelerating the Reaper successor timeline by two years opens a near-term competition for an expendable medium-altitude drone that values attrition tolerance over unit cost, signalling procurement opportunity for non-traditional drone makers willing to undercut legacy platform economics.

NORMAL Europe HIGH · 72
US, Russia block autonomous weapons accord in Geneva
Opposition from the United States and Russia has raised doubts that the 128 states party to the Convention on Certain Conventional Weapons meeting in Geneva will agree on a non-binding text that could pave the way to international rules on lethal autonomous weapons systems. A majority of countries expressed support for a draft text, but the US and Russia proposed late changes and deletions, with Human Rights Watch and the Stop Killer Robots campaign warning that AI-assisted weapons may behave unpredictably.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
US and Russian resistance to binding LAWS standards preserves the legal operating space for AI-enabled autonomous weapons development and procurement, reducing near-term regulatory risk for companies building counter-drone, loitering munition and autonomous targeting systems.
