NORMAL Asia Pacific HIGH · 65
Taiwan lifts space budget to NT$71B through 2031
President Lai Ching-te announced at TASTI 2026 in Tainan that the government has increased funding for the third phase of its National Space Technology Long-term Development Program to NT$71 billion, extended through 2031, up from an original NT$25.1 billion budget with an end date of 2028. Taiwan is building a rocket research and assembly base in Shalun expected to begin operations in 2030, and a national launch facility at Jiupeng in Pingtung County expected to be operational by 2032, after which the island aims to launch domestically manufactured satellites without relying on foreign providers such as SpaceX.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Taiwans commitment to sovereign launch by 2032 creates a decade-long demand signal for domestic and allied suppliers of small-launch infrastructure, ground systems and satellite buses, and reduces its vulnerability to partner-nation export decisions under geopolitical pressure.

CENTCOM launches Task Force Iron Gate on Iran routes

NORMAL North America CRITICAL · 78
Pentagon loans Neros $450M to scale drone output
The Pentagon Office of Strategic Capital provided a $450 million conditional loan to California-based drone maker Neros Technologies to expand domestic manufacturing of small drone components, with Neros committing an additional $165 million to bring total investment to $615 million. The loan follows an $820 million loan to drone maker PDW in July, as the Pentagon moves to reduce dependence on Chinese-supplied components, which currently account for the vast majority of small drone components used in US assembly.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Back-to-back nine-figure Pentagon loans to domestic small-drone manufacturers confirm that the Office of Strategic Capital is functioning as an industrial policy instrument, and investors in US drone supply-chain companies should expect further tranches as the administration accelerates onshoring ahead of potential Democratic-led budget scrutiny.

NORMAL Europe HIGH · 68
Kongsberg pitches converted trawler for UK Arctic role
Norways Kongsberg Maritime unveiled the Arctic Hunter concept in a Nov. 9 announcement, proposing a 56-metre converted fishing trawler fitted with Thales UK sonar and autonomy systems capable of unattended operations for approximately 60 days, intended to respond to a UK requirement for unmanned vessels to assist submarine-hunting in the Arctic. The concept of operations envisions a flotilla of ten ships based west of the Bear Gap, the stretch of Arctic Ocean between Svalbard and Norway through which Russian submarines must pass to reach the open Atlantic.

A commercially derived, low-cost unmanned ASW concept backed by an established prime targeting a live UK Type 92 requirement validates the direction of allied naval investment away from expensive crewed platforms and toward attritable, high-endurance sensor carriers — a structural tailwind for Kongsberg, Thales UK and their supply chains.

Iran says US-Israeli strikes hinder IAEA monitoring
Iranian Foreign Ministry Spokesperson Esmaeil Baghaei confirmed at a press conference in Tehran on November 9 that IAEA inspectors conducted inspections at Irans Bushehr Nuclear Power Plant several days ago, but said US and Israeli strikes on Iranian nuclear facilities have created difficulties in fulfilling monitoring obligations. NORMAL Switzerland HIGH · 65
Switzerland votes Nov 29 on easing arms export curbs
Swiss voters will decide on 29 November whether to block a parliamentary amendment that would allow arms shipments to 36 Western countries even when they are involved in active armed conflicts, though not for use in those conflicts. The amendment also removes a requirement for buyer countries to sign a non-re-export declaration, and polls suggest the referendum proposed by a coalition including Social Democrats and the Greens could go either way.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
A yes vote would open a significant new re-export channel for Swiss-sourced defence equipment to allied nations, while a no vote would reinforce neutrality constraints that have limited Swiss arms firms from supplying partners supporting Ukraine.
