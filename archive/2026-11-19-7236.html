URGENT North America CRITICAL · 85
Vulcan failure haunts US launch and Amazon LEO
United Launch Alliance's Vulcan rocket suffered a mission-ending malfunction 10 minutes after launch on November 15, destroying a payload of Amazon LEO broadband satellites and marking the first total mission failure in ULA history, a joint Boeing and Lockheed Martin venture. The failure threatens NASA's lunar programme timeline and carries national security consequences because the US Space Force is, alongside Amazon, one of Vulcans primary customers.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
With ULA grounded again and SpaceX dominating the launch market, US government and commercial customers face a narrowing of viable heavy-lift options at precisely the moment Gulf conflict operations are driving demand for resilient space-based communications and ISR.

NORMAL South Asia HIGH · 72
Indias Vikram-1 marks first private orbital launch
Skyroot Aerospaces Vikram-1 rocket lifted off from the Satish Dhawan Space Centre in Andhra Pradesh on July 18, 2026, placing a half-dozen small satellites into low-Earth orbit 280 miles up, becoming the first privately built and owned Indian rocket to successfully orbit a payload. Prime Minister Narendra Modi called the launch a defining moment in Indias space journey, and Union Minister Jitendra Singh witnessed the event at the launch site.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Indias private launch market has cleared its most significant credibility threshold, opening a new competitive corridor for small satellite operators seeking non-Western launch options and signalling increased procurement opportunity for Indian domestic space infrastructure.

URGENT Middle East CRITICAL · 92
US Navy fired recoded expired munitions in Iran war
Acting Navy Secretary Hung Cao disclosed during a Senate Armed Services Committee nomination hearing that the US Navy recoded and fired missiles classified as expired or unserviceable during Operation Epic Fury and the broader Iran war, referring to materiel categorised as code echo and code foxtrot under Defense Logistics Agency classification. The disclosure follows reporting that the US military burned through at least 850 Tomahawk long-range cruise missiles after the first month of Operation Epic Fury alone, raising concerns about stockpile depth.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Confirmation that the Navy is drawing on degraded-classification munitions in an active conflict, combined with the GAO finding that the Pentagon lacks an overarching munitions production plan, makes Tomahawk and precision-munitions replenishment the highest-urgency procurement signal for defence investors in the current cycle.

URGENT North America CRITICAL · 90
GAO warns Pentagon has no munitions production plan
A Government Accountability Office report made public this week warned that the Department of Defense does not have an overarching plan linking munitions production and funding to actual requirements, with Pentagon officials acknowledging they can be reactive to global events rather than driven by total requirements. The public version of a classified study completed in September 2026 also highlighted that a new Texas plant for 155-millimeter howitzer shell components cost nearly $500 million but failed to produce any shell components.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
The absence of a requirements-driven munitions plan, exposed publicly during an active conflict that has already consumed at least 850 Tomahawk missiles, creates a structural demand pull for artillery, missile and munitions producers that is likely to accelerate congressional pressure for emergency supplemental funding.

URGENT Middle East CRITICAL · 88
USS George Washington departs, two carriers remain in Gulf
The USS George Washington, a Nimitz-class carrier, has departed the Middle East after a three-month deployment and returned to the 7th Fleet for routine operations in the Asia-Pacific, reducing US carrier strike groups in the region from three to two, confirmed by US Pacific Fleet spokesperson Cmdr. Katie Koenig. The USS Theodore Roosevelt arrived in the CENTCOM area of responsibility on Sunday, and the US Navys Arabian Sea presence now comprises two carriers, 13 destroyers, two cruisers and a littoral combat ship.

URGENT Europe CRITICAL · 87
Ukraine to double drones and triple deep strikes in 2027
Ukrainian Defense Minister Yevhenii Khmara announced at the 37th Ukraine Defense Contact Group meeting at NATO headquarters in Brussels that Ukraine plans to double drone supplies and more than triple its long-range strike capability next year, with drones projected to make up more than half of weapons needs in 2027 and 62 percent of funding directed to drone programmes. The British government announced it will send Ukraine more than 1,000 long-range strike drones capable of flying more than 620 miles, in a package valued at approximately $342 million, with Prime Minister Andy Burnham set to announce the offer in Berlin at the Coalition of the Willing meeting.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Ukraines commitment to making drones the dominant weapons category in 2027, backed by the British $342 million package, represents the most concrete near-term demand signal for long-range strike drone manufacturers and their supply chains in the European defence market.

NORMAL Europe CRITICAL · 78
France and Sweden hold first joint nuclear deterrence drill
France and Sweden conducted their first joint exercise under Macrons forward deterrence initiative, with French nuclear-capable Rafale jets and an Airbus A330 MRTT tanker operating over Sweden alongside Swedish Gripen fighters in an exercise called Excalibur, held in northern Sweden from November 16 to 18. The exercise follows a previous drill in which British Eurofighter Typhoon jets escorted French Rafales on a simulated nuclear-strike mission, as France works with European allies to implement Macrons proposal to strengthen European deterrence amid Russian nuclear signalling and uncertainty about US security guarantees.

The pace of Europes bilateral nuclear deterrence exercises under the French forward deterrence framework is accelerating, expanding the market for interoperability upgrades, tanker aircraft and advanced avionics across non-nuclear NATO members that are integrating into Frances airborne deterrent posture.

MBDA and Atmos study orbital deep-strike weapons
MBDA and German startup Atmos Space Cargo announced a cooperation agreement on Wednesday to jointly assess the feasibility of a precision orbital deep-strike capability, combining MBDA military payloads with Atmos reentry vehicles and orbital transport systems. The initial phase will focus on system architecture, technical feasibility, payload integration and possible technology demonstrations, with Atmos CEO Sebastian Klaus framing the objective as conventional deterrence capable of striking any legitimate military target anywhere on Earth within 60 minutes.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
A credible European programme to weaponise orbital reentry vehicles would create a new class of hypersonic-adjacent strike asset and signals that the gap between commercial reentry technology and military deep-strike applications is narrowing faster than most Western acquisition timelines anticipate.
