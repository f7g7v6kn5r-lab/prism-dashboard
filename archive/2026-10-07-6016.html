SpaceX SDA launch scrubbed, next attempt Thursday
SpaceX aborted its Tranche 1 Transport Layer A mission for the Space Development Agencys Proliferated Warfighter Space Architecture in the final minute of the countdown, then waived off a Tuesday attempt as well, with the next window opening Thursday. ◆ PRISM ANALYSIS

NORMAL North America HIGH · 70
Starship reaches orbit, deploys 26 Starlink V3 sats
SpaceX flew Super Heavy-Starship to orbit for the first time, with the Starship upper stage deploying 26 third-generation Starlink satellites before splashing down in the Pacific Ocean north of Hawaii after a three-hour flight; one of six Raptor engines shut down early but the vehicle completed orbital insertion using a single engine firing. The milestone is described as a critical step toward qualifying the Starship lunar lander variant for NASAs Artemis moon landing programme, which requires multiple Super Heavy-Starship tanker flights to fuel the lander before it can head for the moon.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
First orbital Starship flight materially de-risks the Artemis lander schedule and validates the platform for commercial heavy-lift customers, compressing the timeline for potential displacement of legacy launch vehicles in the national security segment.

China-Laos base shows attack jets on new runway
Satellite images taken by commercial provider Vantor show what security analysts believe are eight K-8 light attack jets and more than two dozen support vehicles at Chinas new joint training facility at Ban Keun outside Vientiane, the day after the Chinese Ministry of National Defense publicly announced the facility. NORMAL Global HIGH · 52
Megaconstellation supply chain faces critical shortages
A supply chain analysis involving the Aerospace Industries Association and PricewaterhouseCoopers found that the rush to build megaconstellations is colliding with shortages of specialised components and competition from the AI infrastructure industry, with nine critical components identified as contributing to bottlenecks. Companies are considering downgrading to non-space-qualified components, accepting launch delays or facing consolidation as the squeeze forces fundamental rethinking of what timelines are achievable.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Supply chain constraint at the component level will compress margins and extend schedules for every constellation operator simultaneously, favouring vertically integrated primes over pure-play smallsat assemblers and creating pricing power for specialist component suppliers.

The Pentagons Operational Energy Capability Improvement Fund is supporting two space-based solar power demonstration approaches: Overview Energys near-infrared light transmission system, which received an OECIF contract to develop a homing beacon for satellite-to-ground energy delivery, and Katalyst Spaces microwave-based system developed with the US Naval Research Laboratory under the ASGARD programme. URGENT Middle East CRITICAL · 82
Oil shuttle ops restore Hormuz flows as costs soar
Middle East crude shipments have recovered substantially through ship-to-ship shuttle operations designed to evade Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps attacks in the Strait of Hormuz, eight months into the US-Iran war, with JPMorgan estimating exports at 98 percent of prewar volumes while maritime intelligence firm Kpler calculated a recovery to 77 percent for the period from September 21 to 27. Saudi and Yemeni government forces have launched a campaign to retake the Red Sea area including the Bab el-Mandeb strait from Houthi rebels, but shuttle operation costs of $30 million to $40 million per operation mean oil prices are unlikely to return to prewar levels even if volumes fully recover.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Structural cost premiums embedded in the shuttle workaround mean that energy price relief requires physical security of the Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb straits, not just volume recovery, making the outcome of the Saudi-Yemeni Red Sea campaign a direct input to Gulf sovereign defence budget capacity.

NORMAL North America CRITICAL · 78
Pentagon names directed-energy systems for base pilot

NORMAL United States CRITICAL · 75
Pentagon drops Anthropic eight months after blacklist
The US Department of Defense has stopped using Anthropic products, including Claude AI models, eight months after designating the company a supply-chain security risk in February, with the actual cutoff arriving two months behind the deadline set by Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth. Claude had been embedded in Maven Smart System, the Palantir-operated intelligence and data platform used by the Pentagon, making removal technically difficult and explaining the delay, according to Lauren Kahn, a senior research analyst at Georgetowns Center for Security and Emerging Technology.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
The forced extraction of a deeply integrated commercial AI model from the Pentagons core intelligence platform exposes how quickly vendor dependency can become a national security liability, signalling stricter AI supplier vetting across allied defence organisations.
