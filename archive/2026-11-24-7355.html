URGENT North America CRITICAL · 80
Starship caught by tower arms after 22-hour flight
SpaceXs Starship upper stage returned from a 22.5-hour, 14-orbit mission and was caught for the first time by the mechanical arms at Starbase, Texas, with SpaceX mission commentator Dan Huot describing the ship catch as the missing link and holy grail of spaceflight. The Super Heavy booster failed to make a similar return to the pad on this flight, meaning full two-stage reusability was not demonstrated. During the mission Starship deployed 26 Starlink satellites in orbit while cruising at approximately 161 miles altitude.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Demonstrating orbital-duration endurance combined with a tower catch removes a key technical barrier to full reusability and accelerates the timeline toward operational Starship missions for both commercial and government customers.

BREAKING Africa CRITICAL · 93
Ukraine confirms drones sank Russian corvette Merkury
President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Ukraine was behind the sinking of the Russian corvette Merkury in the Libyan port of Benghazi, with the Security Service of Ukraine saying it carried out the operation jointly with Ukraines Defense Forces using seven Ukrainian-made naval drones that struck the moored ship on November 22. Zelenskyy said a Ka-27 helicopter aboard the corvette was also destroyed, and the dry-cargo ship MV Angara was targeted in the same operation, which the SBU describes as a sanctioned vessel used to move weapons and military equipment on Russian routes to Syria and Libya. Reuters cited local medical sources reporting ten injuries including Russians and Libyans, and Al Jazeera reported one person killed.

URGENT Middle East CRITICAL · 85
Allianz sees $790B GCC output loss by 2030 from Iran war
Allianz Research projects GCC economies will lose $790 billion in output by 2030 compared to pre-war trends, representing a cumulative 5.9 percent of GDP, with Qatar forecast to contract by 14 percent, Kuwait by 11 percent and Bahrain by 8 percent. A separate $200 billion figure covers physical damage to energy infrastructure, with over 80 energy and oil production sites including refineries and pipelines hit. GCC states are demanding Iran pay for losses caused by over 7,000 missiles and drones Tehran fired during its war with the United States and Israel.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
The scale of projected economic damage to GCC states crystallises the strategic case for Gulf sovereigns to accelerate investment in air and missile defence, energy infrastructure hardening and maritime security capabilities regardless of when a ceasefire is reached.

Iran war environmental toll could last decades, expert warns
Iranian environmentalist Mohammad Darvish told the Tehran Times that fires at oil facilities, damage to protected habitats and lost vessels carrying hazardous materials could carry consequences far beyond Irans borders, with contaminants entering the Persian Gulf threatening marine life and food chains in neighboring countries. Trump and Xi agree humans must control military AI use
Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said President Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping agreed at their Shenzhen meeting that humans should always be involved in military decisions using artificial intelligence, and that AI should not be allowed to act autonomously on major military actions. NORMAL Germany HIGH · 68
German loitering munitions pass live-fire trials
Two German one-way attack drone variants, Starks Virtus and Helsings HX-2, passed live-fire trials during the week of November 9, clearing a step toward initial deliveries and troop training before the end of the year, according to Germanys military procurement office. The trials, held at the Bundeswehr weapons and ammunition test center in Meppen, tested whether the systems could reliably hit designated targets and whether their warheads worked as intended on impact. Though the systems have not yet completed full qualification, the German Army plans to begin equipping its armoured brigade in Lithuania in 2027.

◈ WATCH: Super Heavy booster return to the pad
There is no scheduled space catalyst here, so the standing indicator is whether a Super Heavy booster achieves a pad return after the booster failed to do so on the flight where the Starship upper stage was caught by the tower arms at Starbase, Texas.
▲ BULL CASE  /  ▼ BEAR CASE ▾
▲ BULL CASE
The read is that a booster return would complete the two-stage reusability that this 22.5-hour, 14-orbit mission left unproven, and would pressure the cost assumptions of every operator that has to buy or build lift, with RKLB up 9.3 percent at the last close showing how fast launch-linked names move on sentiment.
▼ BEAR CASE
The implication of repeated booster losses is that the catch of the upper stage, described by SpaceX mission commentator Dan Huot as the missing link, stays a partial win, and deployment economics for payloads such as the 26 Starlink satellites carried on this flight remain unverified.
