SpaceX catches Starship upper stage after 22-hour orbit
SpaceXs Starship upper stage, designated Ship 42, completed 14 orbits over 22.5 hours and was caught by the Mechazilla arms at Starbase, Texas. The Super Heavy booster failed to return to the pad, meaning full vehicle reuse was not achieved on this flight. SpaceX has spent more than $15 billion on Starship development, with a contract valued at more than $4 billion awarded by the space agency in 2021 to return humans to the moon.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Upper-stage catch capability closes a major technical gap in full Starship reusability, sharply compressing the marginal cost of Starlink deployment and widening the competitive moat against every other heavy-lift provider, including the now-failed Vulcan.

Iran fires six drones at Bahrain in second Gulf strike in 72 hours
Irans IRGC claimed it launched six drones at the US air defence radar at the Al-Riffa helicopter base in Bahrain on 15 November, the second Iranian strike on a Gulf country within 72 hours and the first such attacks since early September. A resumption of IRGC strikes against Gulf host-nation infrastructure after a two-month pause, combined with disputed intercept outcomes, signals that point-defence coverage of US forward radar and command assets remains a critical and unresolved vulnerability for investors in layered air defence systems.

Army whistleblower says Kuwait post lacked air defences before Iran strike
Army Major Stephen Ramsbottom filed a whistleblower complaint to the House and Senate Armed Services Committees alleging that senior leaders dismissed warnings about thin fortifications at Port Shuaiba, the Kuwait command post where six US soldiers were killed by an Iranian drone on 1 March. URGENT Iran CRITICAL · 75
US expands Iran supply disruption beyond naval blockade
US Central Command established Task Force Iron Gate on 7 November, a team of approximately 20 civilian and military personnel based in Tampa, Florida, tasked with coordinating across US government agencies to cut off Irans land border and Caspian Sea supply routes for advanced weapons, dual-use technologies and sanctioned materials. CENTCOM estimates its naval blockade has already prevented Iran from exporting more than $25 billion worth of crude oil, while a Reuters investigation found Iran imported at least $2 billion in military-related hardware between January 2025 and June 2026 through Caspian, Central Asian and Chinese air freight routes. Analysts say the task forces practical impact could be limited without cooperation from regional governments, as the Caspian is enclosed by five countries and the United States has no direct territorial access.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Extending interdiction to land and Caspian corridors signals a systemic tightening of Irans access to weapons components and revenue, with direct consequences for the durability of its drone and missile production pipeline.

NORMAL United Kingdom HIGH · 60
UK investors ready to rearm but demand contract clarity
A House of Lords committee report published this week found that private capital interest in UK defence has never been higher, but that the governments inability to get meaningful contracts out of the door is holding back investment, according to a venture capital investor who testified to peers. TheCityUK said delays to the Defence Investment Plan had damaged confidence ahead of a Defence Investment Summit the government is hosting on 1 December. The government published an investment plan in June setting out 298 billion pounds of spending over four years, and the British Business Bank and National Wealth Fund have been told to put up to 5 billion pounds into defence and dual-use technology.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Until the UK government translates spending ambitions into signed contracts with predictable timelines, private capital will remain on the sideline and the industrial base expansion required to meet the 2030 target will stall.

NORMAL Belgium HIGH · 55
Belgium orders NASAMS in 1 billion dollar air defence deal
Belgium signed contracts for 10 Kongsberg NASAMS short- to medium-range air defence systems, 20 Rheinmetall Skyranger 30 short-range systems and an undisclosed number of Thales GM200 radars to form a layered Blue Dome air defence architecture. The NASAMS order, signed in September, is worth 1 billion US dollars, making Belgium the 18th country to select the system. The three systems will provide complementary capabilities, with GM200 radars detecting and tracking threats, NASAMS providing short- and medium-range missile defence, and Skyranger 30 providing very short-range protection particularly against uncrewed aerial vehicles.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Belgiums billion-dollar layered air defence commitment reflects the accelerating European demand signal for integrated short- and medium-range protection that benefits Kongsberg, Rheinmetall and Thales across their respective system lines.
