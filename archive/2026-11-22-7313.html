NORMAL North America HIGH · 72
SpaceX launches classified USSF-317 for Space Force
A SpaceX Falcon 9 lifted off from Space Launch Complex 4 East at Vandenberg Space Force Base at 8:20 p.m. Pacific Standard Time on November 20, 2026, carrying the classified USSF-317 national security payload for the US Space Force after a weather scrub the previous evening. The mission was the sixth in a series of Space Force launches assigned to SpaceX beginning in August, following USSF-627 on November 12, with 23 satellites catalogued after each of the five preceding flights. The booster, designated B1100, flew for the 11th time and landed aboard the droneship Of Course I Still Love You in the Pacific Ocean.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
The sustained cadence of classified Space Force launches on commercial Falcon 9 vehicles underscores the Pentagons structural dependence on a single commercial provider for rapid national security satellite deployment, a concentration risk that the OneWeb and alternative LEO debates have so far failed to resolve at operational scale.

NORMAL Global HIGH · 68
OneWeb wins Pentagon contract as Starlink alternatives sought
The Pentagon had OneWeb on contract by June under the same program that buys Starshield, and France holds a priority-access contract with OneWeb worth up to one billion euros, following an October episode in which Starshield terminals in Ukraine began dropping links when moving faster than about 100 mph, prompting allied governments to seek a second low Earth orbit network. On November 17, Eutelsat Network Solutions announced a Warrior Plans award it estimates at more than $60 million over a two-year period covering OneWeb airtime, deployable terminals and alternative positioning, navigation and timing. Amazon Leo had launched 396 satellites before a United Launch Alliance Vulcan failure on November 15 destroyed 40 more, leaving the constellation well short of the more than 3,300 it needs.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Governments paying for a Starshield alternative are funding a capability that cannot be delivered at operational scale before 2030 without a step-change in non-SpaceX launch supply, making launch-vehicle diversification the gating constraint for allied resilience rather than satellite manufacturing.

URGENT Middle East CRITICAL · 90
Iran FM signals possible nuclear doctrine review
Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi told state-run IRNA news agency that Irans stockpile of enriched uranium would be used according to its needs however those needs are defined, increasing speculation that Tehran may be moving away from its policy of limiting its nuclear programme to civilian purposes. Araghchi acknowledged an ongoing debate among lawmakers and officials about altering Irans nuclear doctrine, saying those in favour of reform argue the current approach has failed to protect the countrys nuclear facilities, but added that for now the doctrine has not changed and the official position remains the same. The International Atomic Energy Agency estimated in 2025 that Iran had about 440 kilograms of uranium enriched to about 60 percent, far beyond the roughly 3 percent needed for power generation.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
A formal Iranian shift in nuclear doctrine would transform the conflict from a conventional-plus-proxy campaign into a proliferation crisis, eliminating the diplomatic pathway Washington has conditioned on nuclear guarantees and locking in an indefinite demand surge for missile defence systems.

URGENT North America CRITICAL · 88
CSIS: US expended up to two-thirds of key munitions
A Center for Strategic and International Studies analysis current as of November 3 found that in the 39-day campaign against Iran until the April 8 ceasefire and subsequent operations, the United States expended between a third and two-thirds of key munition inventories. Seven of nine assessed missile categories were classed as having detrimental effects on resupply capacity, with Patriot, Terminal High Altitude Area Defense and Standard Missiles identified as heavily used with no substitutes, and long-range ground-attack missiles including Tomahawks, AGM-158 JASSM cruise missiles and Precision Strike Missiles described as heavily used with some substitutes. The report warned that rebuilding inventories will require a multiyear commitment and that the problem is not money but time.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
With the IRGC now actively targeting Patriot radars in Bahrain while CSIS confirms critical depletion of precisely those interceptors, the gap between operational demand and production ramp timelines is the defining constraint on how long the current US posture in the Gulf can be sustained without either a ceasefire or a capability cliff.

URGENT North America CRITICAL · 82
GAO warns Pentagon lacks munitions production plan
A Government Accountability Office report, the public version of a classified study completed in September 2026, found that the Department of Defense does not have an overarching plan linking munitions production and funding to requirements, with Pentagon officials acknowledging they can be reactive to global events rather than driven by total requirements. The report noted that the Army ramp-up in production of 155-millimeter artillery shells was not driven by a need to address the total munitions requirement, and pointed to a new Texas plant that cost nearly $500 million but failed to produce any shell components. GAO recommended that DoD create an overarching plan to match munitions requirements, production and funding, as well as metrics to track progress.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
The absence of a requirements-driven production plan means that even significant congressional appropriations for munitions restocking risk misallocation, creating execution risk for investors in defence manufacturers whose order pipelines depend on coherent DoD demand signals.

NORMAL Europe CRITICAL · 78
Denmark ratifies Greenland security deal with US
Denmarks parliament unanimously ratified a security agreement with the United States on Greenland, following the September signing by the US, Denmark and Greenland after President Trump repeatedly threatened to take control of the mineral-rich self-governing Danish territory of 57,000 people. The agreement expands the US militarys role in Greenland, including permission to establish a Golden Dome missile defence system, while reaffirming Danish sovereignty and Greenlanders right to self-determination. The parties will now begin work on two new US military bases and a law screening outside investors on security grounds, Denmarks Defence Minister Jeppe Bruus told Reuters.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Unanimous ratification converts an ambiguous political commitment into a defence infrastructure programme with concrete base-planning milestones, opening a defined acquisition pipeline for Arctic-capable missile defence, surveillance and basing systems over the near term.

France, Sweden hold first joint nuclear deterrence exercise
France and Sweden conducted Exercise Excalibur in northern Sweden on November 16 to 18, their first joint exercise under French President Emmanuel Macrons forward deterrence initiative, with French nuclear-capable Rafale jets and an Airbus A330 MRTT tanker operating alongside Swedish Gripen fighters. BREAKING Bahrain / Strait of Hormuz CRITICAL · 95
Iran escalates Gulf attacks amid blockade tensions

Trump and Xi agree humans must oversee military AI

NORMAL Germany HIGH · 60
German attack drone variants pass live-fire trials
Two German one-way attack drone variants, Starks Virtus and Helsings HX-2, passed live-fire trials held the week of November 9 at the Bundeswehrs weapons and ammunition test centre in Meppen, clearing a step toward initial deliveries and troop training before the end of the year. The procurement office announced both systems passed but did not disclose how many shots were fired or the weapons success rates, and noted that before loitering munitions can be used within the German Armed Forces they must be thoroughly tested on the interaction of the various system components. The German Army plans to begin equipping its 45th Armored Brigade in Lithuania with the systems starting in 2027, though qualification has not yet been completed.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Germanys first domestic loitering munition live-fire validation, targeting NATO front-line deployment in Lithuania, marks a concrete step in Europes push to field autonomous lethal systems outside US supply chains, with implications for allied procurement strategies across the continent.

NORMAL Netherlands HIGH · 65
Netherlands targets 40 percent defence industry growth by 2030
The Netherlands presented a defence-industrial plan targeting 40 percent growth in its defence technological and industrial base by 2030, with local defence industry revenue reaching around 15 billion euros, and the government has earmarked 1.6 billion euros for the plan. The plan focuses on integrated air and missile defence, data-driven command and digital resilience, unmanned and autonomous systems and military sustainment, with spending including 600 million euros for industrial ecosystems and scaling production, 320 million euros for international cooperation and 150 million euros on supply chain resilience. The Netherlands is counting on the domestic share of Dutch defence-industry revenue to grow 50 percent by 2030 to more than 10 billion euros, while exports are targeted to grow by about 25 percent.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
A government-backed €1.6 billion commitment to grow a mid-sized European defence base signals durable procurement pipeline for air and missile defence and autonomous systems suppliers operating in the NATO market.
