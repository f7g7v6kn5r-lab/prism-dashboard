URGENT North America CRITICAL · 90
Vulcan fails to deliver 40 Amazon satellites
United Launch Alliances Vulcan rocket suffered an anomaly about 10 minutes into flight after lifting off at 12:40 p.m. EST from Space Launch Complex 41 at Cape Canaveral on November 15, failing to deliver 40 Amazon Leo broadband satellites to orbit in the companys first total mission failure. Northrop Grumman said the solid rocket boosters performed as expected and separated before the anomaly, leaving the cause unconfirmed, while ULA said it did not yet have specific data to understand what occurred. Amazon must wait for ULAs investigation before flying Vulcan again, and its Federal Communications Commission license requires the full first-generation constellation in orbit by July 30, 2029.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
ULA now carries both reputational and financial risk into a backlog of military launch contracts at a moment when it has no demonstrated recovery plan, while Amazon LEOs rural broadband rollout timeline slips further, extending the competitive window for rival constellations.

URGENT North America CRITICAL · 82
Starship Flight 15 catches Ship 42 from orbit
SpaceX caught Starship upper stage Ship 42 at Pad 2 at Starbase, Texas at 3:20 p.m. CST on November 14 after the vehicle completed about 14 orbits during a flight of roughly 22.5 hours, marking the first time an upper stage had been caught by the tower after an orbital flight. Super Heavy Booster 22 called off its own tower catch and was destroyed above the Gulf of Mexico after ending its boostback burn early following a low fuel mass alarm; SpaceX said the root cause was still under investigation. The next mission, Flight 16, is expected to pair Ship 43 with Booster 23 and to be the first Starship launch from Launch Complex 39A at Kennedy Space Center in Florida.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Recovering the upper stage — the more expensive and complex piece — validates the core reuse economics that underpin both SpaceXs commercial launch pricing and the Starship lunar lander NASA plans to use for Artemis III, making this milestone directly relevant to government launch dependency assessments.

URGENT Europe CRITICAL · 78
Iceye bids 2 billion euros for rocket maker Avio
Finnish satellite operator Iceye made a 2 billion euro cash offer for Italian rocket maker Avio, proposing to invest 1 billion euros in Avio in Italy over five years and to expand Avios Vega rocket customer base while taking charge of Avios expansion into the US missile business. The Finnish government backed the offer, with Finnish Defence Minister Antti Hakkanen framing the deal as a step toward sovereign European defence supply security; Avios board began assessing the bid, which Iceye said represents a 21.3 percent premium to Avios closing price on Monday and a 34.8 percent premium to its three-month volume-weighted average price. Iceye CEO Rafal Modrzewski said the company held consultations with governments and other parties before launching the bid and that it would take a few more months to close the transaction.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
If completed, the deal would create a vertically integrated European space and defence company combining satellite intelligence with launch capability, directly challenging the dependence on US and Chinese providers that European governments have flagged as a strategic vulnerability.

NORMAL North America HIGH · 55
SpaceNukes opens Los Alamos manufacturing facility
Space Nuclear Power Corp opened its Manufacture and Materials Development Facility at 150 Central Park Square in Los Alamos, New Mexico on November 13, dedicated to producing its Generation 2 reactor using additive manufacturing of nickel-based and refractory alloys including tungsten, molybdenum, tantalum, rhenium and niobium. CEO Andrew Phelps said the next-generation reactor is designed to run much hotter than current designs, with the principal technical barrier identified as materials rather than nuclear physics. The facility is led by Omar Mireles, director of manufacture and materials, who previously spent 13 years as a research engineer and principal investigator at NASAs Marshall Space Flight Center.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Establishing proprietary manufacturing capability for refractory alloys that cannot be sourced commercially signals that SpaceNukes is building supply chain independence ahead of anticipated demand for high-power space fission systems from defence and civil customers.

US Navy reaches three carrier strike groups in region
The USS Theodore Roosevelt arrived in the Middle East on Sunday, bringing the US Navy to three carrier strike groups in the region simultaneously for the second time in 2026, a concentration not seen before this year since the 2003 invasion of Iraq, according to US Central Command. NORMAL North America HIGH · 62
Pentagon fails ninth straight audit; majority of assets pass
The Pentagon failed its annual financial audit for the ninth consecutive year, according to an inspector general report released Monday, but Defence Department CFO Jules Hurst said 14 of 25 standalone reporting entities passed in fiscal year 2026, up from 11 the prior year, covering 56 percent of the departments assets and 86 percent of its liabilities. The department is seeking a record 1.5 trillion dollars in national defence spending from Congress and faces a congressional deadline to pass a full audit by the fiscal year beginning in October 2027. The Pentagon is using artificial intelligence tools from Palantir and xAI, alongside in-house applications, to automate financial reconciliations and documentation as it works to consolidate 25 separate audit opinions into eight ahead of seeking a full department-wide opinion in fiscal 2028.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Passing a majority of assets for the first time is a credibility signal that matters as the Pentagon pursues its record budget request, but failing the overall audit for a ninth year sustains congressional scepticism about financial oversight at exactly the moment the department needs legislative trust to unlock that spending.

NORMAL Philippines / Australia HIGH · 65
Australia and Philippines sign defence cooperation pact
Australia and the Philippines signed a Defence Cooperation Agreement on November 15 covering intelligence-sharing, military training, the transfer of aerial and surface drones, maritime activities and Australian investment in Philippine military infrastructure, replacing a framework largely governed by a 1995 memorandum of agreement. Philippine Defence Secretary Gilberto Teodoro said bureaucratic steps for a single activity would fall from roughly 100 to 10, while Australian Strategic Policy Institute senior fellow Euan Graham said the Philippines is Australias first line of defence. The new pact is expected to facilitate discussions on potential Australian investments in Philippine military infrastructure, with a list of five Luzon bases slated to receive funding for eight infrastructure projects expected to be finalised, though only one base has been discussed in detail so far.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
The agreement opens a formal pathway for Australian defence capital to flow into Philippine basing and logistics infrastructure, expanding the Indo-Pacific footprint available to allied operators and creating procurement opportunities for drone and maritime system suppliers.
