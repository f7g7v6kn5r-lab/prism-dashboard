Ukraine, Denmark test Obria Nano satellite in orbit

NORMAL North America HIGH · 50
Florida logs 60 launches in 2026 as Starlink cadence slows
As of October 26, Florida recorded only 60 orbital rocket launches in 2026, well below the record 109 that lifted off from the Space Coast in 2025, driven primarily by SpaceX ending frequent Florida Falcon 9 Starlink missions. A New Glenn explosion in May knocked Blue Origin off the Eastern Range manifest for the foreseeable future, and there are not likely to be any launches from Florida in November as of October 26.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
A near-halving of Florida launch cadence from its 2025 peak signals that range infrastructure and support industries built around high-frequency Starlink missions face utilisation headwinds, shifting competitive positioning toward launch providers with diversified manifest depth.

US forces ready to strike Iran within 96 hours of Trump order
Two US officials told CNN that US forces in the Middle East are prepared to launch new, potentially major, military operations against Iran within 96 hours of receiving a presidential order, with sufficient forces already deployed in the region. Leaders in US Central Command and the Pentagon have developed a wide-ranging target list including Iranian energy infrastructure, military sites and nuclear facilities, and officials said it is a question of when, not if, Trump will issue a go-order, most likely after the US midterm election.

A credible and near-term military posture against Iran means operators and investors must treat renewed major conflict in the Gulf as a base-case planning scenario, with immediate consequences for energy prices, Gulf aviation, and defence supply chains dependent on Strait of Hormuz stability.

The US Air Force awarded Lockheed Martin a $694 million production contract for the AGM-183A Air-Launched Rapid Response Weapon, moving ARRW from prototyping and flight testing into production under the Defense Departments Enterprise-Wide Agile Acquisition Contract. NORMAL United States HIGH · 70
Pentagon advances Cyber Effects-as-a-Service acquisition model
The Pentagon has been advancing a new acquisition paradigm called Cyber Effects-as-a-Service, under which the department contracts for specific cyber outcomes rather than bespoke tools, with Assistant Secretary of Defense for Cyber Policy Katie Sutton describing the goal as shifting from a contractual relationship to an operational partnership with industry. Sutton said in September that demand for cyber capabilities across combatant commanders far exceeds the current supply, and the department hosted a tabletop exercise in late September with members of industry across the AI and defense technology sectors along with US Cyber Command.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
A shift to outcome-based cyber contracting could fundamentally reshape how defence technology firms price and deliver offensive and defensive cyber capabilities, creating new recurring revenue models and favouring vendors with demonstrated operational track records over traditional platform integrators.

India ammunition exports to Europe top $872M since Ukraine invasion

Britain declines to join Canada-led defence bank
Britain will not join the Canada-led Defence, Security and Resilience Bank, after the two countries failed to agree on headquarters location, joint procurement scope, lending rules and membership terms, according to a person familiar with the matter.
