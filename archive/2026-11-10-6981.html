URGENT North America CRITICAL · 85
NASA awards $10B CLPS-2 contract to eight firms
NASA named eight companies — Blue Origin, Firefly Aerospace, Intuitive Machines, ispace-US, Lockheed Martin Space, Northrop Grumman Space Systems, SpaceX and Voyager Technologies — to its Commercial Lunar Payload Services CLPS-2 contract vehicle, which carries a ceiling of $10 billion over ten years, to compete for future lunar delivery, lander build and operation contracts. NASA also awarded six task orders under its Venture-Class Acquisition of Dedicated and Rideshare launch services contract to Arrow Science and Technology, Blue Origin and Impulse Space, with a combined value of approximately $1.3 billion, covering orbital transfer vehicles and cargo transport to the Moon Base; Arrow Science and Technology confirmed its individual award is worth $185 million and will use a SpaceX Falcon 9 and a Quantum Space orbital transfer vehicle.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
The CLPS-2 ceiling and the $1.3 billion in VADR task orders together represent the largest single tranche of Moon Base infrastructure contracting to date, locking in a multi-year revenue pipeline for primes and new-space players across launch, in-space transport and lander segments.

URGENT Europe CRITICAL · 82
France orders two IRIS spy satellites for over EUR1.5B
Frances Directorate General of Armament awarded a contract worth more than EUR1.5 billion to Airbus and Thales Alenia Space for two military Earth-observation satellites in the IRIS programme, intended to replace three existing CSO orbiters and maintain a sovereign space-based image-intelligence capability beyond 2035. Airbus will provide satellite platforms, a significant share of payload on the first satellite and the ground segment, while Thales Alenia Space will develop the payload on the second satellite; the first IRIS satellite is planned to become operational in 2033 with both in orbit around 2035.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Frances IRIS award confirms that European NATO members with sovereign ISR ambitions are committing large long-term capital to replace ageing constellations, creating durable pipeline for prime integrators and sensor suppliers insulated from US policy shifts on intelligence sharing.

NORMAL Global HIGH · 70
ESCAPADE smallsats head to Mars after powered Earth flybys
NASAs two ESCAPADE smallsats, Blue and Gold, completed consecutive powered Earth flybys, with Blue passing within approximately 1,600 kilometres and Gold within approximately 1,050 kilometres on November 9, using the Oberth effect to maximise propulsive efficiency — described as the first time NASA has used a powered Earth flyby to send spacecraft toward Mars. The Rocket Lab-built spacecraft, which launched last November aboard Blue Origins New Glenn, spent nearly a year at the Earth-sun L-2 Lagrange point before executing the dual manoeuvre, and are now on trajectories to arrive at Mars two days apart in September 2027.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
The first successful NASA powered Earth flyby for a Mars-bound mission validates both Rocket Labs smallsat bus capabilities and Blue Origins New Glenn as a credible science-mission launch platform, strengthening both companies positioning for future NASA awards.

Taiwan targets 2032 orbital launch site at Jioupeng
President William Lai told the Taiwan International Assembly of Space Science, Technology and Industry in Tainan that the government has selected Pingtung Countys Jioupeng as the site of a national rocket launch facility, expected to become operational in 2032, and approved a rocket research, development, assembly and testing base in Tainans Shalun district, due to begin operations in 2030. US KC-135 Stratotankers evacuated from RAF Mildenhall
At least ten Boeing KC-135R Stratotanker refuelling aircraft departed RAF Mildenhall in eastern England on Monday, with one flying to Germany, one to Scotland and two to Spain, while the destinations of the remaining six were not publicly confirmed; at least three Osprey multipurpose aircraft also departed Mildenhall and landed at RAF Lakenheath four miles away. Britain said its bases were safe and the US European Command declined to comment on force protection measures, while the Wall Street Journal attributed the movement to an Iranian threat — the second such apparent evacuation in three months.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
The second evacuation of US strategic air assets from a UK base in three months signals that Iranian threat vectors against European-based force enablers remain active even as Trump publicly holds off on fresh strikes, exposing a gap between the diplomatic pause and the operational posture that operators and basing planners must account for.

URGENT North America CRITICAL · 82
GAO finds Pentagon cannot track $31B F-35 spare parts pool
The Government Accountability Office reported that as of March 2026 the F-35 programmes joint spare-parts pool comprised approximately 26 million parts valued at around $31 billion, of which approximately 17,000 parts worth up to $2.5 billion were not recorded in the Pentagons Enterprise Logistics Management System. The GAO described DoD records as outdated, inaccurate and incomplete, noting minimal progress since problems were first identified seven years ago; a 2023 GAO audit found one prime contractor lost more than one million F-35 spare parts worth more than $85 million between 2018 and 2022.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Persistent F-35 parts tracking failures worth up to $2.5 billion in unrecorded inventory create sustained sustainment-contract risk and regulatory exposure for prime contractors, while reinforcing Congressional appetite for right-to-repair provisions in the fiscal 2027 NDAA that industry is actively opposing.

US inflation stays high as petrol eases below $4 a gallon
