NORMAL Global HIGH · 60
Russian Progress cargo ship docks at ISS after delay
The Progress M-25 cargo ship docked at the space-facing port of the Poisk module at 9:44 a.m. EDT on Saturday, 260 miles above the Black Sea, after lifting off from Baikonur on September 16 following a week-long delay caused by a technical problem with the Soyuz-2-1b rockets third stage. Roscosmos did not disclose the nature of the issue, which required the rocket to be rolled off the pad and returned to its hangar; it was the first time the Soyuz-2-1b variant had been used to launch a Progress mission.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
The undisclosed third-stage fault and non-standard booster variant introduce a reliability question for Roscosmos resupply operations at a time when ISS logistics margins are already compressed, a consideration for any operator or insurer with ISS-dependent programmes.

US clears potential $24.3B F-35 sale to Saudi Arabia

US Air Force declares $14B aircraft parts shortage
Air Force Materiel Command Lt. Gen. Linda Hurry told the Air and Space Forces Association 2026 conference that the US Air Force faces a $14 billion hole in aircraft parts supply, with the supply-driven aircraft grounding rate up 87 percent over seven years. Around 53 percent of the fleet is over 30 years old and 55 percent has exceeded its useful service life, compounding the sustainment challenge as operational demand from the Iran conflict intensifies.

An 87 percent rise in supply-driven groundings combined with a $14 billion parts deficit means US air power availability is structurally constrained at exactly the moment the service is being asked to sustain combat operations and extend B-1 and B-2 service lives, creating durable demand for sustainment and MRO contractors able to deliver at speed.

URGENT North America CRITICAL · 85
Pentagon and Lockheed sign AIM-260 production framework deal
The US Defense Department and Lockheed Martin signed a framework agreement to expand production of the AIM-260 Joint Advanced Tactical Missile, laying groundwork for a potential future multiyear buy; the agreement is neither a contract nor a missile order but a demand signal intended to justify capacity investment. Latvias government approved the acquisition of Morana 155mm self-propelled wheeled howitzers from Czech manufacturer Excalibur Army, reversing a June 2025 letter of intent with Sweden's BAE Systems Bofors for 18 Archer systems, citing more favourable pricing, delivery terms and local defence industry involvement.
