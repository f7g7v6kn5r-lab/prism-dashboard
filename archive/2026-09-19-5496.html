NORMAL North America HIGH · 60
SpaceX wins NASA StarBurst launch as Falcon 9 task order
NASA selected SpaceX to provide launch services for the StarBurst mission, a small satellite designed to detect short gamma-ray bursts from neutron star mergers, with liftoff scheduled no earlier than 2028 aboard a Falcon 9 from Space Launch Complex 40 at Cape Canaveral Space Force Station. The award is a firm-fixed-price task order under NASAs Venture-Class Acquisition of Dedicated and Rideshare contract, an indefinite-delivery/indefinite-quantity vehicle with a maximum potential value of $1 billion across all participating contractors over ten years.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
The selection reinforces SpaceXs dominance of NASAs low-cost rideshare pipeline and adds another anchor customer to the Bandwagon programme, which increases launch cadence predictability for smallsat operators planning mid-inclination orbits.

NORMAL Global MEDIUM · 45
NASA Roman telescope powers on coronagraph after solar array deployment
NASAs Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope, launched on a Falcon Heavy on a $4.3 billion mission, has deployed its solar array and communication systems and powered on its Coronagraph Instrument, advancing the observatory toward its operational configuration at the L2 Lagrange point. The telescope carries a 300-megapixel wide-field camera and is expected to downlink 2,500 terabytes of data over its five-year primary mission, scanning the cosmos approximately 1,000 times faster than Hubble.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Roman reaching commissioning milestones on schedule validates Falcon Heavys role as a workhorse for high-value science payloads and positions NASA to begin delivering the dark matter and dark energy survey data that underpins multiple downstream astrophysics and national security sensing programmes.

US approves potential $24.3B F-35 sale to Saudi Arabia
The US State Department approved the potential sale of 48 Lockheed Martin F-35 Lightning II aircraft, 49 Pratt and Whitney engines, communications equipment, spare parts and support items to Saudi Arabia, with an estimated value of $24.3 billion. URGENT Asia-Pacific CRITICAL · 85
AI chatbot nearly triggered US military boarding of Chinese ship
A US Special Operations Command Pacific analyst used an AI chatbot to analyse a Chinese vessels manifest during the Iran war, and the system incorrectly concluded the ship was carrying nuclear weapons programme components by fusing open-source and classified signals intelligence; the analyst then used AI again to format the finding into a standard intelligence report that was sent up the chain of command. US military aircraft were airborne and armed personnel were preparing to board the vessel before senior officials intervened and identified the AI-generated error just before the operation commenced.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS

US weighs pulling up to 40,000 troops from Europe
NBC reported that Gen. Alexus Grynkewich, commander of US European Command and NATOs Supreme Allied Commander Europe, submitted a report to Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth outlining options to withdraw between 25,000 and 40,000 of the approximately 80,000 US troops stationed in Europe, a reduction that would be the largest since the end of the Cold War. A drawdown of this scale would force European NATO members to accelerate sovereign defence spending and procurement programmes on an urgent timeline, creating near-term demand for European defence primes and indirectly reducing US forward-basing infrastructure that currently absorbs significant sustainment contracts.
