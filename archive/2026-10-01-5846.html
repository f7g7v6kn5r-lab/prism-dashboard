URGENT North America CRITICAL · 90
Starship reaches orbit on Flight 14 with Starlink payload
SpaceX sent Starship into orbit for the first time during its 14th test flight on 28 September, a milestone the company describes as a major step for the worlds largest launch system. Flight 14 also carried 26 Starlink V3 satellites, beginning what SpaceX hopes will become a much larger next-generation satellite network. SpaceX has already caught the Super Heavy booster three times using the launch tower chopstick arms, but the upper stage Ship still needs to be caught, with Elon Musk indicating a first Ship catch could be attempted during Flight 15.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Orbital Starship capability transforms SpaceXs satellite throughput economics and validates the heavy-lift architecture NASA requires for future lunar missions, making this the single most consequential launch milestone for both commercial and government space programmes this year.

NORMAL North America CRITICAL · 78
Space Force picks Northrop and True Anomaly for GHOST-R
Space Systems Command awarded prototype contracts to Northrop Grumman and True Anomaly under the Geosynchronous High-Resolution Optical Space-Based Tactical Reconnaissance programme, known as GHOST-R, on 18 September, targeting high-resolution space-to-space imagery of objects more than 22,000 miles above Earth. Both companies are already under contract with the Space Force to develop space-based interceptors for the Golden Dome missile defence initiative. The new GHOST-R prototypes are set to launch directly to geosynchronous orbit by 2028 and fly government-owned and operated missions by 2029.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
GHOST-R directly addresses the find-fix-track-target gap in geostationary orbit that US Space Command has publicly acknowledged, and the dual award to an established prime and a four-year-old startup signals the Space Force is deliberately seeding a competitive orbital-ISR industrial base ahead of anticipated adversary GEO activity.

URGENT North America HIGH · 72
NASA Crew-13 set for ISS launch from Cape Canaveral
NASA and SpaceX are preparing to launch Crew-13 aboard Dragon Grace on a Falcon 9 from Space Launch Complex 40 at Cape Canaveral Space Force Station, targeting 11:10 a.m. EDT on Thursday. Commander Jessica Watkins is joined by NASA astronaut Luke Delaney, Canadian Space Agency astronaut Joshua Kutryk, and Roscosmos cosmonaut Sergey Teteryatnikov, with a 55 percent chance of acceptable weather forecast at launch. Docking is scheduled to occur about eight hours after launch, and the booster, B1101, will target a touchdown at Landing Zone 40 less than eight minutes after liftoff.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Crew-13 maintains continuous US crew access to the ISS through 2030 and validates the Dragon Grace vehicle, sustaining the operational tempo that underpins NASAs commercial crew contract pipeline and Roscosmos partnership at a time of heightened geopolitical tension.

NORMAL North America HIGH · 62
NASA plans unpiloted Starliner test in December
NASA and Boeing are targeting a December-January unpiloted Starliner test flight to validate redesigned propulsion system valves and other modifications ordered after the 2024 mission that stranded two astronauts on the station for more than nine months. If the test succeeds, a piloted flight with commander Warren Hoburg and other crew members to be named later is planned for mid 2028. SpaceX Crew Dragon remains the only currently operational US astronaut ferry ship, and NASA has stated its goal of restoring two independent crew transportation providers before the ISS retires in 2030.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
A successful unpiloted Starliner test is a precondition for Boeing recovering any share of NASA crew rotation revenue, making the December window the most important near-term binary for assessing whether Boeing can sustain a credible commercial crew business alongside its sixth-generation fighter commitments.

China flies Guowang and Yaogan-40 in September push
China completed its ninth and tenth launches of September, orbiting the 26th group of Guowang national satellite internet satellites on a Long March 8A from Hainan on 23 September and a fourth group of Yaogan-40 satellites on a Long March 6A from Taiyuan on 24 September. URGENT North America CRITICAL · 93
Boeing wins US Navy F/A-XX development contract
The Pentagon awarded Boeing a development contract to produce test aircraft for the US Navy next-generation stealth carrier fighter, designated F/A-XX and designed to replace the F/A-18E/F Super Hornet, beating rival Northrop Grumman; the programme could grow to hundreds of billions over its lifetime as production scales and international customers place orders. The award follows Boeing selection in March last year to build the Air Force F-47, giving Boeing two sixth-generation fighter contracts in consecutive years with development work concentrated in its St Louis, Missouri facilities. Lockheed Martin was eliminated from the competition in March 2025 after Navy officials determined its design had not met required criteria.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Boeing now holds the central position in Americas entire sixth-generation fighter architecture, but simultaneous development of F/A-XX and F-47 from a single site that is also winding down Super Hornet and Growler production lines creates a critical industrial capacity bottleneck that could force schedule trades and create subcontractor opportunities for firms that can absorb overflow work.

Hegseth cuts general ranks, launches new warfare command
Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth announced at Marine Corps Base Quantico that the US military has reduced its general and admiral ranks by 20 percent, describing a 10 percent firing and a 10 percent slot reduction in what he called accountability that was long overdue. NORMAL North America CRITICAL · 78
Defense Department invests in Elmet for tungsten supply
The Defense Department announced a $450 million investment in The Elmet Group, described as the only US-owned fully integrated producer of tungsten and molybdenum materials and components, to strengthen the domestic supply chain for a critical mineral used in bullets, missiles and other weapons systems. The investment comes ahead of a 1 January deadline when a federal procurement rule barring tungsten sourced from China, Russia, Iran and North Korea from defence applications takes effect, at a time when the US has not mined tungsten domestically since 2015. Elmet will use $165 million of the funds to upgrade domestic manufacturing operations in Maine, Michigan and Ohio, approximately $150 million toward restarting tungsten mining in Imlay, Nevada, and another $100 million for establishing a refining and trading division.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
The January deadline creates a hard supply-chain discontinuity for any defence contractor currently sourcing tungsten from restricted-country supply chains, and the Elmet investment signals the Pentagon will use direct capital placement rather than tariffs alone to rebuild minerals independence -- a model likely to be replicated across other critical minerals facing similar sourcing restrictions.

NORMAL North America CRITICAL · 75
US Army signs five firms for PrSM Increment 4 development
The US Army signed Other Transaction Authority agreements with Anduril, Castelion, Lockheed Martin, Mach Industries and RTX to develop advanced long-range missile prototypes for the Precision Strike Missile Increment 4 programme, requiring missiles launchable from HIMARS and capable of hitting moving maritime and land targets at ranges exceeding 621 miles. A competitive fly-off and down-select is planned for fiscal year 2027, followed by a final fly-off in fiscal year 2028, with effective designs potentially earning additional agreements. ◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
The five-way competition forces Anduril, Castelion and Mach Industries -- all newer entrants -- to demonstrate credible long-range precision strike capability against Lockheed Martin and RTX by 2027, compressing the development timeline and creating a near-term revenue and reputational inflection point for each challenger firm.

NORMAL Iraq HIGH · 55
US Iraq withdrawal complete, anti-ISIL mission ends
The Pentagon confirmed that the final US troops deployed to Iraq as part of the anti-ISIL coalition have departed Erbil Airbase in the Kurdistan region, along with other international coalition forces and related equipment, in what Defense Department spokesman Sean Parnell described as an orderly conclusion to a 12-year campaign. Pentagon creates Autonomous Warfare Command for drones and AI
Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth announced the creation of the Autonomous Warfare Command, to be led by a four-star officer, dedicated to building and fielding autonomous and robotic capabilities across the US military, noting that creating a new command is rare with the last being US Space Command in 2019. A dedicated four-star autonomous warfare command institutionalises Pentagon demand for AI and drone systems at the highest organisational level, accelerating procurement timelines and signalling a sustained budget commitment that defence-tech investors and autonomy-focused firms can plan around.

NORMAL Ukraine HIGH · 68
Ukraines Fedorov launches Army of Robots initiative
Former Ukrainian Defense Minister Mykhailo Fedorov announced an Army of Robots initiative at the IT Arena conference in Lviv, calling for engineers, startups and capital to pool talent in a concentrated push to develop humanoid and other ground robots capable of replacing infantry on the front line. NORMAL Italy HIGH · 52
NaviGate proves onboard orbit software on D-Orbit carrier
Italian startup NaviGate, spun out of Sapienza University of Rome, successfully completed an in-orbit demonstration of its NaviCode Caravel software aboard a D-Orbit ION Satellite Carrier, computing the spacecraft position in real time using only the raw data of an existing onboard GNSS receiver with no additional hardware. Onboard positioning reached a best-case formal uncertainty of 5.2 cm, with mean values in the decimeter range across multiple runs using GPS-only measurements, and NaviGate verified the solutions were consistent with reference orbits reconstructed using the European Space Agencys flight dynamics software GODOT. The software moves precise orbit determination from the ground to the satellite itself, enabling real-time positional awareness and forward prediction directly where operational decisions are made.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
A validated software-only orbit determination product that runs on existing satellite hardware lowers the cost and complexity of autonomous constellation management, directly relevant to defence operators building responsive, ground-independent space architectures.

UAE used sanctioned Russian airline for Africa missions
A Defense News investigation drawing on thousands of flight records and leaked internal documents found that the UAE appears to have regularly used Aviacon Zitotrans, a sanctioned Russian airline linked to Kremlin military and paramilitary operations, for more than 100 missions flown on behalf of the UAE government and royal family since mid-2025. ◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
The exposure of UAE reliance on a US-sanctioned Russian carrier adds compliance and reputational risk to defence and aerospace firms operating in or through the UAE, and could complicate the countrys Western security partnerships at a moment when Gulf alignment is under close scrutiny.
