URGENT Europe CRITICAL · 82
Five nations agree joint CV90 vehicle purchase
Sweden, Finland, Norway, Lithuania and the Netherlands have agreed a joint purchase of more than 430 CV90 infantry fighting vehicles manufactured by BAE Systems Hagglunds, with an estimated outlay exceeding 7.5 billion euros, in what Finnish Defence Minister Antti Hakkanen called the largest joint land defence purchase in NATO. The contract will enter into force once all five countries simultaneously sign a joint order at a ceremony scheduled for Friday in Stockholm. Estonia had signed a declaration of intent in 2025 but subsequently withdrew from the initiative.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
A joint order of this scale through the Nordic Defence Cooperation initiative sets a procurement template that other NATO flank nations are likely to follow, concentrating European ground-vehicle spending on a small number of proven platforms and reinforcing BAE Systems Hagglunds as the dominant supplier for the northern flank.

URGENT North America HIGH · 72
Starship Flight 15 booster splashes in Gulf after abort
SpaceXs Starship Flight 15 attempted a double-catch of its booster using mechanical chopstick arms but the catch was aborted mid-test, sending the booster into the Gulf of Mexico instead of returning it to the launch tower. The failure highlights the engineering precision required to scale catch-and-reuse architecture to Starships size and directly affects launch pricing models for commercial satellite operators, defence contractors and NASA.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Until the double-catch sequence is proven reliable, Starship reusability rates and the cadence assumptions underpinning commercial and government manifest commitments remain at risk, which matters directly to any operator holding launch reservations on the vehicle.

FCC conditionally authorises 1 million Starmind satellites
The Federal Communications Commission granted SpaceX conditional authorisation to deploy up to 1 million Starmind satellites designed as orbital data centres, with FCC Chairman Brendan Carr describing the decision as opening a new frontier for computing infrastructure to support US leadership in artificial intelligence. Houthi strike on Jazan refinery kills one worker
A Houthi strike on a refinery in Jazan, southern Saudi Arabia, killed a Nepali worker and injured several others of different nationalities, triggering a temporary shutdown of some operations and a fire, according to Saudi state media citing the energy ministry. URGENT South Asia CRITICAL · 80
Makkah pact nations meet in Islamabad on Yemen conflict
Senior defence and foreign ministers from Pakistan, Saudi Arabia and Turkiye are meeting in Islamabad on Thursday under the Makkah pact, with Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia on the agenda, Defence Minister Khawaja Asif confirmed. Pakistan, Saudi Arabia and Turkiye signed the pact in August, pledging to come to each others aid in the event of an attack, though Asif described the agreement as still at a nascent stage requiring frequent meetings over the coming months. Pakistan brokered a June accord that led to a brief halt in fighting between the US and Iran, but Asif said the outcome of ongoing mediation was still fragile.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
A collective defence pact that explicitly excludes offensive action inside Yemen offers Saudi Arabia political solidarity but no near-term operational relief from Houthi strike campaigns, leaving Gulf air-defence and counter-drone demand structurally elevated regardless of diplomatic outcomes.

Pentagon delivers 25,000 plus drone systems to CENTCOM
The US Department of War announced that more than 25,000 drone and counter-drone systems were delivered to US Central Command by the Direct Reporting Portfolio Manager for Unmanned Systems, with more than 24,000 of those being counter-unmanned aerial systems delivered through Joint Interagency Task Force 401 and described as capable of creating a multi-layered defensive shield. NORMAL Europe HIGH · 73
UK MOD awards Babcock 8 billion pound submarine deal
The UK Ministry of Defence awarded Babcock an 8 billion pound Gateway Agreement to transform submarine maintenance and naval base services at His Majestys Naval Base Clyde, with Babcock describing it as the largest contract in its history at 8.7 billion pounds over five years including an 8 billion pound contract backlog and a 0.7 billion pound framework. The agreement is expected to create around 3,000 highly skilled roles including 900 jobs and 450 apprentice and graduate opportunities at HMNB Clyde, and is intended to tackle bottlenecks and modernise working practices to return Royal Navy submarines to sea faster. The contract, effective from 1 December, also covers HMNB Devonport and Devonport Dockyard.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
A long-term maintenance contract of this scale locks Babcock into the centre of UK nuclear deterrent sustainment, reinforcing its position as the dominant naval support prime at a moment when submarine availability is a live operational concern for NATO planning.

NORMAL Asia Pacific HIGH · 68
Philippines confirms six Chinooks in US defence package
Philippine Defence Secretary Gilberto Teodoro confirmed that six Boeing CH-47 Chinook heavy-lift helicopters, expected for delivery by 2029 if Manila secures a waiver, are part of a US defence agreement announced by President Trump during his Manila visit, comprising 2 billion dollars in foreign military financing and a 400 million dollar Maritime Defence Allocation. Teodoro said the US would also sell exclusive hard-to-get defence weapons to the Philippines as part of the deal, though he declined to disclose further details of the package. Confirmed Chinook procurement within a US-structured financing package deepens Philippine heavy-lift and maritime deterrence capacity in the West Philippine Sea, signalling sustained Boeing rotorcraft demand from Southeast Asian allies navigating Chinese pressure.

Lebanese military court pursues Hezbollah in explosives cases
Lebanese Military Court commissioner Judge Claude Ghanem issued three search notices on Wednesday for Hezbollah officials, including Bilal al-Asaad, after they failed to appear for questioning in the Dahr al-Baidar explosives case covering the Bcharre and Batroun highlands. First Military Investigating Judge Ghada Abu Alwan separately issued in-person arrest warrants for five suspects detained in northern Lebanon on 10 November and charged with forming an armed group and recruiting, equipping and arming its members. NORMAL Mali / West Africa HIGH · 60
FPV drones reshape Sahel insurgency in Mali and beyond
Low-cost first-person-view drones fitted with explosives, tactics developed in Ukraine, are being used by Tuareg-led fighters and an al-Qaeda affiliate in Mali against government forces and their Russian partners, with videos from April attacks showing coordinated strikes on bases, aircraft, fuel depots and vehicles. The Armed Conflict Location and Event Data project recorded 187 attacks by JNIM from 2023 through mid-November, with a combined figure reaching 235 across four West African countries when including operations by the Azawad Liberation Front and its predecessors. The threat is spreading to Burkina Faso, Niger and other West African conflict zones, challenging governments whose air-power advantage once shielded strategic infrastructure.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
The rapid geographic diffusion of sub-$1,000 FPV drone attack tactics from Ukraine into the Sahel signals that counter-drone technology and electronic warfare capabilities will face demand well beyond traditional high-intensity conflict zones, expanding addressable markets for defence autonomy firms.

NORMAL United States HIGH · 58
Pentagon Drone Dominance Program holds bomber uncrewed aerial vehicle trials
The Pentagons Drone Dominance Program held its Gauntlet 2.5 event on 13 November at Camp Grayling, Michigan, where eight suppliers competed in the first competition for reusable bomber uncrewed aerial vehicles. US manufacturer Swarm Defense developed a prototype platform in just two and a half weeks for what the DDP described as the programmes most demanding and expedited testing to date. All but two of the eight qualifying vendors were Ukrainian, reflecting the expertise Ukraine has built in this category of uncrewed aerial vehicle.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Gauntlet 2.5 demonstrates that the Pentagon is institutionalising speed-to-prototype as a competitive criterion for autonomous bomber platforms, compressing development timelines in ways that favour agile non-traditional vendors and challenge established primes.

NORMAL United States HIGH · 65
US Senate proposes $544M for containerised ground missiles
The US Senate Appropriations Committees fiscal year 2027 defence funding proposal, released on 16 November, recommends an additional $544 million for 1,000 low-cost containerised missile rounds and 62 ground launchers under a line designated Low-Cost Containerised Munition, Ground Launch Systems. The original fiscal year 2027 budget estimate contained no funding under this line, and the proposed increase has not yet been enacted into law. The recommendation forms part of a broader congressional effort to expand production of affordable precision weapons, alongside a separate proposed $267.315 million increase for 2,000 additional rounds under Low-Cost Cruise Missile Systems.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
The Senate proposal signals sustained congressional appetite for affordable, scalable distributed-strike munitions, offering a growth signal for lower-cost missile and launcher manufacturers competing for a share of a rapidly expanding containerised fires market.

NORMAL Italy HIGH · 60
Italy claims 2.8 percent GDP defence target met in 2026
Italian officials told Defense News that the country has achieved combined defence and domestic security spending equal to 2.8 percent of GDP in 2026, fulfilling a promise made at the July NATO summit, even though the figure does not appear explicitly in the late-issued 2026 budget. Pure defence spending, calculated from other figures, reached an estimated 23.6 billion euros, up from 22.49 billion euros the prior year, with procurement calculated at 9.33 billion euros from the defence ministry. Government sources said the remaining 0.7 percent of GDP, covering activities such as frontier control and combatting terrorism, is handled outside the defence budget.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Italys claimed milestone matters to defence primes because it validates a sustained procurement budget trajectory toward the NATO 5 percent objective by 2035, underpinning multi-year platform programmes that suppliers are already pricing into forward order books.
