URGENT North America CRITICAL · 95
Starship reaches orbit on flight 14, deploying 26 Starlink V3 sats
SpaceX launched Starship on its 14th flight from Starbase, Texas, achieving orbit for the first time in the programmes history. One of three main engines shut down during ascent, prompting the flight control team to briefly call off the orbital insertion before reversing that decision after engineers confirmed other onboard engines could complete the burn; Starship then deployed 26 third-generation Starlink satellites at an altitude of roughly 269 km before ending the mission three hours into a planned ten-hour flight and splashing down in the Pacific.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Starships first orbital revenue-generating mission validates the architecture needed for NASA moon landing plans and positions SpaceX to begin consolidating commercial launch volume away from Falcon 9, but the early engine failure and shortened mission signal that routine operational cadence remains further away than the $2 trillion SpaceX valuation implies.

NASA plans unpiloted Starliner test flight in December-January
NASA is working with Boeing to launch an unpiloted Starliner capsule in the December-January timeframe to test redesigned propulsion system valves and other upgrades ordered after a 2024 mission stranded two astronauts on the International Space Station for more than nine months. If the unpiloted flight succeeds, NASA and Boeing plan a piloted Starliner mission in mid-2028, commanded by veteran astronaut Warren Hoburg, with the programme aimed at restoring a second US crew transport capability alongside SpaceXs Crew Dragon.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Boings path back to crewed ISS missions now runs through at least two sequential milestones spanning 18 months, meaning any further propulsion anomaly resets the timeline and leaves Crew Dragon as the sole US astronaut ferry well into 2028, concentrating programme risk and revenue on a single provider.

NASA confirms PRIMA telescope at $1.2B cost cap for 2033 launch
NASA announced the Probe far-Infrared Mission for Astrophysics, PRIMA, is moving into Phase B development, with a maximum project cost of $1.2 billion excluding launch, and designated it the founding mission in a new Probe Explorers class within the Explorers Program. URGENT Europe CRITICAL · 90
Russia raises 2027 defence budget 27% to $202.6B
Russian government documents seen by Reuters show plans to spend 17.1 trillion roubles, equivalent to $202.6 billion, on defence in 2027, a 27 percent increase over the 13.5 trillion roubles originally budgeted, and the highest figure since the start of the war in Ukraine in 2022. Total defence spending over the next three years is set at 50 trillion roubles, equivalent to $591.7 billion, while the government raised its 2026 budget deficit estimate to 3.2 percent of GDP from 1.6 percent previously.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Russias sustained defence spending escalation, funded partly through new windfall taxes and a 26 percent increase in net borrowing, signals that Moscow is planning for a prolonged conflict horizon well into 2028 and beyond, which will keep European rearmament pressure elevated and sustain demand for NATO-aligned defence procurement.

US appeals court upholds Pentagons blacklisting of Anthropic
A federal appeals court upheld the Pentagons designation of Anthropic as a national security supply chain risk by a 2-1 decision, ruling it was reasonable for the Pentagon to act after Anthropic refused to allow its products to be used for autonomous weapons or mass surveillance. The majority opinion, citing Defence Secretary Pete Hegseths argument, stated that overly constrained AI models could cause important military operations to fail, while Anthropic said it is considering its options including full appeals court review.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
The ruling entrenches a Pentagon doctrine that AI safety restrictions constitute an operational liability, creating a structural advantage for AI vendors willing to remove guardrails and a corresponding legal and reputational risk for safety-focused competitors seeking military contracts.
