SpaceX stacks Starship Flight 14 for Sept 28 launch bid
SpaceX assembled Ship 41 atop Super Heavy booster B21 at Starbase in Texas, with a launch window opening at 7:15 a.m. CDT on September 28, pending FAA licence approval. The mission would deploy 26 next-generation Version 3 Starlink satellites on a nearly 10-hour flight, and a temporary flight restriction filed by the FAA through October 7 is already in place.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
FAA approval is the single binary gate controlling whether a broad class of lunar, heavy-lift and satellite supply-chain contracts remain on 2027 timelines or slip further, making the licence decision the most consequential near-term catalyst for the entire upstream space investment thesis.

Space supply chain sits in neutral waiting on Starship
Analysts warn that companies building lunar modules, satellite hardware and heavy space components are sitting in neutral until Starship becomes operationally reliable, a dependency that runs through NASA and Department of Defense roadmaps. Redwire is up 48.16 percent year-to-date at $11.26 while Karman Holdings is down 54.8 percent year-to-date at $33.07, including a 35.62 percent drop over the past month, with the market pricing the two names as separate trades despite shared Starship exposure.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
The divergence between Redwire and Karman demonstrates that the market is not pricing Starship dependency uniformly, creating a potential mean-reversion trade if orbital qualification resolves the near-term uncertainty on schedule.

NORMAL North America HIGH · 70
SpaceX stops taking Falcon 9 bookings past 2028
SpaceX is reportedly no longer accepting Falcon 9 launch reservations beyond 2028, signalling a plan to shift customers to Starship as engineering and manufacturing resources move away from Falcon once the newer vehicle is flying at pace. In 2025 Falcon 9 launched 165 times and accounted for nearly half of all launches worldwide, meaning the customer base that depends on rideshare and dedicated Falcon 9 missions will need alternative options within approximately two years.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Smaller satellite operators and startups that rely on Falcon 9 rideshare pricing face a structural disruption to their launch economics if no competitor scales to comparable cadence before 2028, directly affecting mission planning timelines and investor confidence in constellations not yet fully deployed.

Starfish Space Otter set for Oct 1 orbital debut
Tukwila-based Starfish Space is launching its full-size 300-kilogram Otter space servicing vehicle no earlier than October 1 on SpaceXs Transporter-18 rideshare mission from Vandenberg Space Force Base, backed by NASAs $15 million Small Spacecraft Propulsion and Inspection Capability mission. NORMAL North America HIGH · 60
NASA moves PRIMA telescope into Phase B development
NASA announced that its Probe far-Infrared Mission for Astrophysics, PRIMA, is moving into Phase B development, with a maximum project cost ceiling that does not include launch costs, and the mission will be developed by NASAs Jet Propulsion Laboratory alongside Goddard Space Flight Center and Marshall Space Flight Center. Seven international space agencies from Canada, France, Germany, Japan, South Korea and the United Kingdom are collaborating, with PRIMA establishing a new Probe Explorers class within NASAs Explorers Program and a planned launch in 2033.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
The multi-national structure and $1.2 billion cost ceiling confirm PRIMA as a mid-tier procurement opportunity across seven allied space agencies, offering a long contracting horizon for instrument and detector suppliers seeking stable government revenue into the early 2030s.

NORMAL North America HIGH · 58
Google to launch AI satellite data centre Oct 1
Google is preparing to launch a prototype satellite called the Minimum Viable Product aboard a SpaceX Falcon 9 from Vandenberg Space Force Base on October 1, carrying four tensor processing units with computing power roughly equivalent to a single data-centre server as part of Project Suncatcher. The satellite is designed to answer simple AI queries and is expected to operate for about a year, with Google envisioning eventual compact constellations of solar-powered satellites connected through optical communications links to reduce terrestrial land and resource demand.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Googles first on-orbit AI compute demonstration creates a direct competitive signal for defence cloud and edge computing programmes, as orbital AI processing capability would reduce latency for time-sensitive intelligence workloads beyond the reach of terrestrial data centres.

Iraqi airports halt Iran flights under US sanctions
Four Iraqi airports — Baghdad, Najaf, Erbil and Sulaimaniyah — have suspended all flights to and from Iran following new US aviation sanctions on any global company doing business with Iranian airlines, the latest measure under Operation Economic Outcast launched in August. URGENT North America CRITICAL · 83
Appeals court upholds Pentagons blacklisting of Anthropic
A federal appeals court upheld the Pentagons March designation of Anthropic as a national security supply chain risk by a 2-1 decision, after Anthropic refused to allow its products to be used for autonomous weapons or mass surveillance. The majority opinion, written by Judge Gregory Katsas, stated that overly constrained AI models shutting down unexpectedly could cause important military operations to fail, siding with Defense Secretary Pete Hegseths argument that Anthropics safety restrictions could compromise military operations.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
The ruling sets a binding legal precedent that AI safety restrictions can constitute a disqualifying supply chain risk for defence contracts, forcing every AI company pursuing Pentagon business to choose explicitly between commercial safety guardrails and military eligibility.
