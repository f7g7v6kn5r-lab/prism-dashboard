SpaceX awaits FAA nod for Starship Flight 14
SpaceX has stacked Ship 41 atop Super Heavy booster B21 at Starbase in south Texas, assembling a 124-metre rocket ahead of Flight 14, which targets an earliest launch of 28 September at 7:15 a.m. CDT pending a Federal Aviation Administration licence. A temporary flight restriction lasting from 23 September through 7 October has been put in place by the FAA. The mission plans to deploy 26 Starlink Version 3 satellites on a nearly 10-hour flight.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
FAA approval of an orbital licence for a vehicle of this scale sets a regulatory precedent that will directly shape the cadence of Starship-class commercial and government payload manifests, with implications for launch pricing and small-launcher market share.

SpaceX to launch classified USSF-385 mission
A Falcon 9 is scheduled to lift off from Vandenberg Space Force Base carrying the USSF-385 mission, believed to be the third flight in a series deploying satellites based on SpaceXs Starshield platform, a government-oriented variant of Starlink. NORMAL North America HIGH · 60
NASA moves PRIMA telescope into Phase B
NASA announced the Probe far-Infrared Mission for Astrophysics, PRIMA, is entering Phase B development, with a maximum project cost of $1.2 billion excluding launch and other costs, and a target launch in 2033. The observatory will be developed by NASAs Jet Propulsion Laboratory in collaboration with NASAs Goddard Space Flight Center and Marshall Space Flight Center alongside space agencies from Canada, France, Germany, Japan, South Korea and the United Kingdom. If approved, PRIMA will move forward into Phase C.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
A confirmed $1.2 billion cost cap and multinational partner structure signals sustained government astrophysics procurement into the early 2030s, anchoring instrument and detector supply chains at JPL and allied national space agencies for the next several years.

MDA Space launches defence-focused AURORA BLACK
MDA Space has expanded its AURORA product line with MDA AURORA BLACK, a software-defined satellite tailored to military and government customers requiring enhanced security against physical and cyber threats in contested environments. NORMAL North America HIGH · 50
NASA completes ASCENT dual-mode CubeSat testing
Engineers at NASAs Marshall Space Flight Center in Huntsville, Alabama, have completed environmental and physical testing on the ASCENT Propulsion Dual Mode spacecraft, a shoebox-sized vehicle that feeds both a high-thrust combustion engine and low-thrust electrospray thrusters from a single non-toxic propellant tank. The mission is manifested to launch no earlier than October 1 aboard a SpaceX Falcon 9 from Vandenberg Space Force Base in California, after which it will begin a nine-month mission in an orbit about 325 miles above Earth. The integrated design eliminates the separate fuel tanks traditionally required for two propulsion systems, reducing mass and volume penalties.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Successful flight demonstration of dual-mode non-toxic propulsion on a CubeSat would validate a design approach that could materially reduce launch mass and cost for defence small-satellite programmes requiring both rapid manoeuvre and long-duration station-keeping.

URGENT North America CRITICAL · 88
Pentagon blacklisting of Anthropic upheld on appeal
The US Court of Appeals in Washington ruled 2-1 to uphold the Pentagons designation of Anthropic as a national security supply chain risk, finding it was reasonable after the company refused to allow its products to be used for autonomous weapons or mass surveillance. The majority opinion, written by Judge Gregory Katsas, cited the possibility of overly constrained AI models shutting down unexpectedly and causing important military operations to fail, siding with arguments made by Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth. Anthropic said it respectfully disagrees with the decision and is considering its options, including review by the full appeals court.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
The ruling establishes that the Pentagon can exclude AI vendors whose safety guardrails conflict with autonomous weapons requirements, creating a structural bifurcation in the defence AI market between unrestricted and compliance-constrained model providers and accelerating demand for rivals without equivalent restrictions.

Trump administration obligates Ukraine security funds
The Trump administration has made legally binding commitments to spend almost all of the $400 million Congress approved for military aid to Ukraine late last year, ensuring that the money will not expire on 30 September, according to three sources familiar with the plan. As of early this week, $307 million of the $400 million Ukraine Security Assistance Initiative funding had been obligated, with plans to lock in the remaining $93 million before the fiscal year ends. US F-35s debut at Indias Tarang Shakti exercise
US Air Force F-35 fighters are joining Indias multinational Tarang Shakti air exercise at Jodhpur air base in Rajasthan, bringing fifth-generation stealth aircraft to Indian air drills for the first time alongside Rafale jets from France and F-16s from the United Arab Emirates, with eight countries participating and 32 more attending as observers. NORMAL North America HIGH · 72
US Navy stands up autonomous systems war centre
The US Navy established the Robot and Autonomous Systems Warfighting Development Center at Joint Expeditionary Base Little Creek, Virginia, with Chief of Naval Operations Admiral Daryl Caudle stating that a prototype is not combat power and that trained sailors operating reliable systems within defined command frameworks are the measure of operational readiness. The centre will first train sailors to employ autonomous systems and then develop a broader dissemination programme across the fleet. Caudle defined the intended cycle as field, employ, learn, improve and scale.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Institutionalising autonomous systems training at a dedicated Navy centre creates a formal demand signal for vendors to meet fleet-ready rather than prototype-ready standards, which will favour companies with established logistics, maintenance and software support infrastructure over early-stage developers.

Iran offers Hormuz deal if US lifts blockade
Irans foreign minister Abbas Araghchi detailed a proposal at a private gathering on the sidelines of the UN General Assembly in New York, offering to reopen the Strait of Hormuz and resume nuclear talks within seven days if the US lifts its naval blockade, waives sanctions on Iranian oil sales and observes a ceasefire including Lebanon. Oil falls on US-Iran truce hopes

NORMAL France / NATO HIGH · 72
Thales in advanced talks on HexaForce AI system
French defence technology firm Thales said it is in quite advanced discussions with a number of NATO countries about deploying its AI-powered command-and-control system HexaForce, which was tested during NATOs Coalition Warrior Interoperability Exercise in Poland in June, according to the companys vice president for multi-domain operations Patrick Moreau. The system is positioned as a multi-domain C2 overlay that plugs into legacy and national systems from strategic to tactical level and allows users to choose their own AI models to avoid vendor lock-in. Thales defined a goal with end users to be able to plan 1,000 target missions a day, a tenfold increase.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Thales advancing HexaForce discussions with multiple NATO buyers signals that European sovereign AI C2 is moving from demonstration to near-term procurement, creating a competitive pressure point for Palantir and other US-based C2 incumbents in the alliance.

NORMAL India HIGH · 55
India outlines AI integration plans at DEFCON 2026
At DEFCON 2026 in New Delhi on September 24, Defence Minister Rajnath Singh and Lieutenant General Puneet Ahuja outlined plans to integrate AI across Indias armed forces in areas including threat prediction, target acquisition, surveillance, logistics, predictive maintenance and cyber defence. Ahuja stated that AI will change the way soldiers fight rather than replace them, emphasising a human-machine teaming model in which soldiers will control many unmanned systems. Chief of Defence Staff General N S Raja Subramani said future battlefield capabilities will rely more on networked systems, data, algorithms and software, and less on independent weapon systems.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Indias public commitment to an AI-driven military modernisation agenda at ministerial level signals an accelerating procurement pipeline for AI, autonomy and sensor technology suppliers seeking to enter one of the worlds largest defence markets.

NORMAL Italy CRITICAL · 78
Italy signs deal for two DDX destroyers
Italy signed a contract brokered by European procurement office OCCAR for two DDX destroyers, to be built by Orizzonte Sistemi Navali, a joint venture between state shipyard Fincantieri with 51 percent and Leonardo with 49 percent, with delivery scheduled for 2033 and 2035. The 13,500-tonne, 180-metre vessels will carry a crew of 251 and have a range of 4,000 nautical miles, featuring Leonardos SADOC 5 command-and-control system, digital S-band AESA radar and MBDA weapon systems. The destroyers will sail alongside but ultimately replace the Italian navys two Horizon vessels.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
The €3.7 billion DDX award is one of the largest single European naval contracts of 2026 and reinforces the multi-year fleet renewal spending cycle that is driving sustained revenue for Fincantieri, Leonardo and MBDA across European defence primes.

NORMAL United States HIGH · 55
Ethereal Space wins $27.4M NOAA contract
Ethereal Space has been awarded Task Order 1 under NOAAs ProTech 2.0 Space-Based Environmental Monitoring programme, with a total contract value of approximately $27.4 million and a 24-month term, with initial data deliveries scheduled to begin December 1, 2026.
