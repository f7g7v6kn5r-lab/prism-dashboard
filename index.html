Todays dominant theme is the structural unsustainability of the US war posture against Iran: the Congressional Budget Office confirms $38 billion spent through August 1, with between one-half and two-thirds of key missile-defense interceptor stocks consumed, and the Defense Department inspector general separately documenting loss of at least 30 MQ-9 Reaper drones and damage to hundreds of military buildings across eight countries, while rebuilding those interceptor stocks will take at least five years even at accelerated production rates. The contradiction that should alarm defence investors is that the US Air Force simultaneously confirmed deployment of on-orbit space weapons and signed a Germany-US agreement to expand Patriot and Tomahawk co-production, signalling an intent to escalate and modernise even as the CBO makes clear the current munitions base cannot sustain a second simultaneous theatre commitment — the posture is expanding faster than the industrial base can reload it. Watch over the coming days whether the Fed rate hike to 3.75-4 percent, the first in more than three years and driven explicitly by war-inflation, triggers a measurable tightening in defence credit markets or pushes Congress toward an emergency supplemental authorisation, since either response will set the pace of the interceptor recapitalisation programme that RTX, LMT and the Germany-US co-production deal all depend on.

US confirms on-orbit space weapons amid arms race fear
US Air Force Secretary Troy Meink stated at the Air, Space and Cyber Conference that the United States has deployed on-orbit space control weapons capable of defending the joint force against hostile adversary action, the strongest public acknowledgement yet of US weapons in orbit. China responded through foreign ministry spokesperson Guo Jiakun, urging Washington to stop expanding military build-up in outer space, while Chinese state media and military analyst Song Zhongping warned the disclosure marks a shift toward direct orbital weapons deployment and risks forcing other countries to respond.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
An acknowledged on-orbit weapons capability formalises a new domain of great-power competition that will accelerate sovereign investment in space situational awareness, hardened satellite architectures and counter-space systems — creating sustained demand for the commercial Earth observation and signals intelligence operators already servicing defence customers in this edition.

URGENT North America CRITICAL · 85
US unprepared for nuclear attack in space, war games find
An April 2026 report by the American Foreign Policy Council, drawing on two 2025 war games conducted with former officials from the National Security Council, the Pentagon, the intelligence community, the Space Force and US Space Command, found that US decision-makers lack clear guidance, legal clarity and operational plans for a space-based nuclear crisis. The war games tested three scenarios — a suspected nuclear device in orbit, a confirmed weapon used for coercion, and a detonation — and found the US unprepared in each case, even as Moscow has demanded demilitarisation of space and Ukrainian intelligence has published documents on a Russian next-generation military space platform.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
The documented gap between an acknowledged on-orbit weapons posture and a complete absence of contingency planning for nuclear escalation in space exposes a critical governance and operational risk that defence primes with space resilience and hardening portfolios should position around immediately.

URGENT North America CRITICAL · 75
SpaceX to launch USSF-259 national security payload tonight
SpaceX is preparing to launch the USSF-259 mission for the US Space Force from Vandenberg Space Force Base on September 16, after a Tuesday attempt was scrubbed prior to fueling with no public explanation, with liftoff now scheduled for 9:07 p.m. EDT. The classified payload, designated TH-1 in public documents and expected to deploy into a low Earth orbit inclined roughly 80 degrees from the equator, will be the third Space Force national security launch in a month under the National Security Space Launch Phase 3 Lane 1 contract, following USSF-366 on August 15 and USSF-153 on September 10.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Three Space Force national security launches in one month under a single NSSL Phase 3 Lane 1 contract demonstrates an accelerating cadence of classified constellation deployment that reinforces Space Systems Command reliance on SpaceX as the primary NSSL provider and signals growing urgency around on-orbit capability given the space weapons environment confirmed today.

Roman telescope fuel reserve extends mission to 22 years
NASA announced that the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope, launched on a SpaceX Falcon Heavy from Kennedy Space Center on August 30 2026 and not yet at its Sun-Earth L2 orbit, has sufficient fuel for at least 22 years of potential science operations, more than doubling the original ten-year plan. NASA Goddard Space Flight Center director Jamie Dunn attributed the extended fuel reserve to precise launch execution by SpaceX and a spacecraft mass lower than the maximum budgeted value, reducing the propellant consumed during post-launch burns.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
A 22-year Roman mission substantially expands the science return on the investment and reinforces the commercial value of precision launch services, with SpaceX Falcon Heavy accuracy directly extending the operational life of a flagship NASA observatory.

BREAKING Middle East CRITICAL · 100
Iran war costs $38B and depletes two-thirds of US interceptors
The Congressional Budget Office estimates the Iran conflict has cost the US military approximately $38 billion through August 1, with between one-half and two-thirds of the combined US inventory of Patriot, THAAD, Standard Missile-3 and Standard Missile-6 interceptors consumed, at a replacement cost of $13.1 billion within a total munitions bill of $21.7 billion. The CBO found that rebuilding those interceptor stocks will take at least five years even if production rates increase, while a separate Defense Department inspector general report estimated the total cost of Operation Epic Fury at $33.4 billion through June 29, documenting loss of at least 30 MQ-9 Reaper drones and damage to hundreds of military buildings across bases in Kuwait, Bahrain, Qatar, UAE, Saudi Arabia, Iraq, Oman and Jordan.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
A confirmed depletion of between one-half and two-thirds of four critical interceptor inventories with a minimum five-year rebuild horizon creates a structural, multi-year procurement demand signal for RTX, LMT and co-production partners such as MBDAs German operations — but also exposes a readiness gap that adversaries can observe and exploit before stocks recover.

Germany and US sign Patriot and Tomahawk co-production pact
German Defense Minister Boris Pistorius and US Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth signed an agreement positioning Germany as an additional production hub for Patriot interceptors and Tomahawk cruise missiles during a visit by Pistorius to the Pentagon, with MBDAs German arm already producing PAC-2 interceptors and set to begin PAC-3 MSE production at its Schrobenhausen plant later this year. Germany is also considering which Tomahawk components it could supply and intends to buy the missiles alongside Typhon launchers as part of an emerging deep precision strike capability.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
German co-production of PAC-3 MSE and potential Tomahawk components directly addresses the five-year interceptor rebuild gap confirmed by the CBO, creating immediate industrial and contract opportunity for MBDA Germany, RTX and LMT at a moment when US production alone is demonstrably insufficient.
