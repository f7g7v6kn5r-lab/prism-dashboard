NORMAL Europe HIGH · 62
Himera and GomSpace test Obria Nano satellite
Ukrainian startup Himera and Danish manufacturer GomSpace confirmed on October 27 that their Obria Nano satellite, launched October 1 aboard the SpaceX Transporter-18 rideshare mission, is undergoing orbital tests. The project is one of at least two European-backed initiatives to give Ukraine greater control over satellite communications, following disruptions in October to SpaceX Starlink and Starshield services that affected Ukrainian military operations.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Ukraine building sovereign satellite communications capacity outside SpaceX dependency reduces a single-point-of-failure risk that has already demonstrably degraded battlefield command networks, making European-backed smallsat constellation investments a structurally defensible programme even under fiscal pressure.

NORMAL Europe MEDIUM · 42
PERSEUS project to test laser technology for space comms
Craft Prospect, Fraunhofer Centre for Applied Photonics and Vector Photonics have launched the PERSEUS project to evaluate Photonic-Crystal Surface-Emitting Lasers for space-to-space and space-to-ground optical communications, funded through the Advanced Connectivity Technologies programme delivered by Innovate UK and funded by the Department for Culture, Media and Sport. The project will test PCSEL performance under space-relevant conditions including radiation, vibration and thermal-vacuum campaigns, and identify engineering steps toward a commercial optical link solution.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
If PCSELs prove radiation-tolerant in orbit, they offer a path to longer-range and higher-capacity free-space optical links that could reduce dependence on radio-frequency spectrum, a significant operational advantage for both commercial and classified satellite networks.

Astranis announced on October 26 that it has upgraded its fourth launch contract with SpaceX to a dedicated Falcon 9 mission in 2028, carrying all six satellites in its Block 4 MicroGEO production run via direct-inject to geostationary orbit using an Impulse Helios upper stage. Goheung space complex clears review, 1,673 jobs expected

URGENT Middle East CRITICAL · 95
Iran war costs reach $47.1B with munitions above 60 percent
The Pentagon told Congress that cumulative costs of the Iran war through October 16 reached $47.1 billion, with more than 60 percent attributable to bombs, missiles and other munitions, adding approximately $3.5 billion in the roughly six weeks since the previous estimate. Of the total, $99 billion has been spent on munitions, depleting global stockpiles of critical weapons including Patriot air-defense interceptors and drawing down supplies from US military commands in Asia and Europe, according to congressional officials.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
A $99 billion munitions burn with no announced replenishment ceiling signals acute restocking demand across precision-strike and air-defense programmes, directly benefiting Lockheed Martin and RTX but raising serious questions about US deterrence capacity in the Indo-Pacific while the conflict in Iran continues.

NORMAL North America CRITICAL · 82
Air Force awards Lockheed $694M ARRW production deal
The US Air Force awarded Lockheed Martin a $694 million contract in late September to move the AGM-183A Air-Launched Rapid Response Weapon from prototyping and flight testing into production and fielding, with the announcement made Monday. Secretary of the Air Force Troy Meink described the award as a turning point in the advanced weapons roadmap, and Air Force Chief of Staff General Ken Wilsbach said the boost-glide weapon, capable of speeds greater than Mach 5, gives the Joint Force the ability to hold targets at risk anywhere in the world.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Transitioning ARRW from test to production under a $694 million award locks Lockheed Martin into a hypersonic strike production role at the moment when adversaries have demonstrated the ability to defeat conventional US air defenses, making this programme a direct beneficiary of the Iran conflict lessons.

Poland selected to host European PAC-3 maintenance hub
The US government has selected Poland to host a new European maintenance facility for Patriot Advanced Capability-3 interceptors produced by Lockheed Martin, with Pentagon acquisition chief Michael Duffey citing the investment as reinforcing deterrence along NATO eastern flank. URGENT Global CRITICAL · 77
Pentagon fears drone attacks on US and allied bases
Two government officials told The Intercept that top Pentagon leaders are expressing increasing concern about the vulnerability of US military bases globally and within the United States, following Iranian destruction of key Middle East bases including Navy facilities in Manama, Bahrain and the Combined Air Operations Center at Al Udeid Air Base in Qatar, as well as plots against US bombers at a British air base and a Russian campaign damaging European military facilities. NORMAL United States HIGH · 70
Pentagon pushes Cyber Effects-as-a-Service model
The Pentagon has been developing a Cyber Effects-as-a-Service model over the past several months, seeking to contract for specific cyber outcomes rather than bespoke tools, with Assistant Secretary of Defense for Cyber Policy Katie Sutton describing a desired shift from a contractual relationship to an operational partnership with industry. Sutton said demand from combatant commanders and department leadership for cyber capabilities far exceeds current supply, and Sutton's office hosted a tabletop exercise in late September with members of industry across the artificial intelligence and defense technology sectors along with US Cyber Command to game out how the Pentagon can build new operational models with industry.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
A shift from equipment-based to outcome-based cyber contracting would restructure how defence primes and specialist cyber firms bid for Pentagon work, favouring companies with persistent operational presence over those offering one-time tool deliveries.

NORMAL United Arab Emirates HIGH · 55
Dubai Police drones reach scenes ahead of patrols
Dubai Police has integrated an autonomous drone dispatch capability into its 999 command and control centre, allowing drones to be automatically deployed to emergency locations when surveillance cameras do not cover the incident site, giving officers a real-time view before the first patrol arrives. Captain Abdullah Rashid Al Maamari told Khaleej Times that the Drone Box system, introduced in 2021, combines GPS-based incident location, nearest-camera identification and automatic drone dispatch to help operators decide on additional emergency resources before ground units are on scene.

Spot robot rebranded as Cerberus for ICE deployment
Radeco, an official Boston Dynamics partner, modified the Spot quadruped robot and sold four units to US Immigration and Customs Enforcement under the name Cerberus, fitting the platform with thermal imaging, 360-degree cameras, sensors that detect radiation, toxic gases and explosives, military-grade communications equipment and a robotic arm; Boston Dynamics did not sell directly to ICE. NORMAL India CRITICAL · 75
India ammunition exports to Europe surge over 1,600 percent
India's Defence Secretary Rajesh Kumar Singh said demand from the European Union's ReArm Europe initiative, combined with India's expanding manufacturing base, has driven at least $872 million in ammunition and related exports to Europe in the four years since Russia's invasion of Ukraine, a more than 1,600 percent increase over the preceding four-year period. Central and East European countries including Romania, Slovenia, the Czech Republic, Slovakia, Bulgaria, Croatia and Poland were among the largest customers, with European destinations accounting for 40 percent of Indian global arms and ammunition exports in the last financial year.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Indias emergence as a major ammunition supplier to NATO-aligned customers diversifies European supply chains away from sole reliance on US and domestic EU producers, creating a durable export revenue stream for Indian defence manufacturers and opening deeper industrial cooperation pathways.

NORMAL Canada HIGH · 65
Britain declines to join Canada-led defence bank
Britain will not join the Canada-led Defence, Security and Resilience Bank, with the two countries instead agreeing to develop a cooperation framework between their separate defence-funding initiatives, the Multilateral Defence Mechanism and the Defence, Security and Resilience Bank. The two governments failed to agree on key issues including headquarters location, joint procurement levels, lending to members and rules around new members, with the DSRB, to be based in Canada, continuing to face challenges securing support from larger European nations including Germany.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
The failure to consolidate NATO allied defence financing into a single institution leaves the DSRB with reduced scale and credibility, slowing the pace at which pooled capital can reach European defence industry supply chains.

The US Army published a market survey asking whether the defence industrial base can deliver and support nearly 21,000 Infantry Squad Vehicles across two variants, the nine-passenger ISV-9 and the five-passenger ISV-Utility, subject to funding availability and potential Foreign Military Sales demand.
