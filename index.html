URGENT North America HIGH · 72
Starship Flight 15 booster splashes in Gulf
SpaceX attempted a double-catch of Starship hardware during Flight 15 but aborted the initial catch mid-test, sending the booster into the Gulf of Mexico rather than returning it to the launch tower. The failure underscores the engineering precision required to scale chopstick-arm recovery to Starship dimensions, where millisecond timing and wind shifts can defeat the manoeuvre. Major commercial satellite operators, defence contractors and NASA rely on Starship eventually hitting heavy-payload cost targets to make deep-space logistics commercially viable.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Until SpaceX demonstrates consistent booster recovery at Starship scale, the full reusability cost model underpinning its commercial and government launch pricing remains unvalidated, exposing customers and investors to schedule and cost uncertainty.

URGENT Global HIGH · 55
China antimony export ban suspension expires Nov 27
China suspension of its antimony export ban to the US is set to expire on November 27, 2026, returning supply security to the foreground for defence and energy sector buyers. Global antimony production in 2025 averaged 110,000 metric tons, with China accounting for 40,000 metric tons, or roughly 36.37 percent, while China along with Russia and Tajikistan controls about 90 percent of global antimony production. Antimony is classified as a critical mineral by the US Geological Survey, with flame retardants accounting for roughly 50 percent of global use and the photovoltaic sector identified as a key long-term demand driver.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Expiry of the suspension tightens the near-term supply chain for defence-relevant antimony applications including lead-acid batteries and flame retardants, increasing the investment case for domestic US antimony exploration and production assets.

Ukraine drones strike Russian corvette in Benghazi

NORMAL Europe CRITICAL · 88
UK awards Babcock £8B submarine maintenance deal
The UK Ministry of Defence awarded Babcock an £8 billion Gateway Agreement to transform submarine maintenance and delivery of critical naval base services, targeting faster return-to-sea times for Royal Navy submarines and directly strengthening the nuclear deterrent. The contract will create around 3,000 highly skilled roles including 900 jobs and 450 apprentice and graduate opportunities at His Majesty Naval Base Clyde, and is described as the second major multi-billion submarine-related deal signed since the defence investment plan. Babcock said the agreement, effective from 1 December, is the largest contract in its history and is worth £8.7 billion over five years.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
The £8 billion award to Babcock consolidates its position as the primary UK submarine sustainment prime and signals that the Ministry of Defence is prioritising deterrent readiness over procurement diversification, reducing near-term competitive opportunity for rival maintainers but creating a stable long-horizon revenue base for Babcock and its supply chain at Clyde.

URGENT Europe CRITICAL · 88
Five NATO nations jointly buy 430+ CV90 vehicles
Sweden, Finland, Norway, Lithuania and the Netherlands agreed to jointly purchase more than 430 CV90 infantry fighting vehicles manufactured by BAE Systems Hagglunds in what Finnish Defence Minister Antti Hakkanen described as the largest joint land defence purchase in NATO, with an outlay estimated to exceed 7.5 billion euros. The contract enters into force once all five nations sign a joint order simultaneously at a ceremony scheduled this Friday in Stockholm, with Finland already detailing its portion as 160 vehicles, with an option to acquire 100 more, for a value of 2.8 billion euros. Estonia signed a 2025 declaration of intent but subsequently withdrew from the initiative.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
A confirmed signing this Friday would lock in the largest pooled NATO land procurement on record, validating the Nordic Defence Cooperation model and signalling that European allies are prepared to execute large platform commitments without waiting for the EU budget impasse to resolve.

URGENT Middle East CRITICAL · 80
Pentagon delivers 25,000+ drone systems to CENTCOM
The US Department of War announced that more than 25,000 drone and counter-drone systems were delivered to US Central Command by the Pentagon Direct Reporting Portfolio Manager for Unmanned Systems, with more than 24,000 counter-unmanned aerial systems delivered specifically through Joint Interagency Task Force 401, described as capable of creating a multi-layered defensive shield. The scale of this delivery underlines that counter-UAS is now a volume procurement problem rather than a niche capability gap, favouring suppliers with established production throughput and logistics integration over emerging technology entrants without manufacturing scale.

URGENT Europe CRITICAL · 78
Russia hybrid sabotage on Europe rose in past year
The International Institute for Strategic Studies reported that sabotage attacks on European infrastructure rose by 16 percent in the 12 months leading up to September 2026, with arson and use of explosives roughly doubling over that period. Targeting of critical infrastructure including military-industrial and transport sites accounted for more than half of recorded incidents between October 2025 and September 2026, while a semi-skilled tier of operatives is now using more complex methods including improvised explosive devices with timed and remote detonators alongside unskilled recruits who carry out most operations. The IISS assessed that the campaign has failed in its objective of deterring European support for Ukraine, with military assistance to Ukraine from Western allies continuing to increase.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
The 16 percent rise and shift toward more sophisticated methods signals that European defence manufacturers, logistics nodes and transport infrastructure face a structurally elevated threat environment that will sustain procurement demand for physical security, detection and resilience capabilities well beyond the duration of active hostilities.

NORMAL Asia-Pacific HIGH · 55
QBE Asia: supplier concentration deepens cyber risk
QBE Asia published a report finding that Amazon Web Services, Microsoft Azure and Google Cloud accounted for 63 percent of enterprise cloud network spending in 2025, creating systemic risk where disruption at a single provider spreads across multiple companies and sectors. The report quantified downtime costs at an average of USD $15,000 per minute, with ransomware causing affected organisations an average of 24 days of direct operational downtime, and 60 percent of small companies targeted in cyber attacks forced to shut down operations within six months of an incident. The report also found that the industrialisation of artificial intelligence has cut the time between initial access and exploitation to 30 minutes, down from about 100 minutes in 2021.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Extreme cloud concentration among three providers represents a single-point-of-failure risk for defence supply chains and space operators that rely on commercial cloud infrastructure, making provider diversification and sovereign cloud capability a growing strategic imperative.

NORMAL Italy HIGH · 62
Italy claims 2.8% of GDP in defence spend for 2026
Italian officials told Defense News that Rome reached a combined defence and domestic security spend equal to 2.8 percent of GDP in 2026, fulfilling a commitment made at the July NATO summit, with core defence spending calculated at 2.1 percent and domestic security at 0.7 percent. Pure defence spending, labelled Funzione Difesa, was extrapolated from budget figures at 23.6 billion euros, up from 22.49 billion euros the year before, though the official budget document does not clearly state the totals. The NATO budget total is listed at 48.31 billion euros, equal to 2.1 percent of GDP, up from 2 percent in 2025.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Italys claimed achievement of the 2.8 percent threshold, if accepted by NATO, sets a precedent for how allies may structure and present blended security budgets to meet alliance targets under US pressure to reach 5 percent by 2035.

NORMAL France HIGH · 60
France launches counter-drone industry pact to 2027
France kicked off a new counter-drone pact with its defence industry, running to the end of summer 2027 and extending an existing drone pact to cover counter-drone capabilities, bringing together firms and military end users under the leadership of the Directorate General for Armament and industry association Gicat. France junior minister for the armed forces said the wars in Ukraine and the Middle East had been a quite brutal reality check and that French forces have an urgent need for counter-drone capabilities, adding that France has already faced problems related to having insufficient counter-drone capability. The new pact covers systems to counter unmanned aerial vehicles but also maritime and ground drones, and Gicat chairman Emmanuel Levacher said he expects most or all of the 200 partners in the drone pact to participate.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
The structured pact model, cutting procurement red tape and focusing on fast contract delivery, is becoming a replicable template across European militaries seeking to close counter-drone gaps without full programme timelines.
