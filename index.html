White House ordered Starshield limits for Ukraine
The order to restrict SpaceX Starshield satellite communications for the Ukrainian military came from inside the White House, according to two US officials and a Western official briefed on the matter, with Pentagon leaders receiving a message that the service needed to be slowed. Ukrainian officials said military-grade Starlink receptors stopped working when mounted on fast-moving objects, degrading drone guidance, though a person in Ukraines Defence Ministry said the receptors were restored but only functional at speeds up to 100 miles per hour.

SpaceX launches classified USSF-481 for Space Force
A SpaceX Falcon 9 rocket launched the classified USSF-481 mission, also referenced as TH-2, from Space Launch Complex 4 East at Vandenberg Space Force Base at 5:01 p.m. PDT on Thursday, with first-stage booster B1081 completing its 28th flight and landing on the droneship Of Course I Still Love You — the 228th landing on that vessel and the 671st Falcon booster landing overall. ◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Rapid cadence of classified Space Force missions on reused boosters confirms that Falcon 9 reliability is now the operational baseline for national security launch, compressing the competitive window for any rival vehicle seeking a share of that market.

Texas State student CubeSat reaches orbit on Falcon 9
Texas State University launched its first-ever satellite, a student-built CubeSat named PROVES-MAIA, aboard a SpaceX Falcon 9 rocket as part of a NASA resupply mission, with the satellite to be deployed from the International Space Station into Earths orbit in about three to four weeks. URGENT North America CRITICAL · 95
US missile defense shortfalls may persist until 2030
Ousted US Army Chief of Staff General Randy George warned in late December — two months before the conflict with Iran began — that American missile defense stockpiles were critically low and could take until 2030 to recover, with the assessment included in the Pentagons fiscal 2027 force allocation planning; Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth removed George in April while the war was under way. A Center for Strategic and International Studies estimate found that US forces entered 2026 with approximately 2,300 Patriot interceptors and expended between 1,050 and 1,430 during the conflict, while the prewar THAAD inventory of roughly 355 interceptors lost an estimated 190 to 290, with Mark Cancian of CSIS telling CBS News that these systems will be crucial in any potential conflict with China.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
A verified multi-year gap in Patriot and THAAD inventories, confirmed by CSIS modeling, makes interceptor replenishment the single most urgent and durable procurement priority in the US defense budget, directly benefiting Raytheon and Lockheed Martin production lines for years regardless of how the Iran conflict resolves.

URGENT Middle East CRITICAL · 88
Iran rebuilding underground missile sites, imagery shows
New satellite imagery showing renewed tunneling and underground construction at three major Iranian sites — Parchin, Khojir, and Shahroud — has raised concerns about resumed missile production, according to a report released by the Foreign Policy Research Institute, with experts noting the findings align with earlier reporting that Iran resumed assembling ballistic missiles in underground facilities using stockpiled components. All three sites remain heavily damaged with rubble largely uncleared, but the imagery suggests Iran may be shifting production and assembly underground to shield capabilities from further strikes.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Resumed Iranian underground missile assembly, even at damaged sites, extends the targeting problem for US and Israeli strike planners and sustains demand for deep-penetrating munitions and persistent ISR capabilities beyond the immediate conflict timeline.

NORMAL Asia-Pacific HIGH · 72
Hanwha lasers down three drones in South Korean army demo
Two laser weapons developed by Hanwha Systems — the stationary Cheongwang laser air defense weapon and a 30-kilowatt-class vehicle-mounted laser — shot down all three target drones during a South Korean Army demonstration on October 14 at the TIGER+ Future Warfare Integrated Combat Exercise, attended by approximately 500 people including Army Chief of Staff General Kim Kyu-ha. The demonstration followed a September 16 event where Cheongwang downed four drones and two larger unmanned aircraft in succession, with Hanwha describing Cheongwang as formally known as the Laser Air Defense Weapon Block-I.
