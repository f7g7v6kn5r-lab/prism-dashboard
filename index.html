BREAKING Iran / Strait of Hormuz CRITICAL · 95
Iran Misses US Deadline, Hormuz Standoff Deepens
The 60-day US-Iran memorandum of understanding -- signed in June and aimed at achieving a final deal within that window -- expired on Aug. 17 with no agreement reached and no mutually consented extension, per multiple wire sources as of Aug. 17-18. The MOU explicitly allowed for deadline extension by mutual consent, but Iran told Reuters it does not consider the 60-day period to have formally begun, and Iranian FM Araghchi said as of Aug. 16 that Tehran had not yet decided to restart direct negotiations with the US. Trump threatened to bomb Oman if it interfered with US efforts to reopen Hormuz, but Iran separately said a transit-route map has been broadly agreed with Oman, though a joint statement has not been finalised (CNN, Aug. 17). Qatar and Pakistan mediators remain active. The standoff is deadlocked, not collapsed into a declared war posture, as of Aug. 19.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
A prolonged Hormuz standoff without a diplomatic framework raises direct risk of naval confrontation in one of the worlds most critical energy and maritime chokepoints, with immediate implications for regional shipping insurance, naval asset deployment, and defence procurement urgency across Gulf states.
