Six months into the US-Iran conflict, the strategic picture is defined by simultaneous military exhaustion and accelerating defence recapitalisation — Patriot interceptor stocks in Europe are now described as beyond critical, the Pentagon is racing to field cheaper interceptors, and the USS Theodore Roosevelt is deploying to the Middle East for up to eight months, all while Boeing secures a $131 billion F-15 Eagle Crest contract covering production, upgrades and sustainment through 2037 that signals a decade-long commitment to legacy air power even as new-space and counter-drone investment surges in parallel. The tension is structural: the US is burning through high-value precision inventory faster than industrial capacity can replace it, yet the $131 billion Boeing contract locks spending into a platform generation that predates the drone-saturated threat environment now consuming those stocks in the Iran theatre, creating a window of genuine vulnerability that NATO planners are now openly discussing. Watch whether the Missile Defense Agencys low-cost interceptor programme, which awarded its first contract to X-Bow Systems in mid-August, attracts additional awardees in the near term — a competitive field signals that the acquisition system is responding to the inventory crisis at speed, while a sole-source outcome would confirm the supply constraint is deeper than the contracting posture can currently address.

URGENT North America CRITICAL · 90
Boeing wins $131B F-15 production and sustainment deal
The US Air Force has awarded Boeing a $131.23 billion ceiling indefinite-delivery/indefinite-quantity contract for the F-15 Eagle Crest programme, covering aircraft production, systems integration, modernisation, upgrades, retrofits and sustainment through 2037, with an ordering period running through 2031 and an option to extend to 2036, according to Breaking Defense and Shephard Media. A $131 billion production and sustainment ceiling through 2037 provides Boeing with a long-term revenue framework that partially offsets commercial aerospace volatility, but the scale of legacy spending it represents may crowd out investment in next-generation platforms at a moment when drone and hypersonic threats are reshaping the air combat environment.
