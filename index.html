URGENT Europe CRITICAL · 78
Isar Aerospace reaches orbit on second Spectrum flight
Isar Aerospace launched its Spectrum small-satellite rocket on its second mission from the Andoya Spaceport in Norway on September 5, achieving orbit after months of scrubs caused by a pressurization valve fault, weather, a composite overwrapped pressure vessel leak and fluid system issues. The mission, named Onward and Upward, flew to a Sun-synchronous orbit carrying five cubesats and one experiment, qualifying Spectrum under operational conditions for the first time.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
A confirmed orbital success from a European small-launch provider strengthens the case for sovereign launch diversity across NATO-aligned nations at a moment when Gulf conflict is concentrating attention on resilient space infrastructure.

The British Government Commercial Agency selected Oxfordshire-based Open Cosmos as a certified partner under the TacSys tactical communications framework to provide satellite connectivity via its ConnectedCosmos LEO constellation, with a contract estimated at up to £8 billion running to June 2034. Artemis III SLS stacking advances with booster segment work
Teams at NASAs Kennedy Space Center have stacked forward center segments on both five-segment SLS solid rocket boosters for Artemis III inside the Vehicle Assembly Building, while all four RS-25 engines have been installed on Core Stage-3, opening remaining outfitting work before the stage can be transferred onto Mobile Launcher 1. NORMAL North America HIGH · 55
New Glenn blast reached instruments 1,046 miles away
Researchers publishing in The Seismic Record found that Blue Origins New Glenn explosion during a May 29 hotfire test at Cape Canaveral Space Force Station produced energy equivalent to 0.131 kilotons, or roughly 144 US tons of TNT, detected by at least 36 infrasound stations across the United States. People near Cape Canaveral reported a powerful shockwave reaching areas up to 135 miles from the launchpad, and the explosion was recorded as one of the most powerful rocket test failures before launch on record.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
The scientific characterisation of the blast scale underscores the infrastructure and safety risk inherent in Blue Origins Cape Canaveral expansion program at a moment when the company is simultaneously building a new Merritt Island test site and rebuilding Launch Complex 36.

URGENT North America CRITICAL · 88
US Army awards AeroVironment $464.8M laser production contract
The US Army awarded AeroVironment a $464.8 million production contract under an Other Transaction Agreement for the Enduring-High Energy Laser program, marking the first directed energy production contract in US military history, with the system delivering dozens of 30 kW LOCUST laser weapons capable of engaging Group 1 through Group 3 unmanned aerial systems. Vice Chief of Staff of the Army General Christopher LaNeve described the award as enabling formations to defeat unmanned systems from the outset, following disappointing operational feedback on the earlier DE M-SHORAD prototypes in the Middle East.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
The first directed energy production contract in US military history sets a commercial and regulatory precedent that should accelerate follow-on laser programs across all services and positions AeroVironment as the reference contractor for counter-UAS directed energy at scale.

EU scraps Kallas defence panel after Berlin, Paris, Rome veto
