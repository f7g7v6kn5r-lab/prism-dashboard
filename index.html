BREAKING North America CRITICAL · 95
Hackers breach Air Force Cloud One platform
Hackers breached the US Air Forces Cloud One cloud computing platform, with the intrusion discovered within the past month during a system upgrade, three people familiar with the matter told Reuters. Cloud One, initiated in 2017, hosts applications including Cloud-Based Command and Control and the Theater Integrated Combat Munitions System, which tracks bombs and missiles across more than 200 US Air Force locations worldwide. Leidos is the current prime contractor, having taken over from SAIC in 2025, and investigators have called in Cyber Command to assess the damage.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
A confirmed breach of the platform managing air-defense data feeds and munitions tracking across more than 200 locations materially raises cyber-resilience risk for every programme that depends on Cloud One and creates an urgent procurement signal for hardened, redundant battle-management architecture.

NORMAL Global HIGH · 60
Governments invested $71.8B in space defence in 2025
Novaspace research published in November 2026 found that governments invested approximately $71.8 billion in space defence and security activities globally in 2025, with the United States, China, Russia, Japan and France accounting for approximately 90 percent of that total. Of the $71.8 billion invested globally, approximately $33.6 billion was contracted to industry, with secure satellite communications, counterspace and intelligence, surveillance and reconnaissance representing the largest areas of government procurement. Novaspace Senior Consultant Rachel Hughes noted that space is increasingly treated as an operationally contested domain rather than a support layer for operations on Earth.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
The concentration of 90 percent of global space defence spend among five nations creates a durable structural advantage for primes and suppliers embedded in those procurement chains, while leaving allied smaller nations dependent on partners for contested-space survivability.

URGENT North America HIGH · 55
SpaceX Starship Flight 15 booster lands in Gulf
SpaceX Starship Flight 15 double-catch attempt failed after the initial booster catch was aborted, with the booster landing in the Gulf of Mexico rather than returning to the launch tower. The mission highlights the engineering complexity of scaling mechanical tower-catch operations to Starships mass, where millisecond timing and wind conditions determine success. Major commercial satellite operators, defence contractors and NASA rely on Starship eventually hitting heavy-payload cost targets to make deep-space logistics commercially viable.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Repeated booster-recovery setbacks at Starship scale extend the timeline for the fully reusable heavy-lift architecture that underpins both commercial and government launch cost assumptions, increasing near-term reliance on existing vehicles and strengthening the case for alternative launch providers.

NORMAL North America HIGH · 50
Firefly stock falls as Needham sets $35 target
Firefly Aerospace shares fell 11 percent over five trading days to $18.14 at Wednesdays close, extending a year-to-date decline of 19 percent, even as Needham initiated coverage on November 25 with a Buy rating and a $35 price target. Needham described Firefly as a rapidly maturing space ecosystem company well positioned to fill large supply gaps in the launch market, citing proven performance in small- and medium-lift launches and lunar landers. The divergence between a bullish analyst initiation and a sustained price decline signals that institutional investors are discounting Fireflys near-term revenue path more aggressively than Needhams medium-term thesis, creating a potential entry point for investors with longer holding horizons if launch cadence data confirms the supply-gap argument.

URGENT Middle East CRITICAL · 95
Iran denies drone strike killing two Italians in Kuwait
Several drones described by Italian Foreign Minister Antonio Tajani as being of Iranian origin struck Kuwaiti military installations including an air defence detection station, killing two Italian technicians employed by Rome-based defence company Rheinmetall Italia. Iran denied responsibility through Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi, who called the accusations baseless and proposed a joint investigation with Kuwait and Italy, writing on X that the incident looked like a pre-cooked recipe for war. If attribution is confirmed through the proposed joint investigation, the killing of allied contractor personnel inside a Gulf state marks a qualitative escalation of the Iran war that will force a reassessment of force-protection requirements and air-defence coverage for civilian defence contractors operating across the region.

URGENT Europe CRITICAL · 90
Starshield speed curbs hit Ukraine strike drones
Speed restrictions on the US-provided Starshield satellite communications system are hampering Ukrainian strike drone operations over Russian-occupied territory, forcing drone losses and mission cancellations, two Ukrainian military sources told the Kyiv Independent on November 27. The restrictions, introduced in October, apply only to territory Ukraine temporarily lost to Russia and have affected medium-range strike drones including the Fire Point FP-2, which carries a payload of up to 200 kilograms and has a range of up to 700 kilometres. President Volodymyr Zelensky confirmed in an interview with the BBC published on November 26 that restrictions on Starlink and its military-grade service Starshield remained in place in Russian-occupied areas of Ukraine.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Politically imposed performance limits on a commercially derived satellite communications system create a direct operational precedent: allied dependence on US-controlled space infrastructure now carries mission-level risk that adversaries can exploit if restrictions are anticipated or detected.

NORMAL Europe CRITICAL · 80
Analysis puts Russian war dead above 720,000
Analysis of leaked data from Russias Rosstat statistical bureau by opposition outlets Mediazona and Meduza estimates that Russian fatalities in Ukraine jumped from 490,000 at the end of September to more than 720,000 since February 2022, a figure that equates to roughly one death every four minutes. The Kremlin has not officially updated its death toll since early in the war, when then-Defence Minister Sergei Shoigu acknowledged nearly 6,000 dead. The estimate does not include the number of soldiers wounded.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Fatality figures at this scale, if credible, indicate a Russian military manpower constraint that will shape Moscows operational tempo in Ukraine and its appetite for escalation elsewhere, relevant for investors pricing long-duration European defence procurement and Eastern flank basing decisions.

URGENT Middle East CRITICAL · 80
Mossad and Israeli military split over Iran strike
Mossad chief Roman Gofman and military Chief of Staff Eyal Zamir are at odds over whether to launch a further strike against Iran, with Gofman favouring a unilateral Israeli attack if no alternative remains and Zamir preferring to await a decision from US President Donald Trump, according to a report published jointly by Ynet and Yedioth Ahronoth. Gofmans position is shaped by concerns that Iran may be secretly continuing to develop chemical weapons, while Zamir judges the threat not yet imminent and believes US participation would provide a significant force multiplier. A security source said the militarys position is that Israel would attack without US involvement only if an immediate existential threat emerged.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
A visible internal split between Mossad and the Israeli military over strike timing introduces near-term uncertainty into Iranian escalation risk and raises the probability of uncoordinated Israeli action that could widen the conflict before US strategic positioning is set.

NORMAL Europe HIGH · 70
France launches counter-drone industry pact
France launched a new counter-drone pact with its defence industry, running to the end of summer 2027, adding a counter-drone dimension to its existing drone pact led by the Directorate General for Armament together with industry association Gicat. Junior minister for the armed forces Alice Rufo said French forces have an urgent need for counter-drone capabilities, describing the wars in Ukraine and the Middle East as a quite brutal reality check, and noted France has already faced problems related to having insufficient counter-drone capability. Gicat chairman Emmanuel Levacher said he expects most or all of the 200 partners in the drone pact to participate in the counter-drone initiative.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Frances institutionalised industry-military pact model, now covering both drone and counter-drone segments, compresses the procurement cycle and directs near-term contract flow toward French-sovereign suppliers including those under Gicat, reducing acquisition timeline risk for investors in the domestic counter-UAS sector.

Lebanon charges three Hezbollah members over arms
Lebanons Military Court commissioner Judge Claude Ghanem charged three Hezbollah members with forming armed groups without state consent and equipping them with individual and medium-range weapons following the detention of five men in the Jurd Al-Batroun and Bsharri areas of northern Lebanon on November 10. NORMAL United Arab Emirates HIGH · 55
Somalia cuts ties with UAE, third Arab state to do so
Somalia became the third African Arab League member to downgrade or sever ties with the UAE in the period covered, ordering the UAE ambassador to leave within 72 hours on November 19, following Algeria in September and Sudan in May 2025. The UAE faces growing backlash over its role in African conflicts, particularly the war in Sudan, where a UN fact-finding mission said there was credible evidence that individuals and entities linked to the UAE played a significant role in networks providing military-related support for the Rapid Support Forces. NORMAL Israel HIGH · 65
Israel fast-tracks data centres for AI ambitions
Israel is cutting red tape and accelerating data centre construction as part of a government plan announced in June, with Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu declaring the ambition to turn Israel into a global AI superpower and stating in September that within five years Israel will be the third AI power. Nvidias Israeli operations, its largest research and development presence outside the US with 6,000 employees across seven centres, underpin the effort, with Nvidia planning a major research and development campus in Kiryat Tivon near Haifa to accommodate up to 10,000 employees. The planned Ofek Data Center north of Ashdod is expected to cost $1.5 billion initially, with an additional $5 billion planned for future upgrades, and will launch with a capacity of 130MW before expanding to 200MW.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Israels state-backed acceleration of AI data centre infrastructure with deep Nvidia integration positions it as a node in the US-aligned AI supply chain with direct implications for military AI processing capacity and dual-use technology export controls.

NORMAL Italy CRITICAL · 75
Italy claims 2.8 percent of GDP on defence in 2026
Italian officials told Defense News that Italy reached a combined defence and domestic security spend equal to 2.8 percent of GDP in 2026, fulfilling a commitment made at the July NATO summit, with 2.1 percent on core defence and 0.7 percent on domestic security. The figures are not explicitly stated in Italys 2026 budget document, but core defence spending can be extrapolated as 23.6 billion euros, up from 22.49 billion euros the previous year. The domestic security spending equivalent to 0.7 percent of GDP is handled outside the defence budget and covers activities including frontier control, combatting terrorism, protecting energy infrastructure and crisis preparation.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Italy meeting its self-declared 2.8 percent target strengthens the political case for continued procurement acceleration, with €9.33 billion allocated to investment and procurement, representing a growing pipeline for European and US defence suppliers.
