SpaceX Super Heavy B21 moves to pad for orbital launch
SpaceX placed Super Heavy booster B21, standing 236 feet tall with a 9-metre diameter, on the launch mount at Pad 2 at Starbase in Texas, targeting a launch no earlier than 28 September at 7:15 a.m. CDT pending a Federal Aviation Administration licence modification. NORMAL Global HIGH · 50
Progress M-25 docks at ISS after delayed Soyuz launch
The unpiloted Russian Progress M-25 cargo ship docked at the space-facing port of the Poisk module at 9:44 a.m. EDT on Saturday, completing a three-day rendezvous after lifting off from Baikonur Cosmodrome on 16 September following a one-week delay caused by an undisclosed technical issue with the Soyuz-2-1b rockets third stage. URGENT North America CRITICAL · 95
Patriot stocks fall to 759-827 as Pentagon keeps production data private
A Center for Strategic and International Studies analysis found US Patriot interceptor stocks fell from roughly 2,330 before the Iran war to between 759 and 827 by end of July, while THAAD stocks fell from 452 to between 234 and 278, with production lead times of approximately 42 months for PAC-3 MSE and 53 months for THAAD interceptors. Deputy Defense Secretary Steve Feinbergs 5 August memo asked industry to propose faster delivery schedules for 16 critical programmes by 26 August, but the Pentagon has not publicly disclosed how many companies responded or what timelines were offered.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
With interceptor stocks at a fraction of pre-war levels and lead times exceeding three years, the US cannot rebuild air-defence depth at operational tempo without immediate production investment, creating urgent procurement pressure that directly benefits Lockheed Martin and RTX and should be a primary focus for defence investors monitoring the fiscal 2028 budget request.

URGENT North America CRITICAL · 85
US Air Force reports $14B parts supply gap affecting readiness
Commander of Air Force Materiel Command Lt. Gen. Linda Hurry told the Air and Space Forces Association conference that the US Air Force has a $14 billion hole in parts supply, with the supply-driven aircraft grounding rate up 87 percent over seven years and roughly 53 percent of the fleet over 30 years old. Approximately 55 percent of the fleet has exceeded its useful service life, and the service is managing shortfalls through partnerships with the Defense Logistics Agency and depot-level sourcing.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
An $14 billion parts gap concentrated in an aging fleet under active operational pressure from the Iran war creates a sustained aftermarket and sustainment revenue opportunity for prime contractors and MRO specialists, while signalling that readiness risk is structural rather than cyclical.
