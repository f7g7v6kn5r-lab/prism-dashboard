CENTCOM: Hormuz strikes not effective
CENTCOM commander Admiral Brad Cooper privately advised Trump and senior officials that the Hormuz bombing campaign had reached the limit of its effectiveness because most designated targets had already been struck, and that further limited strikes would produce diminishing returns absent a decision to resume major combat operations targeting the remaining approximately 20 percent of Operation Epic Fury objectives. That assessment, reported by Axios on 27 July 2026, contributed to Trumps decision that day to pause -- not end -- the 13-day bombing campaign. The framing in the original brief that CENTCOM publicly stated strikes are not effective overstates the formality: the recommendation was privately delivered to the Pentagon, Joint Chiefs and White House; CENTCOM spokesman Captain Tim Hawkins declined to comment on it. A separate factor cited by multiple outlets was concern over dwindling US stocks of air-defence interceptors.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
The CENTCOM-White House divergence on Hormuz effectiveness is a critical strategic signal for energy market participants and Gulf-based operators, as it suggests the strait remains a genuine vulnerability rather than a resolved chokepoint, sustaining elevated risk premiums across energy and regional logistics.

Iran Rejects Hormuz Deal as Oil Spill Hits Oman
Iran has dismissed Trumps claim of total control over the Strait of Hormuz, stating as of 13 August 2026 that the waterway will remain closed until the US changes its behaviour and accepts Tehrans conditions, which include lifting the naval blockade, withdrawing forces and paying compensation. The situation is a stalemate rather than a formal deal rejection: Iran and Oman have separately been negotiating shipping-route arrangements, with Iran confirming on 5 August 2026 that geographical coordinates for a proposed safe route had been agreed, though Tehran has made clear any such arrangement would not reopen the strait to US-aligned traffic without broader US concessions. Mutual compensation demands added by both sides as of 10-11 August 2026 have further diminished near-term deal prospects. On the oil spill: Omans Environment Authority confirmed as of 12 August 2026 that crude oil from the sanctioned Suezmax tanker Caroline Bezengi -- a shadow-fleet vessel carrying Russian crude that grounded on rocks near the Hallaniyat Islands after an explosion in June -- has reached beaches on Omans mainland at Ras Madrakah; the slick had grown to approximately 390-400 square kilometres by 10 August 2026 per Windward satellite data. The vessel is not described in current sourcing as a stricken tanker from a recent incident but as one that has been breaking up and leaking since late June 2026.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Continued closure of Hormuz and spreading environmental damage signal a prolonged disruption to global energy supply chains, directly elevating risk premiums for defence contractors and satellite operators supporting Gulf maritime security.
