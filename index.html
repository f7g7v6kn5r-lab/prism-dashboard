URGENT Global HIGH · 65
Cosmos 2612 spent 24 hours near ICEYE radar satellite
The United States told a United Nations committee on 21 October that Russian military satellite Cosmos 2612 conducted manoeuvres near ICEYE-X36, a US-registered radar imaging satellite operated by ICEYE US, which also supplies radar imagery to Ukraines military, in a pattern the US said appeared consistent with an on-orbit weapons operation. Space-tracking firm COMSPOC estimated Cosmos 2612 spent about 24 hours within 2 kilometres of ICEYE-X36 between 16 and 18 October, with the closest pass estimated at about 60 meters. Russia rejected the accusation during the same UN debate, with a Russian representative describing the US and Finnish statements as unfounded and saying Russias space activities were peaceful.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Deliberate proximity operations against a commercially operated intelligence satellite supplying a combatant in an active conflict establish a precedent that commercial imagery providers face direct on-orbit threat, sharpening the case for in-orbit situational awareness and debris-avoidance investment.

NORMAL North America HIGH · 52
Redwire wins $15M AscendArc solar contract
Redwire announced a $15 million contract to supply its Extensible Low-Profile Solar Array wings to AscendArc for a geosynchronous communications satellite, adding to earlier November wins including a $20 million DARPA contract under the Otter programme and a $40 million NASA Moon Base solar power demonstration award. ◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Three contract wins in a single month across defence, civil and commercial customers demonstrate that Redwires solar array product line is gaining cross-sector traction, but the persistent year-to-date decline suggests investors are discounting near-term profitability despite rising order flow.

NORMAL North America MEDIUM · 45
Four advanced reactors reach criticality by July 4
President Trump signed an executive order setting a goal of at least three new privately built advanced reactors reaching criticality by 4 July 2026, and that goal was exceeded, with Antares Nuclear achieving criticality on 4 June, followed by Valar Atomics and Deployable Energy later that month, and Aalo Atomics on 4 July bringing the total to four. In Michigan, Holtec began loading fuel back into the Palisades reactor on 30 August, preparing to restore more than 800 megawatts of generating capacity, with the plant expected to return to service later this year or early 2027. The Department of Energy has cut its reactor-authorization process from 17 steps to 11 and eliminated more than 900 pages of guidance as part of the broader reform effort.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Restored nuclear baseload capacity directly supports the energy-intensive ground infrastructure required for large satellite constellation operations and AI-driven space situational awareness systems, reducing a key constraint on long-term sector expansion.

URGENT North America CRITICAL · 92
US expended up to two-thirds of key missiles in Iran campaign
A Center for Strategic and International Studies analysis of munitions expenditures current as of 3 November found that the United States expended between a third and two-thirds of key munition inventories in the 39-day campaign against Iran until the 8 April ceasefire and subsequent operations, with seven of nine missile categories assessed as having detrimental resupply capacity. Patriot missiles, THAAD missiles and Standard Missiles are listed as heavily used with no substitutes, while long-range ground-attack missiles including Tomahawks, AGM-158 JASSM cruise missiles and Precision Strike Missiles are described as heavily used with some substitutes. The CSIS report states the US has enough munitions to renew the fight against Iran but that against China there is great concern, and rebuilding inventories will require a multiyear commitment extending beyond the current crisis.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
The documented inventory gap between current stockpiles and a two-front deterrence posture is the single most consequential structural signal for defence procurement budgets in this edition, and any further Iran escalation before restocking compresses the deterrence window against China to a degree that should accelerate allied missile production investment immediately.

URGENT North America CRITICAL · 82
Pentagon lacks plan linking munitions to requirements
A Government Accountability Office report, the public version of a classified study completed in September 2026, warned that the Department of Defense does not have an overarching plan linking munitions production and funding to requirements, with Pentagon officials acknowledging they can be reactive to global events rather than driven by total requirements. The report cited a new Texas plant costing nearly $500 million that failed to produce any shell components as one example of the mismatch, and found that the Army ramp-up in production of 155-millimetre artillery shells was not driven by a need to address the total munitions requirement. GAO recommended that DoD create an overarching plan to match munitions requirements, production and funding, as well as metrics to track progress.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
The GAO finding, combined with the CSIS inventory depletion analysis on the same day, confirms that the US munitions industrial base faces a governance failure at the planning level, not merely a funding shortfall, which means additional appropriations alone will not close the gap on the timeline that current operational tempo demands.

NORMAL Europe CRITICAL · 75
Netherlands targets €15B defence industry revenue by 2030
The Netherlands presented a defence-industrial plan targeting 40 percent growth in its defence technological and industrial base by 2030 and revenue of approximately €15 billion, with €1.6 billion earmarked by the government for the plan, focusing on integrated air and missile defence, unmanned and autonomous systems, data-driven command and digital resilience, and military sustainment. Spending on the plan will include €600 million for industrial ecosystems and scaling production, €320 million for international cooperation and €150 million on supply chain resilience. The Netherlands is the seventh-largest defence spender among European NATO countries, and the plan notes the country is home to Destinus, whose Ruta cruise missiles were used by Ukraine to target the Kerch Strait bridge.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
The combination of a named government funding commitment, an operational combat debut for a Dutch-made precision weapon, and a 40 percent growth target makes the Dutch defence-industrial base one of the most concentrated near-term European investment plays on the page today.

NORMAL Lebanon HIGH · 60
Lebanon disarmament and Israeli presence at impasse
Israeli Defense Minister Israel Katz signalled Israel will not withdraw from its current positions in southern Lebanon until Hezbollah is disarmed, Hamas is dismantled and threats from Syria are eliminated, while Hezbollah has refused to disarm, describing such an arrangement as surrender. NORMAL China CRITICAL · 80
Trump and Xi agree humans must control military AI
US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said President Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping agreed that humans must always be involved in military decisions that use artificial intelligence and that AI should not be allowed to act autonomously on major military actions. Bessent, speaking to CNBC after returning from Trumps meeting with Xi in Shenzhen, described the agreement as among the major achievements of the trip and said it would not be undermined by the two countries competition for AI supremacy. Bessent also said the H200 remains the most advanced chip China can purchase with licenses and that China is probably many years behind in building its own chip stack.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
A US-China bilateral commitment on human-in-the-loop requirements for military AI sets a de facto international baseline that will shape how defence AI developers design autonomy guardrails and seek export approvals across allied markets.

NORMAL Germany HIGH · 73
Germany clears two loitering munitions in live-fire trials
Starks Virtus and Helsings HX-2 loitering munitions both passed live-fire trials held the week of 9 November at the Bundeswehr weapons and ammunition test centre in Meppen, Germanys military procurement office said, with photos showing both systems moments before impact into a tank. NORMAL Denmark / Greenland HIGH · 68
Denmark ratifies Greenland security deal with US
Denmarks parliament unanimously ratified a security agreement with the United States on Greenland, expanding the US military role including permission to establish a Golden Dome missile defence system while reaffirming Danish sovereignty and Greenlanders right to self-determination. The US, Denmark and Greenland had signed the agreement in September after Trump repeatedly threatened to take control of the territory, and work will now begin on a new Greenland investor-screening law and plans for two new US military bases as outlined in the agreement. Denmarks Defence Minister Jeppe Bruus said he expects the US will now start to be more concrete on the purposes and arrangements around the two areas.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Ratification opens concrete procurement and basing investment in one of the most strategically contested Arctic locations, with Golden Dome infrastructure and two new bases creating near-term programme opportunities for missile defence and construction primes.
