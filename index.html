Roman telescope mission extended to 22 years after launch
NASA announced the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope has sufficient fuel for at least 22 years of science operations, more than doubling the original 10-year plan of a five-year primary mission followed by a five-year extension. The extended lifetime results from a precise Falcon Heavy launch on August 30, 2026, an accurate low-Earth orbit departure burn, and the spacecraft coming in lighter than its design maximum, leaving more propellant than budgeted.

BREAKING North America CRITICAL · 98
CBO: Iran war cost $38B and drained two-thirds of US interceptors
A Congressional Budget Office analysis released Tuesday estimated total US military costs from combat operations against Iran at $38 billion through August 1, and found the conflict had consumed between one-half and two-thirds of the combined US inventory of Patriot, THAAD, Standard Missile-3 and Standard Missile-6 interceptors since June 2025. The CBO assessed that rebuilding those stocks could take at least five years even if production rates increase, and identified $13.1 billion in interceptors and $7.3 billion in land-attack cruise missiles among the $21.7 billion in munitions the Pentagon would need to replace.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
A five-year minimum interceptor rebuild timeline at current production rates creates a calculable window of reduced US missile-defense capacity that directly threatens allied assurance commitments in both the Middle East and the Indo-Pacific, making accelerated interceptor production contracts the single most consequential near-term procurement signal for defence investors.
