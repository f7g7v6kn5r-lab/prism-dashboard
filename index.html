SpaceX launches classified USSF-317 for Space Force
A SpaceX Falcon 9 lifted off from Space Launch Complex 4 East at Vandenberg Space Force Base at 8:20 p.m. Pacific Standard Time on November 20, 2026, carrying the classified USSF-317 payload for the US Space Force as the sixth mission in a series that began in August. The first stage, designated B1100 on its 11th flight, landed on the droneship Of Course I Still Love You in the Pacific Ocean. The Space Force has kept the number of spacecraft aboard and their purpose classified, though each of the five previous launches in the series resulted in 23 cataloged satellites.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
The sustained pace of classified Space Force constellation builds — six missions in roughly three months — demonstrates that Pentagon reliance on rapid Falcon 9 cadence for military satellite replenishment is structural rather than opportunistic, creating durable launch demand that is insulated from commercial market volatility.

Trump and Xi agree humans must control military AI
US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent confirmed after the Trump-Xi summit in Shenzhen that both presidents agreed humans must always be involved in military decisions using what the Trump administration terms super intelligence, and that autonomous AI should not be permitted to launch major military actions. Middle East cable routes challenge Egypts data dominance
New communications corridors being developed through Syria and Iraq are challenging Egypts traditional dominance of data traffic between Asia and Europe, with Saudi telecommunications group STC winning the SilkLink project in Syria valued at approximately $800 million and a separate WorldLink initiative aiming to connect the UAE to Turkey through Iraq with a planned investment of approximately $700 million. URGENT North America CRITICAL · 90
US missile stocks critically depleted after Iran campaign
A Center for Strategic and International Studies analysis found the US expended between a third and two-thirds of key munition inventories in the 39-day Iran campaign through the April ceasefire and subsequent operations, with seven of nine missile categories assessed as having detrimental resupply capacity. Patriot missiles, Terminal High Altitude Area Defense missiles and Standard Missiles are listed as heavily used with no substitutes, while long-range ground-attack missiles including Tomahawks, AGM-158 JASSM cruise missiles and Precision Strike Missiles are described as heavily used with some substitutes. The report concludes that while the US retains enough munitions to renew the fight against Iran, a conflict with China would face great concern, and rebuilding inventories will require a multiyear commitment extending beyond the current crisis.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
The documented depletion of Patriot, THAAD and Standard Missile stockpiles — the same systems now under sustained attack pressure in Bahrain — means accelerated production contracts for US missile manufacturers are a near-certainty, but the multiyear rebuild timeline exposes a window of genuine deterrence degradation that adversaries can observe and exploit.

URGENT Middle East CRITICAL · 85
Saudi Arabia seeks interceptors beyond US suppliers
Facing persistent missile and drone attacks and drained interceptor stockpiles, Saudi Arabia signed a $3.2 billion missile defense deal with South Korea prior to the Iran war and subsequently signed a defense deal with Ukraine for low-cost counter-drone systems after a ceasefire and the resumption of Houthi fighting in July. The kingdom has also sought defense network assistance from Greece, Pakistan, Turkey, the United Kingdom and Egypt, while Saudi-based startup SR2 Defense Systems forged a partnership with Utah-based Vector to produce attritable systems domestically near Riyadh. Analysts estimate that by July the kingdom had expended 86 percent of its interceptor inventory, with Lockheed Martin having received a reported $58 billion contract with Saudi Arabia to triple PAC-3 production capacity by the end of 2030 to more than 2,000 units.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Riyadhs deliberate bypass of exclusive US procurement dependency — anchored by a $3.2 billion South Korea deal and domestic drone production — signals a structural shift in Gulf arms sourcing that creates exportable market share for non-US interceptor and counter-UAV suppliers willing to offer competitive pricing and technology transfer.

A Pentagon fact sheet accused Canada of falling short as a defence partner, citing only 41.9 percent serviceability of the Royal Canadian Air Force fleet and 53.9 percent of the naval fleet over 2025 to 2026, with the Canadian Army posting an operational readiness rate of 48 percent over the same period. The restrictions now require prior Pentagon approval for US military personnel to interact with Canadian counterparts at official events, though defence and intelligence sharing remain unaffected. Canada is already reconsidering billions of dollars in planned purchases of American weapons, and US policymakers say Ottawa has not yet explained how it will pay for its stated target of reaching 5 percent of its economy on defence by 2035.

NORMAL Germany HIGH · 57
German attack drone variants pass live-fire trials
Two German one-way attack drone variants, Starks Virtus and Helsings HX-2, passed live-fire trials held the week of November 9 at the Bundeswehr weapons and ammunition test centre in Meppen, with testing focused on the approach-to-target and warhead effect phases rather than full service certification. The procurement office announced both systems passed but did not disclose how many shots were fired or the weapons success rates, with photos showing both an OWE-V Virtus and an HX-2 loitering munition moments before impact into a tank. The systems have not yet completed full qualification; the German Army plans to begin equipping its 45th Armored Brigade in Lithuania in 2027, with contracts worth 268 million euros each approved for Stark and Helsing by the parliamentary budget committee in February.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Successful live-fire trials for two distinct loitering munition designs give Germany credible near-term delivery capability and validate a human-in-the-loop autonomous attack model that NATO allies and procurement offices will closely track.

NORMAL Netherlands HIGH · 65
Netherlands targets 40 percent defence industry growth by 2030
The Netherlands has presented a defence-industrial plan targeting 40 percent growth in its defence technological and industrial base by 2030, with domestic defence industry revenue aimed at around 15 billion euros, and the government earmarking 1.6 billion euros for the plan. The plan focuses on integrated air and missile defence, data-driven command and digital resilience, unmanned and autonomous systems and military sustainment, with 600 million euros allocated for industrial ecosystems and scaling production, 320 million euros for international cooperation and 150 million euros on supply chain resilience. The government plans to establish the National Institute for Defence Technology in 2028 with a budget climbing to 350 million euros a year by 2030, and will set up a 200 million euro investment fund for dual-use companies in defence and security together with Invest-NL.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
A government-backed 1.6 billion euro industrial investment programme from the seventh-largest European NATO defence spender signals a structurally expanding procurement pipeline for primes and suppliers with radar, autonomous systems and sustainment capabilities.
