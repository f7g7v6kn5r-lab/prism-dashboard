URGENT North America CRITICAL · 85
Vulcan fails to deliver 40 Amazon satellites
United Launch Alliances Vulcan rocket suffered an anomaly about 10 minutes into its Nov. 15 flight from Space Launch Complex 41 at Cape Canaveral Space Force Station, failing to deliver 40 Amazon Leo broadband satellites to orbit in what ULA acknowledged as a failure to meet its mission-success standard. The launch, called Vulcan Leo 1 or LV-01, had already slipped twice — once after a gaseous nitrogen plant failure and once due to network problems between the rocket and ground systems — and was intended to demonstrate Vulcan was ready to operate again after nine months on the ground. ULA has not named a failed component or announced a return-to-flight date, and Amazon must wait for ULAs investigation before using Vulcan again.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Amazon LEOs first-generation constellation now faces a significant coverage and schedule setback, strengthening the competitive position of rival launch providers and raising questions about ULAs reliability as a government and commercial launch partner at a moment when assured access to orbit is a strategic imperative.

NORMAL North America CRITICAL · 80
SpaceX catches Ship 42 after orbital flight
SpaceX caught Starship upper stage Ship 42 at the Pad 2 tower at Starbase, Texas on Nov. 14 at 3:20 p.m. CST, after the vehicle completed approximately 14 orbits during a flight of roughly 22.5 hours that also deployed 26 Starlink V3 satellites. Super Heavy booster Booster 22 called off its own tower catch and was destroyed just above the Gulf of Mexico after ending its boostback burn early following a low fuel mass alarm, with SpaceX still investigating the root cause. NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman reacted to the flight by writing Making more believers by the day, noting NASA looked forward to using Starships on-orbit capability on Artemis III.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Reusable upper-stage recovery is central to the cost economics underpinning both SpaceXs commercial launch pricing and the Starship lunar lander NASA plans to use for Artemis III, so this partial success materially de-risks the programme timeline even as the booster failure keeps full reusability out of reach.

NORMAL Europe CRITICAL · 75
Iceye makes €2B cash offer for Avios Vega rocket maker
Finnish satellite operator Iceye made a €2 billion cash offer for Italian rocket maker Avio, pledging a €1 billion investment in Italy over five years to expand Avios Vega rocket customer base and placing Iceye in charge of Avios expansion into the US missile business. The offer, made through wholly owned Italian subsidiary Iris 2 at €41 per share, represents a 21.3 percent premium to Avios closing price on Monday and a 34.8 percent premium to its three-month volume-weighted average price. Avios board said it had started assessing the bid, while the CEO of Leonardo, which holds an 18 percent stake in Avio, said he was pleasantly surprised and would consider it; US and Italian government approval would be required in the event of a takeover.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
A successful acquisition would create one of Europes first vertically integrated intelligence-to-launch companies and directly challenge Europes dependence on non-sovereign launch providers, with significant implications for EU defence procurement strategy and for competitors in the small-to-medium launch market.

NORMAL North America HIGH · 60
SpaceNukes opens Los Alamos materials facility
Space Nuclear Power Corp opened its Manufacture and Materials Development Facility at 150 Central Park Square in Los Alamos on Nov. 13, dedicating it to additive manufacturing of nickel-based and refractory alloys including tungsten, molybdenum, tantalum, rhenium and niobium for its Generation 2 space reactor. CEO Andrew Phelps described the programme as primarily a materials and manufacturing challenge, noting that raising reactor operating temperature is the key to reducing radiator mass in space fission power systems, and that the underlying nuclear physics was proven in 2018. The facility is led by Omar Mireles, who spent 13 years as a research engineer at NASAs Marshall Space Flight Center and led the Refractory Alloy Additive Manufacturing Build Optimization project there.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Sovereign space nuclear power manufacturing capacity at Los Alamos positions SpaceNukes ahead of a coming inflection point in high-power satellite and deep-space mission demand, with refractory alloy additive manufacturing know-how representing a dual-use technology barrier that few competitors can clear quickly.

URGENT Europe CRITICAL · 85
EU proposes €45B Ukraine loan tied to reparations
The European Commission proposed a new €45 billion loan to Ukraine to help fill a projected €70 billion funding gap for next year, with European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen stating Ukraine would only need to repay the loan after Russia had paid reparations for damage caused by the war. Up to €25 billion of the proposed loan could be spent on military aid and up to €20 billion on general budget support, with the Commission planning to borrow on international markets on behalf of the EU to finance the instrument, subject to approval from member states. ◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Conditional repayment tied to Russian reparations effectively converts this into a long-duration quasi-grant, deepening Europes financial commitment to Ukraines military sustainment and signalling that EU member governments expect the conflict to extend well beyond any near-term ceasefire.

NORMAL North America CRITICAL · 80
Pentagon fails ninth audit as IG flags fraud risk
Days after the Pentagon failed its ninth consecutive audit, the Defense Department Inspector General released its annual report identifying seven top strategic challenges for the upcoming fiscal year, with fraud elimination and achieving a clean financial audit listed alongside deterring China and energising the defence-industrial base. The report noted the Pentagon carries the largest discretionary federal budget and is seeking a 42 percent increase in its current funding — a request of $1.5 trillion for national defence — from Congress. Though the Pentagon failed again this year, an official told Reuters that 14 of 25 Pentagon entities passed their financial statements, up from 11 the prior year, with auditors now using artificial intelligence to process and test more data on many of the Pentagons outdated systems.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
A ninth consecutive audit failure, paired with a record $1.5 trillion budget request, creates a significant congressional friction point that could slow or reshape appropriations for major defence programmes and add compliance cost pressure across prime contractors dependent on DoD as their primary revenue base.

NORMAL North America CRITICAL · 75
Hanwha fires Chunmoo missiles from ship in Mississippi
Hanwha Defense USA fired two Chunmoo missiles from a palletizable multi-pod launcher system aboard a test range vessel operated by the Spaceport Company off the coast of Mississippi, demonstrating that heavy maritime strike capability can be packaged into units deployable on most vessels. The demonstration aligns with the US Navy Containerization Plan announced by Admiral Daryl Caudle in April, which calls for modular containerised systems that decouple payloads from platforms and allow rapid force reconfiguration across the fleet. According to Hanwha, the palletized launcher system demonstrated can be used by both crewed and uncrewed systems, meaning autonomous surface vessels could potentially equip multi-rocket launchers previously reserved for ground combat.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Successful at-sea firing of containerised Chunmoo rockets positions Hanwha as a credible supplier in the US Navys distributed maritime strike build-out, with competitive implications for incumbent shipboard weapons integrators and for allied navies watching the US containerisation concept develop.

NORMAL Middle East CRITICAL · 75
Pakistan, Saudi Arabia, Turkiye to meet on Makkah pact
Turkish Foreign Minister Hakan Fidan announced that Pakistan, Saudi Arabia and Turkiye would hold fresh talks in November to advance institutionalisation of the Makkah Joint Defense Agreement, signed on Aug. 7, which treats an armed attack on one member as an attack on all three. The alliance previously established a permanent secretariat in Saudi Arabia and named retired Pakistani Lt. Gen. Nauman Mahmood as its first secretary-general for a three-year term at an Oct. 5 Riyadh meeting. A separate meeting expected in Germany would be the second between the Regional Four — Pakistan, Saudi Arabia, Turkiye and Egypt — and counterparts from Germany, France, Britain and Italy.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Accelerating institutionalisation of the Makkah alliance, combined with Iranian strikes on Gulf states this week, transforms what began as a trilateral declaration into an operational collective defence structure with direct implications for regional deterrence architecture and for Western arms exporters seeking alignment with the new grouping.

URGENT Gaza, Palestine CRITICAL · 80
Gaza reconstruction stalls as $18B in pledges sits unspent
More than a year after the October 2025 ceasefire, donors have pledged about $18 billion for Gaza through Trumps Board of Peace but reconstruction has not begun, with nearly 1.4 million Palestinians still living in makeshift shelters and most of the $280 million called in for the initial building phase remaining unspent as of a September briefing. The board warned in its latest report submitted on Tuesday that the truce between Israel and Hamas could collapse unless immediate steps are taken to carry out the ceasefire deal, and on Wednesday Israel began its first major military ground operation in the Gaza Strip since the ceasefire began, attacking refugee camps in the central part of the enclave. Since the ceasefire took effect on October 10, 2025, Israel has killed at least 1,658 Palestinians and injured 6,000, according to the Palestinian Ministry of Health, and continues to block entry of 60,000 mobile homes promised under the ceasefire deal.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
The stalled reconstruction and renewed ground operations signal that the ceasefire framework is under acute stress, increasing the probability of a return to full conflict that would further disrupt regional stability and defence planning across the Gulf.

URGENT Lebanon HIGH · 70
UNIFIL finalises exit as Israeli presence complicates handover
UNIFIL officials are finalising an exit plan after 48 years in Lebanon, with the mission mandate ending at 11:59 p.m. on Dec. 31 and troop withdrawals expected to begin in early 2027, potentially extending to September 2027 rather than concluding in June as initially projected. The force currently counts approximately 7,500 military personnel, with the largest contingents from Italy and Indonesia, and an average of 1,000 troops are expected to leave each month beginning in January 2027. Israeli forces occupy approximately 232 square miles of Lebanese territory and are carrying out daily demolitions and other military operations, complicating the handover of UNIFIL facilities to Lebanese authorities, as most positions are located in areas where Israeli forces are present and Lebanese forces remain absent to avoid potential clashes.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
The phased UNIFIL withdrawal under conditions of active Israeli operations in southern Lebanon creates a security vacuum that is likely to accelerate Lebanese military procurement and reshape force deployment planning for contributing nations.

NORMAL Gulf states HIGH · 62
Aviation risk in Gulf set to escalate in 2027
International SOS released its 11th annual Risk Outlook warning that aviation infrastructure in Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Qatar, Bahrain, Oman and Kuwait remains under elevated threat from drone attacks linked to the ongoing standoff between the US, Israel, and Iran, with the group noting it was involved in evacuating large numbers of people from Gulf states. The report cites the increased use of drones in asymmetric warfare by states including Russia and Iran, noting airports in multiple Eastern European states and air navigation radars in Colombia have already been targeted this year, and warns that aircraft operators must brace for more serious and widespread risk in 2027. New research by International SOS subsidiary MedAire also shows a 46 percent increase in clear-air turbulence during flights this year, resulting in significant injuries to passengers and crew as well as costly diversions.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Escalating asymmetric drone threats to Gulf aviation infrastructure raise operational risk for defence logistics, basing and airlift operations across the region, and may accelerate demand for counter-UAS procurement by Gulf states.

NORMAL Europe HIGH · 62
Europe defence spinouts raise more in 2026 than 2020-2025
The European Spinouts Report 2026, launched at Slush in Helsinki, found that defence spinouts raised more capital in 2026 than in the combined years 2020 to 2025, as total deep tech and life sciences spinout value reached $492 billion across more than 9,300 startups employing over 220,000 people, with spinouts expected to raise a record $16.1 billion this year, a 44 percent increase on 2025. Quantum Systems, spun out of Bundeswehr University Munich, raised $1.2 billion at an $8 billion valuation, while Tekever, originating from the University of Lisbon, raised $580 million at a $6.4 billion valuation after being awarded a UK contract worth up to £400 million. ICEYE raised €450 million at a valuation of over €10 billion, up from €2.4 billion just six months prior, and reported over €250 million in revenue for 2025.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
The surge in European defence spinout funding, led by dual-use autonomy and sensing companies, signals a structural shift in how European sovereign capability is being capitalised and points to growing competition for US strategic buyers seeking early-stage defence technology access.
