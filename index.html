NORMAL North America HIGH · 72
SpaceX CRS-35 delivers final ROSA pair to ISS
SpaceX launched the CRS-35 cargo mission from pad 40 at Cape Canaveral Space Force Station at 6:33 a.m. EDT on Tuesday, delivering more than 6,300 pounds of science and supplies to the International Space Station aboard Cargo Dragon C211 on booster B1096s eighth flight. The mission carries the fourth and final pair of Redwire Space Roll-Out Solar Array wings, each capable of providing more than 20 kilowatts of power for more than 10 years, completing a power augmentation programme that began with a demonstration on CRS-11 in 2017. Dragon C211 is expected to dock to the space-facing port of the Harmony module on Thursday morning, where NASA astronaut Anil Menon and Canadian Space Agency astronaut Joshua Kutryk will monitor its arrival.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Completion of the iROSA installation programme removes a long-standing power constraint on ISS science operations and validates Redwire Spaces roll-out solar technology as a flight-proven asset for future commercial station and deep-space platform bids.

NORMAL South Asia HIGH · 62
India urged to impose hard limits on Starlink
The Kutniti Foundation, an Indian policy group, has called on the Indian government to impose stringent, non-negotiable conditions on foreign satellite operators, describing SpaceXs Starlink as a technology of geopolitical control and warning it could give the US strategic leverage over communications infrastructure and undermine Indias technological sovereignty. The report, originally published in November 2024 and updated in October 2026, calls for India to retain control over domestic data flows, network access and emergency shutdowns while accelerating development of an indigenous satellite constellation. Starlink secured key licences in June 2025 and authorisation from space regulator IN-SPACe the following month but has not been able to launch services pending final security clearances and spectrum allocation.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Regulatory pressure on Starlink in India -- which authorised the service in June 2025 -- signals that commercial satellite broadband is entering a phase of geopolitical friction that could fragment market access and accelerate government investment in sovereign constellation programmes globally.

NORMAL North America HIGH · 57
Canada commits $85.5M to Arctic airfield upgrade
Transport Canada announced an $85.5 million federal investment through its Arctic Infrastructure Fund to rehabilitate Rankin Inlet Airport in Nunavut, designating it as a Northern Operational Support Node capable of sustaining the Lockheed Martin F-35 Lightning II and legacy Boeing CF-18 Hornet jets flown by the Royal Canadian Air Force. The airstrip upgrade covers about 1.52 million square feet of airfield pavement, including runway 13-31, taxiways A and C, and associated aprons, and is complemented by a parallel investment from the Department of National Defence. Canadas broader F-35A procurement of 88 aircraft remains under an 18-month procurement review, with current funding committed to 16 aircraft.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Canadas decision to harden Arctic dispersal infrastructure before its F-35 procurement dispute is resolved signals that base-layer investments are proceeding independently of platform decisions, a pattern that strengthens Lockheed Martins political sunk-cost argument in Ottawa even as the contract terms remain contested.

NORMAL North America MEDIUM · 42
Longshot Space raises $20M to launch cargo by gun
Longshot Space Technology, an Alameda, California-based startup, has raised approximately $20 million from venture investors to develop a system that fires cargo into space using a large gun rather than a conventional rocket, reviving a concept dating to Jules Verne. Co-founders CEO Mike Grace and CTO Nato Saichek argue the approach applies proven multi-injection gun technology, built for World War II and 1960s research programmes, to the pressing need for space launch, aiming ultimately for a launch cost of $50 per kilogram. The company is currently firing a 70-foot gun with a six-inch diameter at its test site near Barstow, California, and expects to start firing a 140-foot-long gun with a 30-inch diameter at its Alameda test site.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Longshot represents a bet that non-rocket launch architectures can find a commercial niche for specific cargo profiles, but at seed scale the technology remains far from demonstrated and carries high execution risk for any investor evaluating it against orbital-class rocket incumbents.

Russia strikes Ukraine energy sites after ceasefire claim

Army nearly exhausts FY-26 munitions funds amid CR threat
The US Army has obligated or expended nearly all of its fiscal year 2026 munitions funding, leaving little room to redirect resources toward emerging acquisition requirements, as the service confronts the prospect of a full-year continuing resolution that could further constrain its plans, according to Inside Defense. Lt. Gen. ◆ PRISM ANALYSIS

NORMAL North America CRITICAL · 82
Anduril wins up to $1.8B NGC2 expansion contract
The US Army awarded Anduril Industries a contract on 30 September worth up to $1.8 billion over five years to expand its Next Generation Command and Control system from an experimentation phase to fielding across I Corps, which commands service members across the Indo-Pacific. The Army obligated $162.8 million at award for Anduril to continue work on the NGC2 common data baseline using its Lattice AI operating system, with Palantir, Raft and Striveworks also named as contributors to the baseline. The NGC2 system will allow technologies, weapons, sensors, devices and applications from across the force to operate on the same network and increase decision-making speed on the battlefield.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
The NGC2 award with $162.8 million obligated now and a ceiling of up to $1.8 billion is the most concrete funded signal of the Armys autonomous command architecture pivot, and positions Anduril and its Lattice ecosystem -- including Palantir -- as structurally embedded in the Indo-Pacific command network before competitors can establish comparable integration depth.

NORMAL North America CRITICAL · 78
Army renames futures command to prioritise autonomy
Acting Army Chief of Staff Gen. Christopher LaNeve announced at the Association of the US Army annual meeting in Washington that the Futures and Concepts Command has been redesignated as US Army Futures and Autonomous Systems Command, or FASCOM, aligning the service structurally with its new How the Army Fights guidance published last week. LaNeve charged FASCOM with developing corps-level autonomous capabilities and confirmed the activation this month of the Armys first 390A Robotic Technicians, with capability-based formations to centre on autonomous long-range fires, protection, tanks, logistics and obstacle breaching. LaNeve told industry partners that the Army is not walking away from modernization but sprinting towards it with a recognition that resources are not endless.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Elevating autonomy to the name and mission of the Armys futures command sends an institutional signal to industry that autonomous systems integration is now an organisational priority rather than an experimental programme, shifting procurement risk calculus for companies positioned in uncrewed ground, air and fires domains.

NORMAL North America CRITICAL · 75
US Army awards $4.15B to 10 counter-drone firms
The US Army has awarded contracts totalling $4.15 billion to 10 companies under the Domestic Shield programme, a Department of Defense initiative to protect military installations and critical infrastructure against cheap unmanned aircraft, with seven companies receiving $500 million each and three receiving smaller awards, establishing a multi-vendor arrangement with a maximum value of $7 billion. Allen Control Systems, Digital Force Technologies, DroneShield, L3Harris WESCAM, Napatree Technology, RADA Technologies and SRC each received contracts with $500 million base values, while Echodyne and PVP Advanced EO Systems secured awards worth $250 million apiece and Smart Shooter received a contract valued at $150 million. The Army is working with Joint Interagency Task Force 401 to administer the initiative, which requires open architecture standards so equipment from different suppliers can connect with existing defence networks.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
The Domestic Shield awards structure -- ten vendors sharing a $7 billion ceiling rather than a single prime -- signals the Pentagon is deliberately keeping the counter-UAS market competitive, which limits unit pricing power for any individual firm but widens the addressable opportunity for the listed companies including DroneShield.

Gulf instability deepens as Houthi strikes hit Riyadh
Houthi missile strikes on Riyadh on Saturday and on King Khalid International Airport on Sunday killed at least 14 people and left 300 injured, marking a significant turn in a regional conflict that has intensified since the US and Israel launched their war on Iran in February. ◆ PRISM ANALYSIS

NORMAL United States CRITICAL · 75
Army Operation Jailbreak proves cross-vendor autonomous networks
The Army concluded Operation Jailbreak, a hacking initiative that began in May and ended in late August, staging two major events that brought contractors, operational units and Army technical experts together to demonstrate that autonomous platforms, sensors, weapons and command-and-control technologies can exchange real-time data across disparate vendors. The first three-week event brought 600 people and 50 defence companies to Fort Carson, Colorado, while the second and final exercise, Operation Jailbreak Falcon Fury, concluded on August 28 in Tampa, Florida, with 70 companies utilizing more than 150 pieces of technology. The effort was inspired by observation of Ukrainian forces integrating drones, sensors and shooting platforms on a single network with no software barriers, and dismantled longstanding system silos that previously prevented multi-vendor interoperability.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Proven cross-vendor autonomous networking creates an open architecture precedent that disadvantages single-vendor ecosystem plays and rewards companies whose systems can natively share data across any tactical network.

Project Sandhills 2.0 tests autonomous obstacle breaching

NORMAL United States HIGH · 55
Army to spend $702M reviewing prepositioned war stocks
The Army is nearing decisions on changes to its prepositioned stocks under a review called TAP2E, Total Army Power Projection Equipment, which will cost $702 million in the next year according to Army budget documents cited by Military Times. Maj. Gen. Gavin Lawrence, deputy commanding general of US Army Materiel Command, said the service expects to make a decision within the next calendar year, with the review running through 2032 and assessing the laydown of forward-deployed gear across at least seven publicly identified stockpiles. The Army budget proposal calls for an eighth stockpile in the southwest Indo-Pacific and increases to maintenance and care funding for the existing prepositioned stock focused on Europe.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
A sustained multi-year prepositioned stocks review worth hundreds of millions of dollars annually opens durable contracting opportunities for logistics, vehicle maintenance and weapons storage providers focused on Indo-Pacific and European theatres.
