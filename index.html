URGENT North America CRITICAL · 80
Vulcan returns to flight Nov 13 with Amazon payload
United Launch Alliance announced its Vulcan rocket will return to flight on Friday, November 13, after a nine-month grounding caused by nozzle failures on Northrop Grumman-built solid rocket boosters. The mission will carry 40 satellites for Amazon Leo and will be the first Vulcan flight configured with six strap-on solid rocket boosters. ULA said the integrated team has identified several potential contributors to the anomaly and has mitigated them through design and process improvements to the nozzle.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
A second successive nozzle anomaly on a Northrop Grumman booster raises qualification risk for the new six-booster configuration, and any repeat failure would extend Vulcans grounding further into 2027, threatening Amazon LEOs deployment schedule and ULAs position as a reliable heavy-lift alternative to Falcon 9.

NORMAL Global HIGH · 65
NASA plans final two ISS solar array spacewalks
NASA astronaut Anil Menon and Canadian Space Agency astronaut Joshua Kutryk are scheduled to install the seventh of eight International Space Station Roll-Out Solar Arrays, with a second and final spacewalk planned for November 5. The eight IROSA arrays will raise the stations available power from 160 kilowatts to about 215 kilowatts, a 30 percent increase, supporting operations through the stations planned decommissioning in 2030. Kutryk will become the fifth Canadian astronaut to walk in space and the first since David Saint-Jacques in 2019.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Completing the IROSA upgrade on schedule de-risks the stations remaining operational years and signals that NASA can sustain ISS through its 2030 deorbit without an emergency power shortfall, preserving the timeline for commercial successors to mature before the gap.

NORMAL North America HIGH · 62
NASA sets Artemis 3 as three-rocket lander test
NASA announced its Artemis 3 mission, crewed by commander Randy Bresnik, pilot Luca Parmitano, and mission specialists Andre Douglas and Frank Rubio, will be a 14-day Earth-orbit test in which Blue Origins Blue Moon Mark 2 and SpaceXs Starship Version 3 each attempt to rendezvous and dock with the Orion capsule. The mission involves launching three rockets — New Glenn carrying the Blue Origin lander, the Space Launch System carrying Orion, and a separate Starship launch — in what Artemis Program manager Jeremy Parsons described as likely the most complicated launch campaign ever undertaken. NASA is aiming for all this to happen in the summer of 2027.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Running two competing lander architectures in a single crewed orbital demonstration is an extraordinary qualification gamble: a failure by either vehicle in the Orion docking sequence would trigger a programme review at exactly the moment Chinas crewed lunar timeline is accelerating.

NORMAL Europe HIGH · 60
Ukraine and GomSpace test Obria Nano comms satellite
Ukrainian startup Himera and Danish manufacturer GomSpace confirmed their Obria Nano satellite, launched October 1 aboard the SpaceX Transporter-18 rideshare mission, is undergoing in-orbit tests as part of a plan to build a constellation providing communications coverage across Ukraine. The project follows disruptions in October to SpaceXs Starlink and Starshield services that affected Ukrainian military operations, including efforts to intercept Russian attack drones. A separate initiative by Iceye and Nokia also saw a letter of intent signed with Ukraines Defense Ministry to explore developing a sovereign broadband satellite communications network.

Two parallel European-backed initiatives to give Ukraine satellite communications independence from SpaceX signal that Starlinks dual role as both commercial infrastructure and military backbone is becoming a strategic liability, accelerating sovereign constellation demand and opening a procurement lane for European small-satellite manufacturers.

BREAKING Middle East CRITICAL · 97
Iran war cost hits $47B, above July estimate
The US military has spent over $47 billion to prosecute the war in Iran, more than 25 percent higher than the Defense Departments own estimate in July, according to Pentagon data obtained by Reuters. The latest estimate includes a projected cost of more than $99 billion to replace expended munitions and more than $6 billion to replace lost military equipment and platform losses including aircraft and drones. The disclosure arrives ahead of the November 3 election that will determine control of Congress as Republicans wrestle with the conflicts political fallout.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
A $99 billion munitions replacement liability — on top of platform losses exceeding $6 billion — means the US enters any renewed Iran offensive with both stockpile depth and procurement capacity already under severe strain, compressing the window in which a go-order could achieve decisive effect before logistical limits bind.

URGENT Global CRITICAL · 90
Pentagon fears drone and missile strikes on bases
Senior US defence officials told The Intercept that Iran demonstrated the ability to damage or destroy bases central to US power-projection for decades, including the destruction of Navy facilities in Manama, Bahrain on February 28 and severe damage to the Combined Air Operations Center at Al Udeid Air Base in Qatar. Officials also cited a plot against US bombers at Royal Air Force Fairford and a Russian campaign damaging European military facilities, with the US now scrambling to strengthen fortifications and recall personnel from some locations. The Pentagon has quietly begun seeking advice on new ways to protect bases using advanced materials, camouflage and underground facilities.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Irans proven ability to defeat US air defences using cheap drone-missile combinations — now being studied by other adversaries — structurally invalidates the forward basing model that underpins US power projection and accelerates demand for layered base-defence systems, counter-drone platforms and hardened dispersal infrastructure across all theatres.

US Navy logs most mishaps in a decade in fiscal 2026
The US Navy recorded its most unplanned catastrophic events involving aircraft and vessels in a decade during fiscal 2026, with sixteen Class A aviation and eleven Class A afloat mishaps, according to data released by Navy Safety Command. The toll includes a missing aircrewman presumed dead after an MH-60S Sea Hawk assigned to the USS George H.W. Bush made an emergency landing in the Arabian Sea, and a fire aboard USS Gerald R. Ford in the Red Sea that took over 30 hours to extinguish and damaged more than 600 sailors sleeping arrangements. The tally includes several mishaps that occurred in the Middle East during the Iran war, which began on February 28 after President Donald Trump launched Operation Epic Fury.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
A decade-high mishap rate across both aviation and surface fleets, coinciding with sustained high-tempo Iran war operations, signals that the Navys operational readiness margin is thinning in ways that Class A statistics do not fully capture — a compounding risk if a renewed Iran offensive requires carrier strike group surges before attrition losses are replaced.

NORMAL North America CRITICAL · 82
Air Force awards Lockheed $694M ARRW production deal
The US Air Force awarded Lockheed Martin a $694 million contract in late September to move the AGM-183A Air-Launched Rapid Response Weapon from prototyping and flight testing into production and fielding, announced Monday. The ARRW is designed to accelerate to speeds greater than Mach 5 using a solid-fuel rocket booster before releasing a hypersonic glide vehicle, intended to strike heavily defended targets from standoff range. Secretary of the Air Force Troy Meink called it a turning point in the services advanced weapons roadmap.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Moving ARRW into production under the Enterprise-Wide Agile Acquisition Contract gives combatant commanders a penetrating strike option against hardened Iranian and Chinese anti-access systems, and with LMT last closing at $629.75, the award reinforces the case for LMT as the primary beneficiary of the hypersonic production ramp the Pentagon is accelerating in parallel with its Iran war munitions replacement programme.

US forces said ready to strike Iran within 96 hours
Two US officials told CNN that US forces in the Middle East are prepared to launch military operations against Iran within 96 hours of receiving an order from President Donald Trump, with sufficient forces already deployed in the region. Britain fills £4.7B defence plan gap via reprioritisation
Finance minister John Healey told parliament that reprioritisation across Whitehall would close a £4.7 billion gap in Britains Defence Investment Plan, without specifying which departments would bear the cuts. Pentagon shifts to buying cyber outcomes not tools
The Pentagon has been developing a Cyber Effects-as-a-Service model that contracts for specific operational outcomes rather than bespoke tools, with assistant secretary of defense for cyber policy Katie Sutton calling for industry to move from a contractual relationship to an operational partner, according to Breaking Defense. Sutton said demand for cyber capabilities from combatant commanders and department leadership far exceeds the supply the government currently holds. The model would see companies use their own people, tools and infrastructure to deliver authorised outcomes under government direction and oversight, with the Pentagon retaining responsibility for objectives, authorisations and inherently governmental functions.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
A shift to outcome-based cyber contracting opens a structurally new revenue stream for defence-focused cyber firms and reduces the procurement friction that has historically delayed fielding of offensive and defensive cyber tools.

US, China and Ukraine pursue battlefield humanoid robots

NORMAL Europe HIGH · 52
Rheinmetall: lower airspace now belongs to drones
Reto Maurer, Senior Vice President of Sales at Rheinmetall Air Defence, told Shephard Media that the airspace up to 3,000 metres above ground level now belongs to drones, and that traditional interceptor-based ground-based air defence systems can no longer achieve air superiority at that layer alone due to volume, low radar cross-section and cost asymmetry of uncrewed aerial vehicles. Maurer said future air defence must combine passive and active layered measures under a unified command-and-control system that preserves high-value interceptors for high-end threats while neutralising massed low-cost threats with cost-appropriate measures. He highlighted the Oerlikon Skynex system, built around the Skymaster battle management system, as a key part of Rheinmetalls networked approach to short-range air defence.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Rheinmetalls explicit framing of the lower airspace as a lost domain for legacy systems validates investment in integrated multi-layer counter-UAS architectures and creates a commercial case for networked low-cost effectors across European defence budgets.
