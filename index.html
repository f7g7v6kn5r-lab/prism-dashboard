BREAKING Strait of Hormuz / Gulf Region CRITICAL · 95
Hormuz Shipping Hits One-Week Low Amid US-Iran Standoff
Strait of Hormuz traffic has remained severely suppressed but the one-week low characterisation is stale as of August 13, 2026. Lloyd's List Intelligence data as of August 12 show 78 transits recorded in the week of August 3-9, down from 95 the prior week, against a pre-war norm of roughly 130-140 ships per day. Traffic is effectively closed to standard commercial shipping, with convoys moving under naval escort. Brent crude was tracking at approximately 89 dollars per barrel as of August 12, per live tracker data -- not 88 dollars as stated. The ceasefire framing in the original brief is also stale: the June 17 MoU has collapsed and an informal quiet-for-quiet pause replaced it, with direct US-Iran talks having faltered as of August 10, 2026 per CNN.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
A prolonged Hormuz shutdown directly threatens global energy supply chains and raises operational risk costs for every defence logistics and satellite launch programme dependent on Gulf-routed fuel and components.
