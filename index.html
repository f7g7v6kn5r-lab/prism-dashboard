URGENT North America CRITICAL · 82
FCC clears SpaceX to deploy 1 million Starmind satellites
The Federal Communications Commission granted SpaceX conditional authorization to deploy up to 1 million Starmind satellites designed as orbital data centers, with Chairman Brendan Carr describing the decision as opening a new frontier for computing infrastructure to support US leadership in artificial intelligence. The solar-powered modules would process data in space using optical links supplemented by radiofrequency communications, with SpaceX required to have its first operational satellite in place by late 2033 and the full constellation by 2040. The authorization arrived on the same day SpaceX announced it would contribute newly issued Class A shares to every active Trump Account, a move that sent SPCX shares down as much as 9 percent intraday before closing 1.30 percent higher.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Conditional FCC authorisation for 1 million orbital computing nodes reframes SpaceX from a launch and connectivity company into a potential dominant provider of space-based compute infrastructure, a market position with long-run implications for defence cloud and ISR architectures.

URGENT Asia Pacific CRITICAL · 78
China flies first foreign astronaut to Tiangong on Shenzhou-24
China launched Shenzhou-24 from Jiuquan Satellite Launch Centre atop a Long March-2F Y24 carrier rocket, carrying mission commander Tang Hongbo, flight engineer Wu Yi, and Pakistani Air Force pilot Muhammad Zeeshan Ali, the first foreign national to visit the Tiangong space station. Ali, aged 40, will spend approximately one week aboard the station conducting scientific experiments before returning to Earth on the Shenzhou-23 spacecraft, while his Chinese crewmates will remain for a standard six-month rotation.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Chinas decision to open Tiangong to its first foreign crew member — a Pakistani military pilot — is a deliberate demonstration of an alternative human spaceflight bloc at a moment when NASA plans ISS retirement in early 2031, signalling strategic competition for partner-nation alignment in cislunar space.

URGENT Middle East CRITICAL · 85
Houthis claim strikes on Riyadh and Ras Tanura refinery
Yemens Houthis claimed responsibility for attacks targeting Riyadhs financial district and the Ras Tanura oil refinery, while Saudi air defences intercepted drones over Ras Tanura. Etihad Airways extended the suspension of flights to Riyadh and Dammam until November 30, and the US Mission to the UAE issued a security alert urging American citizens across the Middle East to exercise heightened vigilance amid threats of further attacks by Iran and Iranian-backed forces. US President Donald Trump said Washington had funding for the conflict through the end of January, warning Democrats against forcing an early end to the fighting.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Sustained Houthi targeting of Saudi energy and financial infrastructure raises the probability of supply disruption at Ras Tanura, one of the worlds highest-volume oil export terminals, and directly tests the credibility of US extended deterrence in the Gulf at a moment when Trump has publicly framed the conflict as funded only through January.

URGENT North America CRITICAL · 83
Pentagon in talks with GM and Ford to mass-produce drone parts
The US Department of Defense is in discussions with General Motors and Ford, as well as manufacturers outside the automotive industry, to supply components for military drones used for strikes and reconnaissance after determining that existing US drone producers lack the capacity to meet military volume requirements. The Wall Street Journal reported that automakers would build parts rather than finished drones, and that the Pentagon estimates the military drone market could reach $30 billion, consolidating requirements across branches to create larger contracts. Automakers are described as wary due to reputational concerns, low margins and a lack of guarantees for consistent demand.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
If automaker production capacity is redirected toward drone components at scale, it would structurally reduce the bottleneck that has constrained US unmanned systems output and alter the competitive landscape for established drone primes that currently benefit from supply constraints.

NORMAL North America CRITICAL · 80
Air Force awards Boeing $2.82B for final KC-46 lot
The Pentagon awarded Boeing a $2.82 billion contract for 15 more KC-46A Pegasus tankers, closing out the final production lot under the programmes original 2011 contract and coming in approximately $350 million above the previous 15-aircraft lot purchased for $2.47 billion. The Air Force has 116 KC-46As delivered to date with 203 on contract or in service worldwide, and plans to follow this lot with a Tanker Production Extension programme to buy up to 75 additional aircraft as it works toward a total fleet of 263 to replace its KC-135s. Air Force Vice Chief of Staff Gen. John Lamontagne said the 75-jet buy depends on Boeing fixing deficiencies including issues with the refueling boom and the Remote Vision System.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
The $350 million per-lot cost increase confirms persistent inflation in tanker production just as the Air Force prepares a follow-on buy of up to 75 aircraft, a dynamic that will shape pricing negotiations for the Tanker Production Extension and represents a material risk to Boeings defence margin recovery.

NORMAL North America CRITICAL · 75
US Senate proposes $544M for containerised missiles and launchers
A US Senate Appropriations Committee defence funding proposal dated November 16 would allocate $544 million to procure 1,000 low-cost containerised munitions and 62 ground launchers, intended to expand distributed precision-strike capabilities across land platforms. The proposal is part of a broader Senate effort to strengthen munitions production, expand the defence industrial base, and improve the militarys ability to sustain high-intensity combat operations, and has not yet been enacted into law or resulted in a confirmed procurement contract.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
A Senate push for 1,000 containerised rounds and 62 launchers signals that distributed, volume-fire strike architecture is moving from concept to funded programme, creating near-term opportunities for Anduril, Leidos and Lockheed Martin as the most visible competitors in this space.

URGENT Middle East CRITICAL · 75
UK sanctions Houthis and four senior leaders over Saudi strikes
The United Kingdom announced asset freezes and travel bans on Yemens Houthi movement and four of its senior leaders, including Interior Minister Abdulkarim Amir al-Din Hussein al-Houthi, Security and Intelligence chief Abdul Hakim Hashem Ali al-Khaiwani, and chief prosecutor Sarim al-Din Mufadal, accusing the group of renewed aggression and attacks on civilian infrastructure in Saudi Arabia. URGENT Asia Pacific HIGH · 72
China trains fighter pilots alongside AI loyal-wingman drones
The Peoples Liberation Army Air Force is incorporating simulated loyal-wingman unmanned aircraft into pilot training, allowing crews to practise coordinated air combat with drones designed to scout, support attacks and absorb enemy fire in place of manned aircraft, according to Chinese military publications highlighted by the South China Morning Post. US Air Force researchers John Chen and Emilie B. Stewart, in a 40-page study published by the China Aerospace Studies Institute, documented a 2021 demonstration in which a simulated unmanned wingman was directed to draw incoming fire to protect a human-piloted fighter using algorithms trained on air combat data. China is also developing the GJ-11, a stealth combat drone presented as a potential partner for crewed aircraft, though publicly available footage does not establish the degree of autonomous coordination involved.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Operational integration of AI-directed loyal wingmen into PLA Air Force training cycles accelerates the timeline for a human-machine teaming capability that US Air Force researchers assess as a direct threat to American air superiority over Taiwan, compressing the window for the US to field comparable collaborative combat aircraft systems.

NORMAL Jordan HIGH · 55
Jordan parliament reviews anti-terror law after church attack
Jordans House of Representatives voted to refer draft amendments to the 2006 Anti-Terrorism Law to its Legal Committee after gunmen opened fire during a funeral at a Greek Orthodox church in the village of Hamoud in Karak on October 23, killing nine people and wounding 34. The proposed amendments would raise minimum prison terms to 10 years and up to 15 years in certain cases while retaining the death penalty for the most serious offences, according to the state-run Jordan News Agency. First Deputy Speaker Ayatollah Freihat criticised the amendments as a knee-jerk reaction and raised concerns that vague formulations defining terrorism could harm freedom of expression.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Legislative hardening of Jordans counter-terrorism framework signals a shift toward stricter internal security posture, relevant to allies and defence partners who rely on Jordan as a stable logistics and intelligence corridor in the Levant.

NORMAL Global HIGH · 65
UN Security Council warns AI and drones reshape warfare
The UN Security Council held a session on artificial intelligence in warfare, with Secretary-General Guterres telling members that killer robots must have no place in our future and that AI and other new technologies now spread globally within months rather than decades. Guterres said AI has entered the battlefield in troubling ways, with algorithms increasingly shaping life-and-death decisions from intelligence gathering to target selection, and called for urgently launching negotiations for a legally binding instrument on lethal autonomous weapons systems. The International Committee of the Red Cross president Mirjana Spoljaric warned that humanity is nearing a point at which decisions over life and death could be left entirely to machines, and that states must set limits since AI will not set its own.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Security Council debate on autonomous weapons norms signals that multilateral regulatory frameworks are approaching, creating compliance and reputational risk for defence primes developing lethal autonomous systems and shaping near-term export control conversations.

NORMAL United States HIGH · 60
Pentagon bomber UAV trials test industry speed at Gauntlet 2.5
The Pentagons Drone Dominance Program held its Gauntlet 2.5 competition for reusable bomber uncrewed aerial vehicles on November 13 at Camp Grayling, Michigan, where eight suppliers competed in what the program described as its most demanding and expedited testing to date. US manufacturer Swarm Defense developed a prototype platform in just two-and-a-half weeks to participate in the competition. NORMAL Germany HIGH · 58
Germany moves to acquire stake in Urenco enrichment firm
A surprise line in Germanys 2027 budget draft, which the government had tried to keep secret according to Handelsblatt, earmarks 4 billion euros for the acquisition of a stake in Urenco, a uranium enrichment company operating sites in Britain, Germany, the Netherlands and the United States that supplies around a third of the global market with enriched uranium. Urenco is currently owned by the United Kingdom, the Netherlands and two former German nuclear power plant operators, RWE and EON, which are reported to want to divest their stakes in part because Urenco is becoming increasingly active in the military nuclear sector at the request of the United Kingdom. Urenco has been enriching uranium to 19.75 percent since 2019, just below the 20 percent threshold considered suitable for military purposes, and is reportedly expected to produce enriched uranium for the propulsion of British submarines.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
A German state acquisition of Urenco equity would give Berlin direct influence over a critical node in Western nuclear fuel supply and submarine propulsion, deepening European strategic autonomy in advanced nuclear technology at a moment of heightened great-power competition.

NORMAL Italy HIGH · 73
Italy claims NATO target met in 2026 defence spend
Italian officials told Defense News that Rome reached a combined defence and domestic security spend equal to the target made at the July NATO summit in 2026, with core defence spending calculated at 2.1 percent of GDP and domestic security at 0.7 percent of GDP, for a combined total of 2.8 percent. Core defence spending is calculated at 23.6 billion euros, up from 22.49 billion euros the year before, with procurement spending calculated at 9.33 billion euros from the defence ministry, rising to over 12 billion euros when Enterprise Ministry funding is added. The figures are not explicitly stated in Italys 2026 budget, which was issued months late, and require extrapolation from other budget lines, with Italy now targeting a 5 percent objective split between 3.5 percent core defence and 1.5 percent domestic security by 2035.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
If Rome sustains the trajectory toward 5 percent of GDP, Italian procurement spending could more than double from current levels, opening substantial contract opportunities for European and US defence primes across armour, air and naval programmes.

NORMAL India / Japan HIGH · 65
Japan signs LoI to train on Indian missile ranges
India and Japan signed a Letter of Intent on November 19 allowing Japanese troops to conduct live-fire missile training at Indian ranges, following an October visit by Japanese officials to assess whether Indian facilities could accommodate the roughly 1,000-kilometre range of Japans Type-25 surface-to-ship missile. Limited domestic space for long-range live-fire exercises has driven Japan to seek overseas training options as it deploys long-range missiles to its southern island of Kyushu amid growing concerns over Chinese military activity. Japanese Defense Minister Shinjiro Koizumi said the arrangement was significant not only for expanding the scope of training for Japanese forces but also as a demonstration of a new phase in Japan-India cooperation.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
Access to Indian ranges deepens Japan-India defence cooperation and validates Japans long-range strike investment, potentially accelerating procurement and co-development conversations between the two countries defence industries.

NORMAL United States / Czech Republic HIGH · 60
PBS Aerospace eyes doubled US output for drone engines
Czech jet engine maker PBS Aerospace told Defense News it aims to at least double its manufacturing capacity in the United States, building on a $20 million investment that opened a plant in Roswell, Georgia in September 2025 to supply compact propulsion systems for drones and cruise missiles. Chief government relations and strategy officer Jan Havranek said global conflicts including the war in Ukraine have made clear the importance of a reliable domestic US supply of small engines, with the US government as the end customer through prime contractors including Lockheed Martin and Pratt and Whitney. PBS Aerospace plans to allocate around $100 million to boost capacity in Roswell and increase annual output to between 10,000 and 20,000 engines over the next three years.
◆ PRISM ANALYSIS
PBS Aerospaces US expansion positions a European supplier inside the American defence industrial base at a time of surging drone and cruise missile demand, reducing supply-chain risk for Pentagon primes and creating a template other NATO-allied component makers may follow.
